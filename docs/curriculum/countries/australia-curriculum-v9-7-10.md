# Australian Curriculum v9.0 Science, Years 7–10 (ACARA): the national chemistry foundations

**Purpose:** the reference curriculum for the organic chemistry and energetics games planned in
[`../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md`](../../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md). The Australian jurisdictions
are compared in [`australia-overview.md`](../australia-overview.md).
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent, from primary sources).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher. This is the national layer; no single state
teaches it unchanged in every sector.

Primary sources: ACARA's v9.0 downloads (*Science: Curriculum content 7–10*, *Science:
Comparative information F–10* (v8.4 → v9.0), *Science: Glossary*), ACARA's implementation page,
and the state authority pages listed in Section 7. Anything not confirmed from a primary source
is marked **(unverified)**.

## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **There is no organic chemistry in the national Years 7–10 curriculum.** There are no
  hydrocarbons, functional groups, naming, isomers or polymers. A text search of the 7–10
  content and elaborations finds no "hydrocarbon", "polymer" or "organic"; the glossary uses
  "organic" only for "dead organic material". Every organic game (O1–O10) belongs to a
  state's senior syllabus (Years 11–12).
- **Kinetics and energetics are foundations only.**
  - Year 10 has reaction types and rate factors (temperature, concentration, surface area,
    catalysts; AC9S10U07).
  - Year 8 asks students to "identify indicators of energy change in chemical reactions"
    (AC9S8U07).
  - There is no collision theory, activation energy, energy diagram, ΔH, calorimetry,
    equilibrium or reversibility.
  - Games that teach these ideas below Year 11 are enrichment nationally.
- **v9.0 removed chemistry content that v8.4 had.**
  - v8.4's Year 9 ACSSU179 ("Chemical reactions, including combustion and the reactions of
    acids, … involve energy transfer") was split. Combustion moved to the carbon cycle (AC9S9U03,
    Earth and space sciences).
  - Acids survive only in elaborations (metal + acid, neutralisation).
  - So "exothermic/endothermic" is **not named** in any v9.0 content description.
  - Victoria's adaptation puts it back (VC2S10U09).
- **The Year 10 achievement standard is a good game verb.** Students "predict the products of
  reactions and the effect of changing reactant and reaction conditions". A prediction mechanic
  (choose conditions, predict product and rate) fits Year 10 nationally.
- **Green chemistry is seeded in Year 9.** An AC9S9U07 elaboration asks students to predict how
  green chemistry ideas (less waste, less energy) affect the environment. This is a natural
  bridge to atom economy in senior courses.
- **Tag games by state, not by "Year 10".** NSW has its own syllabus, Victoria and WA adapt
  v9.0, Queensland publishes v9.0 for Queensland (ACiQ), and the others use it more or less
  directly (Section 0). The content above is the common floor, not a guarantee of what a given
  Year 10 class has done.

## 0. What applies in 2026

- **Version:** Australian Curriculum **Version 9.0** (F–10). Education ministers approved it in
  April 2022; it was released in May 2022 for implementation "from 2023" (ACARA). It replaces
  v8.4.
- **Who decides timing:** state and territory authorities and the non-government sectors decide
  "when and how" v9.0 is delivered (ACARA). Schools teach it "either directly from the Australian
  Curriculum in some states and territories, or from curriculum websites incorporating the
  Australian Curriculum in others".
- **No replacement announced.** No v10 or Science revision announcement was found **(not
  searched exhaustively)**. During research the ACARA site carried a maintenance notice (back
  online by 23 Sept 2026), which is not a curriculum change.

## 1. Structure

- **Strands** (Years 7–10 each):
  - **Science understanding:** biological, chemical, Earth and space, and physical sciences.
  - **Science as a human endeavour:** nature and development of science; use and influence of
    science.
  - **Science inquiry.**
- **Per year level:** a year level description, sample inquiry questions, an achievement
  standard, content descriptions ("Students learn to:") with codes such as `AC9S10U07`, and
  optional content elaborations ("This may involve students:").
- **Year-by-year, not banded.** Chemistry has two content descriptions per year.
- **No time allocation or assessment is set nationally.** Assessment and reporting belong to the
  jurisdictions. There is no external exam and no certificate at Year 10. NAP Science Literacy
  (a national sample test) was not checked **(unverified)**.
- **Senior secondary:** ACARA also has Senior Secondary Australian Curriculum Chemistry (Units
  1–4). The states teach their own senior syllabuses (VCE, HSC, QCE, WACE, SACE, TCE, ACT SSC),
  so its role here is background only **(not researched)**.

## 2. Content by year level (Chemical sciences sub-strand, plus related dot points)

Codes and content descriptions are verbatim from *Science: Curriculum content 7–10* (v9.0).
Elaborations are paraphrased.

### Year 7

- **AC9S7U05** "use particle theory to describe the arrangement of particles in a substance,
  including the motion of and attraction between particles, and relate this to the properties
  of the substance". Elaborations: changes of state, density, melting point, diffusion.
- **AC9S7U06** "use a particle model to describe differences between pure substances and
  mixtures and apply understanding of properties of substances to separate mixtures".
  Elaborations: filtration, decantation, evaporation, crystallisation, **chromatography,
  distillation**.
- **Achievement standard:** students "use particle theory to explain the physical properties of
  substances and develop processes that separate mixtures".

### Year 8

- **AC9S8U06** "classify matter as elements, compounds or mixtures and compare different
  representations of these, including 2-dimensional and 3-dimensional models, symbols for
  elements and formulas for molecules and compounds".
- **AC9S8U07** "compare physical and chemical changes and identify indicators of energy change
  in chemical reactions". Elaborations: gas, solid, colour and temperature change; "identifying
  energy changes in different chemical reactions such as differences in temperature".
- **Year level description:** students "understand that chemical reactions also involve energy".
- **Human endeavour elaboration (AC9S8H03):** hybrid, electric and hydrogen vehicles as
  responses to "the depletion of fossil fuels".

### Year 9

- **AC9S9U06:** the changing model of the atom; natural radioactive decay, isotopes, half-life.
- **AC9S9U07** "model the rearrangement of atoms in chemical reactions using a range of
  representations, including word and simple balanced chemical equations, and use these to
  demonstrate the law of conservation of mass". Elaborations:
  - reactants and products
  - closed vs open systems
  - balancing simple equations
  - why most elements are not found in their elemental state
  - **green chemistry ideas** (less unusable waste, less energy)
- **Related, Earth and space sciences, AC9S9U03:** represent the carbon cycle and examine
  processes "including combustion, photosynthesis and respiration". Elaborations include the
  effect of combustion on the carbon cycle and calculating a carbon footprint. The comparative
  document says this was "Realigned from Year 9 Chemical sciences" (v8.4 ACSSU179, which had
  combustion, acid reactions and "energy transfer").
- **Related, Physical sciences, AC9S9U05:** "apply the law of conservation of energy to analyse
  system efficiency in terms of energy inputs, outputs, transfers and transformations".
- **Achievement standard:** students "explain observable chemical processes in terms of changes
  in atomic structure, atomic rearrangement and mass".

### Year 10

- **AC9S10U06** "explain how the structure and properties of atoms relate to the organisation
  of the elements in the periodic table". Elaborations: groups, the Bohr model, flame tests.
- **AC9S10U07** "identify patterns in synthesis, decomposition and displacement reactions and
  investigate the factors that affect reaction rates". Elaborations:
  - word and balanced symbolic equations
  - "identifying reaction type and predicting the products"
  - metals + oxygen; metal + acid, neutralisation and precipitation (as displacement)
  - "investigating the effect of a range of factors, such as temperature, concentration,
    surface area and catalysts, on the rate of chemical reactions"
  - First Nations production of acids and ethanol, and detoxifying plants
  - reactions that produce useful products
- **Related, AC9S10U04:** energy flow between Earth's spheres and climate change.
- **Achievement standard:** students "explain patterns and trends in the periodic table and
  predict the products of reactions and the effect of changing reactant and reaction
  conditions".

**Not present anywhere in 7–10:**
- hydrocarbons or any organic family, IUPAC naming, isomers, polymers (except "biodegradable
  materials" in a Year 8 human-endeavour elaboration)
- the mole
- collision theory, activation energy, energy profile diagrams
- the words exothermic and endothermic, ΔH, calorimetry
- reversible reactions and equilibrium
- acids and bases as a content description

## 4. Conventions and notation

- **Representations named:** 2D and 3D models, symbols, formulas (Year 8); word and "simple
  balanced" symbolic equations (Years 9–10). There is no nomenclature guidance for organic
  compounds.
- **Glossary** (v9.0 Science glossary) defines *synthesis reaction* ("also called combination
  reactions"), *decomposition reaction* and *displacement (reaction)*. The last is defined
  broadly enough to include double displacement, which is why neutralisation and precipitation
  sit under "displacement". Games should use these three labels, not "single/double
  replacement".
- **Spelling:** Australian English (*sulfur*, *aluminium*). "First Nations Australians" is the
  v9.0 term in elaborations.
- **Units and sign conventions:** none specified for energy in chemistry. No data booklet
  exists at this level.

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

- ACARA's v9.0 Science glossary is a **content glossary** (e.g. accuracy, claim, controlled
  variable, decomposition reaction). It is not a command-term list.
- No separate F–10 command-term glossary was found for Science **(unverified)**. Jurisdictions
  use their own (e.g. the VCAA glossary of command terms; see `australia-victoria.md`).
