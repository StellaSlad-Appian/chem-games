# Chemistry curriculum: United States (Years 7–12)

**Purpose:** input for the cross-country concept/year map for chem-games.
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches in the US.
**Key sources:** NGSS performance expectations and Appendix K (Achieve / nextgenscience.org), NRC *Framework* (2012), College Board AP Chemistry CED (effective Fall 2024, clarified June 2026), Texas 19 TAC Ch. 112 (TEKS), NCES and Horizon Research (NSSME+) course-taking data; full list in §6.

## Summary for game design

- **There is no national curriculum.** Each state sets its own standards. The nearest thing to a national framework is the **NGSS** (2013), built on the NRC *Framework for K-12 Science Education* (2012). NSTA reports that **20 states + DC** adopted NGSS and **24 more states** wrote their own standards based on the *Framework*, so about 44 states + DC are NGSS-like. The largest non-NGSS state is Texas (TEKS) [S13, S26].
- **Middle school standards are grade-banded 6–8, and high school standards 9–12.** NGSS does not tie a topic to a grade. States and districts split the bands into courses (NGSS Appendix K). In this file, grade placements inside a band are marked **(typical)**. They are not national rules [S11].
- **Particle ideas start early.** In Grade 5 (Year 5, before this map starts), students learn that matter is made of particles too small to see and that total weight is conserved [S12]. Middle school (Years 6–8) then covers atoms and molecules, physical vs chemical change, conservation of atoms and mass, and exo/endothermic processes. The middle school limits are strict: **no valence electrons, no balancing of symbolic equations, no atomic masses** [S2, S3].
- **Formal chemistry is one full-year high school course**, usually in **Grade 10 or 11** (Year 10/11). It comes after biology in the traditional Biology → Chemistry → Physics order. "Physics First" is a minority pattern. In 2019, about 12% of all physics students were in Physics First courses [S25]. About **76% of 2019 graduates** had taken a chemistry course [S22].
- **The NGSS high school chemistry core is thin.** HS-PS1 has only 8 performance expectations: periodic trends, bonding/IMF evidence, bond energy, collision theory, qualitative Le Chatelier, mole/conservation, and nuclear. Typical courses (and the Texas TEKS) go further: nomenclature, stoichiometry, gas laws, molarity, pH, calorimetry [S6, S7, S26].
- **Track split:** "general/college-prep" vs **honors**, then **AP Chemistry** (a college general-chemistry course) in Grade 11 or 12. IB Chemistry exists but is rare (about 2% of high schools) [S16, S24].
- **Organic chemistry and biochemistry are almost absent** from NGSS, TEKS Chemistry and the AP Chemistry exam. Only "carbon-based molecules" appears, and it sits in biology (HS-LS1-6) [S9, S16, S26]. Game content on organic naming would go beyond the US core.
- **Notation:** US spelling (*sulfur*, *aluminum*, *cesium*), decimal **point**, SI in the lab but **atm / mm Hg / torr** are still used for pressure. AP defines **STP = 273.15 K and 1.0 atm**, which is not IUPAC's 100 kPa. Reaction types are named "single/double **replacement**" (TEKS) [S16, S26].
- **Standards and assessment are stable, but the naming is confusing.** Florida's "Next Generation Sunshine State Standards" are **not** NGSS (unverified detail). Texas TEKS Chemistry moved from §112.35 to **§112.43** (implemented 2024–25) [S26, S28].

**Standards in force for 2026/27**
- **NGSS (2013):** there is no revision in progress on nextgenscience.org. Adoption and implementation vary by state. MS-PS1 (structure and properties of matter, chemical reactions) and MS-PS3 (thermal energy) are banded 6–8. HS-PS1 (8 performance expectations) is banded 9–12 [S2–S8].
- **How the bands are split (NGSS Appendix K):**
  - **Conceptual Progressions model:** puts all of MS-PS1 in Course 1 (Grade 6). A revised version keeps PS1.A in Course 1 and moves PS1.B (chemical reactions) to Course 2 (Grade 7). The reason given was that grade 6 lacked "chemistry lab" readiness.
  - **California's integrated model (Appendix K Table 3A):** puts all of MS-PS1 in Course 2 (Grade 7).
  - **High school "Modified Science Domains" model (Biology / Chemistry / Physics):** puts HS-PS1-1 to HS-PS1-8 in the Chemistry course, plus HS-PS3-1, -3, -4, HS-ESS2-4, -5, -6, and HS-ESS3-2, -5, -6.
  - **California's three-course model:** uses a "Chemistry in the Earth System" course (CDE site blocked; unverified detail) [S11, S29].
- **Reforms being phased in:**
  - **Texas:** revised TEKS (K–8 and HS Biology, Chemistry, Physics, IPC) implemented from **2024–25**. No science change is listed for 2026–27 [S28].
  - **Pennsylvania:** framework-based STEELS standards, implemented **1 July 2025** and fully in place in 2025–26 [S35].
  - **NAEP:** a new **2028 NAEP Science Framework** (Grades 4, 8, 12) was adopted Nov 2023 for the 2028 assessment [S31].
  - **AP Chemistry:** the CED effective **Fall 2024** is current. The June 2026 clarifications are editorial only (Progress Check wording, resource links). College Board lists no AP Chemistry change for 2026–27 [S16, S19, S20].
- **Exams that shape content:**
  - **State tests under ESSA:** science is tested once in Gr 3–5, once in Gr **6–9**, and once in Gr **10–12** [S32]. These are often an 8th-grade test and a high school biology or integrated test. Chemistry is rarely tested at state level (unverified).
  - **AP Chemistry exam:** hybrid digital (in Bluebook), 60 MCQ + 7 FRQ, 3 h 15 min. 2027 date: 6 May 2027 [S18].
  - **NAEP** (national sample).
  - **ACT/SAT:** the ACT has a science section; the SAT has no science section (unverified, not checked this session).

**Typical interval and mechanism**
- **NGSS itself:** a one-off reform (2011–2013). Achieve has not scheduled a revision, and none is announced on nextgenscience.org. State adoption was ad hoc and board-driven, in waves between 2013 and about 2017. Some adoptions were politically contested (unverified detail).
- **State standards:** most states revise when the state board of education initiates it. Several states have a **statutory or scheduled cycle, typically 5–7 years**, for example:
  - Virginia SOL: at least every 7 years (Code §22.1-253.13:1);
  - Kansas: science at least every 7 years;
  - New Jersey: every 5 years;
  - Tennessee: at least every 6 years;
  - South Dakota: 7-year cycle.
  A 2016 WestEd/CSAI scan found that cycles are often not kept to because of capacity limits. In practice, **science standards change about every 10+ years** in many states (Texas: 1998, 2009/10, 2020/21) (Texas 1998/2009 dates unverified) [S33, S34].
- **AP Chemistry:**
  - Major redesign about every **10 years** (2013–14; framework update 2019; refresh Fall 2024).
  - **Yearly "Clarifications and Corrections"** documents and occasional equation-sheet updates (e.g. "effective 2025").
  - Exam-format or delivery changes come in between (hybrid digital from about 2025) [S16, S19, S20, S21].
- **NAEP frameworks:** about every 15–20 years (2009 → 2028) [S31].

**Announced or in progress now**
- No NGSS revision.
- No AP Chemistry framework change announced for 2026–27 or 2027–28. In 2026–27 College Board is changing AP Physics, AP Environmental Science and others, but not Chemistry [S19].
- NAEP 2028 science assessment is in development [S31].
- Individual states continue on their own cycles. These were not surveyed state by state (see §5).

**Stability rating for game content: stable.** The core chemistry concepts (particles, conservation, mole, bonding, rates, equilibrium, acids and bases) have held steady across NGSS (2013), TEKS (2021) and AP (2014/2019/2024). Changes mostly affect emphasis (practices and modelling) and exam format, not the content list. The only churn is local: state-by-state course placement within grade bands.

### Year 7 (Grade 7)

In NGSS states, MS chemistry sits in Grade 6, 7 or 8 depending on the state's course model. Grade 7 is where California's integrated model puts all of MS-PS1, and where the revised Appendix K model puts chemical reactions. **All NGSS-MS items below are band 6–8 (typical Gr 7).**

**Concepts**
- *Matter & particles:*
  - substances are made of different types of atoms that combine into molecules or extended structures (e.g. NaCl, diamond);
  - particle models of solid, liquid and gas;
  - state changes explained by particle motion when thermal energy is added or removed [NGSS-MS, S2].
- *Chemical reactions & equations:*
  - evidence of a chemical reaction from properties before and after (density, melting/boiling point, solubility, flammability, odor);
  - atoms regroup into new substances;
  - **number of atoms is conserved, so mass is conserved**, using physical models and drawings only (**no balancing of symbolic equations, no atomic masses**) [NGSS-MS, S3].
- *Energetics:*
  - some reactions release energy and others absorb it;
  - design a device that releases or absorbs thermal energy (e.g. dissolving CaCl₂ or NH₄Cl) [NGSS-MS, S3].
  - Heat means energy transferred because of a temperature difference; temperature is linked to average particle kinetic energy [S2, S4].
- *Industrial, environmental & applied:* synthetic materials come from natural resources and affect society (medicines, foods, fuels; qualitative only) [NGSS-MS, S2].
- *Biochemistry (via life science):* food molecules are broken down and rearranged through chemical reactions to release energy; photosynthesis cycles matter (no biochemical mechanisms) [NGSS-MS, S5].
- *Contrast [TX] Gr 7:*
  - elements vs compounds, in terms of atoms, molecules, symbols and **chemical formulas**;
  - count the atoms in a formula using the periodic table;
  - physical vs chemical change;
  - aqueous solutions (solute, solvent, concentration, dilution);
  - rate of dissolving (temperature, surface area, agitation) [S27].

**Skills**
- Practical/lab: observe and measure properties; plan an investigation; engineering design cycle (construct, test, modify a hot or cold pack) [S3].
- Quantitative/math: analyze and interpret data tables of properties. Graphs are qualitative [S3].
- Representations/models: particle drawings and 3-D or ball-and-stick models of simple molecules; system models of energy transfer [S2].
- Inquiry/communication: the 8 NGSS science and engineering practices (questions, models, investigations, data, math, explanations, argument, communicating information) [S1, S2].

### Year 8 (Grade 8)

In discipline-based models, Grade 8 is often "physical science" (typical, unverified). In integrated NGSS models, MS-PS1 may sit here instead of in Gr 7. The NGSS-MS content is the same as in Year 7. Many states also place MS-PS3 thermal-energy work here.

**Concepts**
- *Matter & particles / Energetics:*
  - energy transfer, type of matter, mass and temperature change (ice melting, cooling rates);
  - design devices that minimize or maximize thermal energy transfer;
  - no calculation of total thermal energy [NGSS-MS, S4].
- *Chemical reactions:* NGSS-MS PS1 items as in Year 7 wherever the state places them in Gr 8 [S2, S3].
- *Contrast [TX] Gr 8:*
  - classify matter as elements, compounds, homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures;
  - use the periodic table to identify atoms in reactions;
  - **conservation of mass using chemical equations** (incl. photosynthesis);
  - **acids and bases, pH relative to water**;
  - cohesion, adhesion and surface tension of water [S27].
  - [TX] Gr 6 (earlier): metals, nonmetals, metalloids; density; evidence of chemical change (gas, precipitate, color, thermal energy) [S27].

**Skills**
- Practical/lab: controlled investigations of heating and cooling; measurement with balances, graduated cylinders, thermometers and probes, pH indicators [S4, S27].
- Quantitative/math: construct and interpret graphs; use ratios and proportional reasoning. Scientific notation is a Common Core Grade 8 math standard (8.EE, unverified: site returned 403).
- Representations/models: energy-flow diagrams; chemical formulas and simple equations [TX] [S27].
- Inquiry/communication: argument from evidence; engineering design with criteria and constraints [S4].

### Year 9 (Grade 9)

Most Grade 9 students take **biology**. In the HSLS:09 cohort (2009–10), 34% of 9th graders were in biology, 19.5% in physical science, 12.4% in earth science and only 1.7% in chemistry [S23]. Chemistry content in Year 9 comes through **physical science / IPC** or **Physics First**. [TX] IPC (§112.44) is recommended for Grades 9–10 [S26].

**Concepts**
- *Physical science / IPC (typical):* the chemistry half of a physical-science course (atoms, periodic table, bonding basics, reactions and balancing, solutions, acids and bases). Content varies by district (unverified in detail; TEKS §112.44 not read line by line).
- *Biochemistry (via biology):*
  - C, H and O from sugars recombine into amino acids and other large carbon-based molecules;
  - cellular respiration breaks and forms bonds with a net energy transfer;
  - photosynthesis stores chemical energy (no pathways) [NGSS-HS, HS-LS1-5/6/7, S9].
- *Industrial, environmental & applied:* carbon cycling among spheres (HS-LS2-5, HS-ESS2-6); properties of water (HS-ESS2-5). These sit in biology or earth-science courses [S9, S10].

**Skills**
- Practical/lab, representations, inquiry: NGSS practices applied in the biology or physical science context. Modelling inputs and outputs of matter and energy [S9].

### Year 10 (Grade 10)

The **first-year chemistry course** (typical for college-prep and honors students in Gr 10; others take it in Gr 11). NGSS HS-PS1 is banded 9–12. This is where Appendix K's "Chemistry" course usually lands [S11]. The list gives the NGSS minimum first, then the wider typical course, with TEKS §112.43 as the most explicit official list of the traditional scope (TEKS recommends Gr 10–12; prerequisites: one science credit plus Algebra I).

**Concepts**
- *Atomic structure:*
  - nucleus of protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons [NGSS-HS, S6];
  - [typical course / TX] history of atomic models (Dalton, Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr, Heisenberg);
  - isotopes and average atomic mass;
  - light and energy (E, ν, λ); emission spectra;
  - electron configurations and Lewis dot structures [S26].
- *Periodic table:*
  - periodic table as a model; properties predicted from outer-electron patterns (reactivity of metals, bond types, reactions with O₂). Main-group elements only; ionization energy as relative trends only [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-1, S6];
  - [TX] families (alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, halogens, noble gases, transition metals); trends in radius, electronegativity and ionization energy [S26].
- *Bonding & structure:*
  - electrical forces between particles explain bulk properties (melting/boiling point, vapor pressure, surface tension). NGSS does not require naming specific IMF types [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-3, S6];
  - molecular structure explains how designed materials work (metals conduct, long-chain molecules are flexible, drug–receptor fit) [HS-PS2-6, S6];
  - [TX] ionic, covalent and metallic bonding from electronegativity; **VSEPR shapes** (linear, bent, trigonal planar, trigonal pyramidal, tetrahedral) [S26].
- *Formulas & nomenclature:* [TX / typical course] name and write formulas for ionic and covalent compounds and acids/bases using **IUPAC rules** [S26].
- *Chemical reactions & equations:*
  - explain the outcome of simple reactions from outer electrons and periodic trends (Na + Cl₂; C + O₂; combustion) [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-2, S7];
  - [TX] balance equations; classify reactions as synthesis, decomposition, single replacement, double replacement or combustion; acid–base vs precipitation vs redox [S26].
- *Stoichiometry & the mole:*
  - **the mole** links atomic and macroscopic scales; mass is conserved (proportional reasoning, "not rote") [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-7, S7];
  - [TX] molar mass, Avogadro's number, percent composition, empirical vs molecular formula, mass and gas-volume stoichiometry, **limiting reactant**, **percent yield** [S26].
- *Gases:* [TX / typical course] kinetic molecular theory; P–V–n–T relationships (ideal gas); Dalton's law of partial pressures [S26].
- *Solutions & concentration:*
  - [TX] polarity of water; electrolytes; unsaturated, saturated and supersaturated solutions;
  - solubility curves; solubility rules and predicting precipitates;
  - **molarity** and dilution [S26].
- *Acids, bases & salts:* [TX] Arrhenius and Brønsted–Lowry; strong vs weak; neutralization; **pH from [H⁺]** [S26].
- *Energetics & thermodynamics:*
  - energy released or absorbed depends on the change in total bond energy; energy diagrams (no bond-energy calculations) [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-4, S7];
  - thermal equilibrium in closed systems (HS-PS3-4) [S8];
  - [TX] calorimetry; q = mcΔT; thermochemical equations; exo/endothermic; "four laws of thermodynamics" in everyday examples [S26].
- *Kinetics:* effect of temperature and concentration on rate, reasoned from the number and energy of collisions (two reactants, qualitative) [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-5, S7].
- *Equilibrium:* dynamic equilibrium; **Le Chatelier**: change one condition to increase the product yield. **No K calculations** [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-6, S7].
- *Nuclear chemistry:*
  - changes in nuclear composition in fission, fusion and α/β/γ decay; energy scale compared with chemical change (qualitative) [NGSS-HS, HS-PS1-8, S6];
  - [TX] balanced nuclear equations; applications [S26].
- *Industrial, environmental & applied:* in the Appendix K Chemistry course, also energy devices (HS-PS3-3), water properties (HS-ESS2-5), carbon cycle and climate (HS-ESS2-4/6), resource and climate solutions (HS-ESS3-2/5/6) [S11].

**Skills**
- Practical/lab:
  - plan and conduct investigations (bulk properties; calorimetry; rates);
  - [TX] at least **40% of instructional time** on lab and field investigations;
  - glassware: beakers, burettes, volumetric flasks, pipettes;
  - use Safety Data Sheets (SDS) [S6, S26].
- Quantitative/math:
  - mole and stoichiometry calculations (algebra);
  - SI units;
  - analyze data for error and limitations;
  - dimensional analysis (typical, unverified) [S7, S26].
- Representations/models:
  - periodic table as a model;
  - molecular-level drawings;
  - energy-level graphs;
  - Lewis structures and VSEPR;
  - particle diagrams [S6, S7, S26].
- Inquiry/communication:
  - construct and revise explanations;
  - engineering refinement of a chemical system (equilibrium);
  - communicate technical information;
  - argumentation [S6, S7, S26].

### Year 11 (Grade 11)

Three tracks run in parallel:
- **Chemistry (typical):** the same first-year course as Year 10, for the many students who take it in Gr 11 (see Year 10). An **[honors]** version adds more quantitative depth; its content is not standardised nationally (unverified).
- **[AP] AP Chemistry** for accelerated students (prerequisites: an introductory high school chemistry course and Algebra II). Taken in Gr 11 or 12; AP pacing is 5 × 45 min per week, with ≥25% of time hands-on lab and ≥16 labs (≥6 inquiry) [S16].
- **[IB]** DP Chemistry SL/HL year 1 (Structure / Reactivity organisation) [S30].

The AP units below apply to Year 11 or 12. They are listed once, here.

**Concepts [AP]** (unit, MCQ weighting) [S16, S17]
- *Stoichiometry & the mole / Atomic structure* (U1 Atomic Structure and Properties, 7–9%):
  - moles and molar mass; mass spectra of elements (average atomic mass); elemental composition; composition of mixtures;
  - electron configuration (aufbau exceptions excluded; quantum numbers excluded);
  - **photoelectron spectroscopy (PES)**; periodic trends explained with Coulomb's law and shielding; valence electrons and ionic compounds.
- *Bonding & structure* (U2 Compound Structure and Properties, 7–9%):
  - bond types; potential-energy vs internuclear-distance curves; ionic solids; metals and alloys;
  - Lewis diagrams, **resonance and formal charge**;
  - VSEPR plus **sp/sp²/sp³ hybridization**, σ and π bonds (MO diagrams not examined).
- *Gases / Solutions / Analytical* (U3 Properties of Substances and Mixtures, 18–22%, the heaviest unit):
  - IMFs; properties of solids;
  - ideal gas law, kinetic molecular theory, real-gas deviation;
  - solutions and particulate representations; **chromatography/distillation**; solubility;
  - EM spectrum and spectroscopy; photon energy; **Beer–Lambert law**;
  - excluded: phase diagrams, colligative properties, molality.
- *Chemical reactions* (U4 Chemical Reactions, 7–9%):
  - **net ionic equations**; physical vs chemical change; stoichiometry; titration;
  - reaction types; acid–base intro; redox with oxidation numbers ("oxidizing/reducing agent" terms not examined; solubility rules only for Na⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻ salts).
- *Kinetics* (U5 Kinetics, 7–9%):
  - rate laws; integrated rate laws (0, 1st, 2nd order); half-life;
  - elementary steps; collision model; energy profiles; mechanisms; pre-equilibrium approximation; catalysis;
  - no Arrhenius calculations.
- *Energetics & thermodynamics* (U6 Thermochemistry, 7–9%): calorimetry and heat capacity; phase-change energy; ΔH of reaction; bond enthalpies; enthalpy of formation; **Hess's law**.
- *Equilibrium* (U7 Equilibrium, 7–9%): Q vs K; Kc and Kp (no interconversion); ICE-type calculations; Le Chatelier with Q; **Ksp**; common-ion effect.
- *Acids, bases & salts* (U8 Acids and Bases, 11–15%):
  - pH/pOH; weak acid/base equilibria; Ka/Kb; buffers; **Henderson–Hasselbalch**; buffer capacity;
  - titration curves; molecular structure and acid strength; pH and solubility;
  - Lewis acids excluded.
- *Energetics & thermodynamics / Redox & electrochemistry* (U9 Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry, 7–9%):
  - entropy; **Gibbs free energy**; thermodynamic vs kinetic control; ΔG° = −RT ln K; coupled reactions;
  - galvanic and electrolytic cells; E°cell and ΔG°; **Nernst** (non-standard conditions, qualitative/semi-quantitative); electrolysis and Faraday's law.

**Skills [AP]** — six science practices [S16]:
1. Models and representations
2. Question and method
3. Representing data and phenomena
4. Model analysis
5. **Mathematical routines** (the heaviest on the exam)
6. Argumentation

- Practical/lab: 16 required hands-on labs, e.g. spectrophotometry, titration, buffers, rate law by Beer's law. Virtual labs do not count [S16].
- Quantitative/math: scientific or graphing calculator; the AP formula sheet (PV = nRT, Beer–Lambert, integrated rate laws, ΔG, Nernst) [S16].

### Year 12 (Grade 12)

**[AP]** AP Chemistry (as listed under Year 11) is most often taken here by students who took first-year chemistry in Gr 10 or 11 (typical, unverified). **[IB]** DP Chemistry year 2; the exams are in May of Gr 12 [S30]. Also a "second-year advanced" chemistry, offered by 45% of high schools [S24]. Many students take no chemistry, or take physics or electives instead. A general-track student who took biology and then physical science may take first-year chemistry here (typical, unverified).

**Concepts:** AP units 1–9 (see Year 11). IB DP topics: Structure 1–3, Reactivity 1–3, including organic functional groups and IUPAC naming, which are **not** in AP [S30] (IB topic detail unverified in this session).

**Skills:** AP science practices 1–6. IB internal assessment (scientific investigation) (unverified detail).

## 3. Terminology and notation conventions

- **Nomenclature:**
  - The TEKS explicitly require **IUPAC** naming of ionic and covalent compounds and acids [S26].
  - Classroom practice uses Stock names (iron(II) chloride) plus Greek prefixes for molecular compounds (dinitrogen tetroxide) (unverified).
  - Traditional -ous/-ic names (ferrous) appear only occasionally (unverified).
  - Polyatomic ion names such as nitrate, sulfate, ammonium and acetate are standard [S16].
- **Spelling:** American: *sulfur* (which matches IUPAC), ***aluminum*** (IUPAC: aluminium), ***cesium*** (IUPAC: caesium), *liter*, *meter*, *vapor*, *color*, *odor* [S2, S16].
- **Numbers:** decimal **point**, comma as the thousands separator; scientific notation written "6.022 × 10²³" [S16].
- **Units:**
  - SI in science courses ([TX] "collect quantitative data using SI") [S26].
  - The AP sheet uses g, mol, L, m, s, J, V, C, A, °C and K, **plus atm and mm Hg/torr** (1 atm = 760 mm Hg = 760 torr) [S16].
  - R is given as both **8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹** and **0.08206 L atm K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [S16].
  - **STP = 273.15 K and 1.0 atm; 22.4 L mol⁻¹** (AP). IUPAC STP is 100 kPa, so games should not mix the two [S16].
  - Everyday US units (°F, pounds, ounces, gallons) appear in contexts and problems (unverified) but not in the formal standards.
- **Concentration:** molarity **M** (mol/L); AP writes M = n_solute / L_solution. Molality, mass % and volume % are not examined in AP [S16].
- **Equations:**
  - state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq); single arrow →; equilibrium ⇌;
  - **net ionic equations** are central in AP;
  - ion charges are written number-then-sign as a superscript (Fe³⁺, SO₄²⁻) [S16].
- **Reaction-type vocabulary:** "synthesis, decomposition, **single replacement, double replacement**, combustion" (TEKS). UK usage is "displacement" [S26].
- **Acid-base terms:** "Brønsted–Lowry" (TEKS spells it "Bronsted-Lowry") and "Arrhenius". AP uses Ka, Kb, Kw, pKa and Henderson–Hasselbalch [S16, S26].
- **Periodic-table group labels:** both 1–18 and the older "Group 1A–8A / IIIB" style (CAS) appear in US textbooks (unverified). NGSS and TEKS use family names (alkali metals, halogens) [S26].
- **Other US-specific terms:**
  - "grade" (school year); "honors"; "AP" (Advanced Placement); "credit/Carnegie unit";
  - "physical science" (a course name); "IPC" (Texas Integrated Physics & Chemistry);
  - "performance expectation (PE)", "disciplinary core idea (DCI)", "crosscutting concept" (NGSS);
  - "TEKS"; "student expectation (SE)".
  - "Next Generation *Sunshine State* Standards" (Florida) is a different thing from NGSS (unverified).
