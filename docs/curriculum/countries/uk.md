# Chemistry curriculum: United Kingdom (Years 7–12)

**Purpose:** input for the cross-country concept/year map for chem-games.
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches in the UK.
**Key sources:** DfE National Curriculum KS3 (2013) and KS4 (2014) science programmes of study; DfE GCSE subject content (2015); Ofqual GCSE subject-level conditions; AQA 8462/8464/7405 and OCR J248/H432 specifications; Curriculum and Assessment Review final report and government response (Nov 2025); Qualifications Scotland N5/Higher Chemistry specifications (June 2026). Full list in section 6.

Scope: the main body covers **England**, which has the largest system and the one most UK resources are built on. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are handled in the contrast notes at the end of section 1. Education is devolved, so the four nations have different curricula.

## Summary for game design

- **Chemistry starts as part of integrated "science"** in primary school (states of matter in Year 4, properties and changes of materials in Year 5 [S34]). It becomes a clearly labelled strand of science in Key Stage 3 (Years 7–9, age 11–14) [S1]. The KS3 content is set for the whole key stage, not by year, so the order varies from school to school.
- **Many schools start GCSE content during Year 9** (Ofsted 2023 [S19]). In practice "KS3" is often only Years 7–8 plus part of Year 9. Treat Year 9 as a mix of KS3 and GCSE.
- **GCSE (Years 10–11) has two splits that matter for games:**
  - **Combined Science** (a double award, two GCSE grades, chemistry at least 30% of the content) vs **separate GCSE Chemistry** ("triple science"). In 2024/25 about 66% of pupils took Combined Science and about 23% took Chemistry [S13].
  - **Foundation vs Higher tier.** The **mole, reacting masses from moles, limiting reactants, half equations, ionic equations and equilibrium shifts are Higher tier only** [S5][S8]. Foundation-tier pupils never meet the mole at GCSE.
- **Big topics by year (typical placement):**
  - particle model, separation, acids/pH: Y7–8;
  - periodic table, reactivity series, simple equations: Y8–9;
  - atomic structure, bonding, quantitative chemistry, electrolysis, energetics: Y10;
  - rates, equilibrium, organic, analysis, atmosphere, resources: Y11 (typical, following AQA's Paper 1 / Paper 2 split [S8][S20]);
  - A level: physical, inorganic and organic topics at AS level in Y12; thermodynamics, rate equations, Kp, electrode potentials, pH/buffers, transition metals, aromatics, NMR in Y13 [S10][S11].
- **Maths weight:** at least 20% of GCSE Chemistry marks are maths skills (Combined Science: 20% overall, weighted 1:2:3 across biology, chemistry and physics) [S6][S7]. At least 15% of marks assess practical work, and it is examined in the written papers, not by coursework [S6]. There are **8 required practicals** in AQA GCSE Chemistry (6 in Combined Science) [S8], and **12 at A level** plus a separately reported pass/fail practical endorsement (CPAC) [S10].
- **Notation games must respect:**
  - spell **sulfur**, aluminium, caesium;
  - use state symbols (s)(l)(g)(aq);
  - volumes in **cm³/dm³**, concentration in **g/dm³ and mol/dm³** (mol dm⁻³ at A level);
  - GCSE electron configuration written **2,8,1**; s/p/d notation only at A level;
  - "Group 0" for the noble gases at GCSE;
  - UK lab vocabulary (conical flask, measuring cylinder, fume cupboard, Bunsen burner).
- **Surprises:**
  - Earth science (rock cycle, atmosphere, climate) sits in the **chemistry** strand in England [S1][S3].
  - Life-cycle assessment, potable water, nanoparticles and flame emission spectroscopy are GCSE topics [S8].
  - Radioactivity is **physics** in England, but **chemistry** in Scotland's National 5 [S22].
- **Reform:** nothing changes in the classroom in 2026/27.
  - A revised national curriculum is due to be published in spring 2027, for first teaching in **September 2028** [S14].
  - Reformed GCSEs will be first taught in 2029 or 2030. It is not yet confirmed which phase science is in.
  - Reformed A levels will be first taught in 2031 or 2032 [S14].
  - A **triple-science entitlement** is planned [S14].
  - Scotland: a new curriculum framework is due in August 2028 [S24], and N5/Higher exam formats changed in 2026/27 [S22][S23].
  - Wales: new science GCSEs began in September 2026 [S26].

**Reforms in force or being phased in for 2026/27 (England)**
- **Still in force in 2026/27:**
  - The national curriculum published in 2013: KS3 science statutory from Sept 2014 [S1][S2]; KS4 science programme of study from Dec 2014, taught with the new GCSEs from Sept 2016 [S3].
  - GCSE subject content from 2015 (first exams 2018) [S4][S5][S8].
  - A level subject content from 2014 (first teaching 2015, first exams 2017) [S10][S11].
- **Curriculum and Assessment Review (CAR):** final report and government response published on 5 Nov 2025 [S13][S14]. For science, the government accepted:
  - streamlining content and removing duplication;
  - clearer purposes for practical work;
  - more explicit climate science (greenhouse effect, emissions, fossil fuels, deforestation);
  - a review of which equations must be memorised;
  - a **triple science entitlement** "ahead of introducing a statutory entitlement" [S14].
- **CAR timeline:**
  - revised NC published by **spring 2027**, first teaching of the full new NC from **September 2028** (all key stages at once);
  - reformed GCSEs first taught in **2029 (phase 1) or 2030 (phase 2)**;
  - AS/A levels updated for **2031/2032** [S14].
  - The government has "no plans to substantially reform A levels" [S14].
- **Consultations:** DfE said the consultation on draft programmes of study and phase-1 GCSE subject content would open in **September 2026** [S17][S18]. I could not confirm whether it has opened as of 24 Sept 2026, or whether sciences are in phase 1 (see section 5).
- **Academies:** the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 Apr 2026) requires academies, which are most secondary schools, to teach the *refreshed* national curriculum [S14][S30]. In 2026/27 academies are still in practice free of the 2013 NC. GCSE content binds everyone anyway.
- **Equation sheets:** on 5 May 2026 Ofqual decided that equation sheets will be provided in GCSE **physics and combined science** exams from 2028 for the rest of the current specifications' lifetime [S15]. This affects the physics equations in Combined Science. Chemistry relationships (e.g. moles = mass ÷ Mr) are still recalled (unverified: I found no chemistry sheet).
- **Awarding body name:** OCR has traded as "Cambridge OCR" since Sept 2025. Specification content is unchanged [S11].
- **Also changed by 2026/27:** EBacc accountability measures were removed from the 2025/26 performance tables [S14]. They do not affect content.

**National exams that shape content**
- **GCSE**, Year 11. Awarding bodies: AQA (largest), OCR (Gateway A and Twenty First Century B suites), Pearson Edexcel, and WJEC Eduqas.
  - GCSE Chemistry: 2 papers of 1 h 45 min each (AQA 8462) [S8].
  - Combined Science Trilogy: 6 papers of 1 h 15 min each, 2 of them chemistry [S9].
  - Minimum total exam time: 3.5 h for a single science, 7 h for Combined Science [S6][S7].
- **A level**, Year 13. Linear course. AQA 7405 has 3 papers of 2 h each [S10]; OCR H432 has 3 papers [S11]. There is no national test at KS3, and KS2 science tests ended in 2009 [S13].

**Contrast: Scotland (Curriculum for Excellence; Qualifications Scotland, which replaced SQA on 1 Feb 2026 [S22])**
- **Year numbering:** primary P1–P7, secondary S1–S6. By the "count from first primary year" rule, **S1 = Year 8, S4 = Year 11, S5 = Year 12, S6 = Year 13**. P1 starts at about 4.5–5.5, so S1 starts at about 11.5–12.5 (similar in age to England Year 7/8).
- **S1–S3 (Years 8–10):** "Broad General Education". Sciences are taught as an integrated curriculum area against CfE third and fourth level Experiences & Outcomes (unverified: Es&Os text not opened).
- **S4 (Year 11), National 5 Chemistry (SCQF 5, 160 notional hours) [S22].** The course has three areas:
  - Chemical changes & structure: rates, atomic structure and bonding, formulae and reacting quantities, acids/bases;
  - Nature's chemistry: homologous series, consumer products, energy from fuels;
  - Chemistry in society: metals, plastics, fertilisers, **nuclear chemistry**, chemical analysis.
- **S5 (Year 12), Higher Chemistry [S23]:**
  - periodicity, bonding and electronegativity, oxidising/reducing agents;
  - esters, fats and oils, soaps, proteins, oxidation of food, fragrances, skin care;
  - getting the most from reactants, equilibria, enthalpy, analysis;
  - "Researching chemistry".
- **S6 (Year 13):** Advanced Higher.
- **2026/27 changes:**
  - N5 question paper cut from 100 to 80 marks and to 2 h [S22];
  - Higher's two papers merged into one 2 h 30 min paper [S23];
  - course content unchanged.
- **Curriculum Improvement Cycle:** full draft framework due in autumn 2026, new curriculum in schools from **August 2028** [S24]. A "Know–Do–Understand" model will replace the Es&Os (unverified detail).
- **Notation:** Scotland uses **mol l⁻¹**, valency rules for writing formulae, and "ion-electron equations" (England says "half equations") [S22].
- **Year-offset summary:** mole calculations, organic homologous series and nuclear chemistry all appear by Scottish Year 11 (S4). The mole appears for all N5 candidates, not only a higher tier.

**Wales:**
- Curriculum for Wales ("Science and Technology" Area of Learning and Experience) now covers all 3–16 learners from Sept 2026 [S27].
- New made-for-Wales GCSEs were first taught in **Sept 2026**: *The Sciences* (Double Award) and *Integrated Science* (Single Award) [S26].
- Separate GCSE Biology/Chemistry/Physics remain available "until at least summer 2031" [S26].
- Year labels are the same as in England.

**Northern Ireland:**
- Primary P1–P7, then post-primary Years 8–14. Counting P1 as Year 1, NI **Year 8 is the first post-primary year, at age 11**, so it matches England Year 7 by age.
- KS3 (Years 8–10): Science is a strand of the compulsory "Science and Technology" Area of Learning. At KS4 (Years 11–12, GCSE) science is not compulsory, but schools must give access to a science course (CCEA pages returned 403; this is from search snippets, unverified) [S29].
- A new statutory curriculum framework is under consultation until **30 Sept 2026** [S28].

**Typical interval:**
- National Curriculum revisions came in 1989, 1995, 2000, 2008 (KS3) and 2014, with the next in 2028. That is every 5–8 years until 2014, then a **14-year** gap.
- GCSE science changed in 2006, 2011 and 2016, and next in 2029/30: 5, 5, then about 13–14 years.
- A level changed in 2000, 2008 and 2015, and next in 2031/32: 7–8 years, then about 16.

**Scheduled or ad hoc:**
- England has **no statutory review cycle**. The government response says it "will always be open to a government to decide" whether to review. It also promises "light-touch updates in between longer-term holistic reviews", without a fixed cadence [S14].
- Reviews have been political or reform-driven.
- Scotland set up a standing "Curriculum Improvement Cycle" in Dec 2023 on OECD advice ("a systematic approach to curriculum review") [S25], but no fixed interval was found.

**Smaller updates:**
- Exam-board specification versions change often, but mostly for administration.
  - OCR H432 went through versions 2.0 to 3.1 between 2017 and May 2026, about 13 revisions [S11]. Content-relevant changes in that run were only the SI mole redefinition (Jan 2019) and bond-enthalpy guidance.
  - AQA 8462 is on v1.1 (Oct 2019) [S8].
- Qualifications Scotland reissued the N5 and Higher specifications in June 2026 with assessment changes only [S22][S23].
- Exam format tweaks (marks, paper length, formula sheets) happen about every 1–3 years.

**Announced or in progress now (24 Sept 2026):**
- England: draft programmes of study and phase-1 GCSE consultation (Sept 2026); final NC in spring 2027; first teaching Sept 2028; triple-science entitlement (no date).
- Scotland: CIC full draft in autumn 2026; implementation August 2028.
- Wales: new science GCSEs in their first year.
- NI: framework consultation closes 30 Sept 2026.

**Stability rating for game content: moderate churn.** Core chemistry (particles, atoms, bonding, moles, rates, equilibrium, organic basics) has barely changed in 20 years and will survive the reforms. But England's KS3–KS4 content will be restructured from 2028–2030. Expected changes:
- climate content added or moved;
- less GCSE content;
- possible changes to which topics are Higher tier or separate-chemistry only;
- a new sequence for Year 7–9.

So year and tier tags should be versioned, and re-checked after the spring 2027 publication.

## 2. Year-by-year concepts and skills

Placement within KS3 (Years 7–9) is **not set nationally** [S1]. The placements below are typical, based on Oak National Academy's sequence [S20]. Placement within Years 10–11 follows the usual teaching of AQA Paper 1 topics in Y10 and Paper 2 topics in Y11 (**typical**, unverified as a national norm).

### Year 7 (Year 7, KS3, age 11)
**Concepts**
- Matter & particles: solids, liquids and gases in the particle model; changes of state; gas pressure; density and the ice–water anomaly (physics strand); diffusion [S1][S20]
- Mixtures & separation: pure substance vs mixture; dissolving, solute, solvent, solution, solubility; filtration, evaporation, distillation, chromatography; identifying pure substances by melting point [S1][S20]
- Energetics: energy changes on changes of state (qualitative) [S1]
- Acids, bases & salts: acids and alkalis, indicators, pH scale. Oak places these in Y9; many schemes place them in Y7 (unverified) [S1][S20]

**Skills**
- Practical/lab: safe heating with a Bunsen burner; filtration and evaporation; paper chromatography; recording observations [S1]
- Quantitative/math: SI units; tables and graphs; means [S1]
- Representations/models: particle diagrams [S1]
- Inquiry/communication: asking questions, predicting, variables, fair tests, identifying random and systematic error [S1]

### Year 8 (Year 8, KS3, age 12)
**Concepts**
- Atomic structure: simple (Dalton) atomic model; atoms vs elements vs compounds [S1][S20]
- Formulas & nomenclature: chemical symbols; formulae of elements and compounds; IUPAC names [S1]
- Chemical reactions & equations: reactions as rearrangement of atoms; word and formula equations; conservation of mass; combustion, thermal decomposition, oxidation [S1][S20]
- Energetics: exothermic and endothermic reactions (qualitative); fuels [S1][S20]
- Industrial, environmental & applied: Earth as a source of limited resources; recycling; atmosphere composition; CO₂ from human activity and the climate [S1][S20]

**Skills**
- Practical: combustion and decomposition experiments; temperature change measurement [S1]
- Quantitative: simple mass-conservation calculations [S1]
- Models: particle and atom diagrams for elements, compounds and mixtures [S1]
- Inquiry: evaluating data against a prediction [S1]

### Year 9 (Year 9, KS3 end; GCSE often begins)
**Concepts (KS3 completion)**
- Periodic table: Mendeleev's principles; periods and groups; metals and non-metals; predicting reactions from the table; acidity of metal and non-metal oxides [S1][S20]
- Redox & electrochemistry: reactivity series (metals plus carbon); displacement reactions; extracting metals from oxides with carbon [S1]
- Acids, bases & salts: neutralisation defines acids and alkalis; acid + metal → salt + hydrogen; acid + alkali → salt + water [S1][S20]
- Kinetics: what catalysts do [S1]
- Polymers & materials: ceramics, polymers and composites (qualitative) [S1][S20]
- Inorganic/descriptive: composition and structure of the Earth; rock cycle (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic); carbon cycle [S1][S20]

**Concepts (GCSE start, where schools do this, typical)**
- Atomic structure: subatomic particles, electron shells [S19][S20]

**Skills**
- Practical: reactivity and displacement tests; neutralisation with indicators; gas tests, e.g. the hydrogen "pop" (typical) [S1]
- Quantitative: basic data analysis, simple statistics [S1]
- Models: balanced symbol equations (introduction) [S1]

### Year 10 (Year 10, KS4, GCSE year 1: typical placement)
Tags: [HT] = Higher tier only; [Sep] = separate GCSE Chemistry only (not in Combined Science).

**Concepts** [S3][S5][S8][S9][S20]
- Atomic structure: history of atomic models (plum pudding, Rutherford, Bohr, Chadwick); protons, neutrons, electrons; atomic and mass number; isotopes; relative atomic mass; electronic structure (2,8,8)
- Periodic table: development of the table; Group 0, Group 1, Group 7 trends; metals and non-metals; transition metals vs Group 1 [Sep]
- Bonding & structure:
  - ionic, covalent and metallic bonding; dot-and-cross diagrams;
  - giant ionic lattices; simple molecules; polymers;
  - giant covalent structures: diamond, graphite, graphene, fullerenes;
  - alloys;
  - limitations of bonding models [HT];
  - nanoparticles and their uses [Sep]
- Matter & particles: three states in terms of bonds and forces; limitations of the particle model [HT]
- Stoichiometry & the mole:
  - conservation of mass; relative formula mass;
  - mass changes when a gas is involved;
  - the mole and Avogadro constant, reacting masses, balancing from masses, limiting reactants [HT];
  - percentage yield and atom economy [Sep];
  - gas volumes (24 dm³) [Sep][HT]
- Solutions & concentration: concentration in g/dm³; mol/dm³ [Sep][HT]
- Chemical reactions & equations: balanced equations with state symbols; ionic equations and half equations [HT]
- Acids, bases & salts: reactions of acids with metals, oxides, hydroxides and carbonates; making soluble salts; pH and neutralisation (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O); titration [Sep]; strong vs weak acids and a 10× change in [H⁺] per pH unit [HT]
- Redox & electrochemistry: reactivity series; extraction by carbon reduction; oxidation and reduction as electron transfer [HT]; electrolysis of molten and aqueous compounds; aluminium extraction; electrode half equations [HT]
- Energetics: exo- and endothermic reactions; reaction profiles and activation energy; bond energy calculations [HT]; chemical cells and fuel cells [Sep]

**Skills**
- Required practicals (AQA; Combined Science numbers are different):
  - RP1 make a pure dry soluble salt from an insoluble oxide or carbonate;
  - RP2 titration [Sep], including calculating concentration [HT];
  - RP3 electrolysis of aqueous solutions (hypothesis-led);
  - RP4 temperature changes in reacting solutions [S8]
- Quantitative: Ar/Mr; percentages; significant figures; standard form; ratio; changing the subject of an equation [S5][S8]
- Models: dot-and-cross, ball-and-stick and 2D/3D structures; knowing the limitations of each model [S8]
- Inquiry: developing hypotheses; planning; evaluating methods [S8]

### Year 11 (Year 11, KS4, GCSE exams: typical placement)
**Concepts** [S3][S5][S8][S9][S20]
- Kinetics: measuring rate (mean rate; gradient of a tangent [HT]); effect of concentration, pressure, surface area, temperature and catalysts; collision theory; activation energy
- Equilibrium: reversible reactions; dynamic equilibrium; Le Chatelier's principle for concentration, temperature and pressure [HT]
- Organic chemistry:
  - crude oil; alkanes (C1–C4); fractional distillation; properties of hydrocarbons; combustion; cracking;
  - alkenes, alcohols and carboxylic acids [Sep]; esters (ethyl ethanoate) [Sep]; why carboxylic acids are weak [Sep][HT]
- Polymers & materials: addition polymerisation [Sep]; condensation polymers [Sep][HT]; amino acids [Sep][HT]; DNA, proteins, starch, cellulose [Sep]; corrosion prevention, alloys, glass, ceramics, composites, polymers [Sep]
- Analytical & instrumental:
  - pure substances and formulations; chromatography and Rf;
  - tests for H₂, O₂, CO₂ and Cl₂;
  - flame tests, hydroxide precipitates, carbonate, halide and sulfate tests [Sep];
  - instrumental methods and flame emission spectroscopy [Sep]
- Industrial, environmental & applied:
  - evolution of the atmosphere; greenhouse gases and climate change; carbon footprint; pollutants (CO, SO₂, NOx, particulates);
  - finite and renewable resources; potable water; waste water treatment;
  - phytomining and bioleaching [HT]; life-cycle assessment; reduce, reuse, recycle;
  - Haber process [Sep], including the effect of conditions [HT]; NPK fertilisers [Sep]

**Skills**
- Required practicals (AQA):
  - RP5 rate of reaction by gas volume and by colour/turbidity;
  - RP6 paper chromatography with Rf;
  - RP7 identifying ions [Sep];
  - RP8 analysing and purifying water samples [S8]
- Quantitative: plotting and interpreting rate graphs; tangents [HT]; Rf calculation [S8]
- Models: displayed formulae of alkanes and alkenes; repeating units of polymers [S8]
- Inquiry: evaluating life-cycle assessments; weighing evidence about climate; peer review and the provisional nature of models [S5][S8]

### Year 12 (Year 12 / Lower Sixth, A level year 1: typical placement = AS content)
All items [A level], and only for students who choose A level Chemistry. Tags are from AQA 7405 [S10] unless stated; OCR H432 Modules 1–4 correspond [S11].

**Concepts**
- Atomic structure: s/p/d electron configurations; ionisation energies; time-of-flight mass spectrometry
- Stoichiometry & the mole: amount of substance; ideal gas equation pV = nRT; empirical and molecular formulae; percentage yield and atom economy; titration calculations
- Bonding & structure: ionic, covalent, dative and metallic bonding; VSEPR shapes; electronegativity and polarity; intermolecular forces (London forces, permanent dipoles, hydrogen bonding)
- Energetics & thermodynamics: enthalpy change; calorimetry (q = mcΔT); Hess's law; mean bond enthalpies
- Kinetics: collision theory; Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution; catalysts
- Equilibrium: Le Chatelier's principle; Kc
- Redox & electrochemistry: oxidation states; redox half equations
- Periodic table: periodicity across Period 3; Group 2; Group 7 (17) halogens and halide ions
- Organic chemistry:
  - nomenclature; structural and E/Z isomerism; curly-arrow mechanisms;
  - alkanes (cracking, free-radical substitution);
  - halogenoalkanes (nucleophilic substitution and elimination; ozone depletion);
  - alkenes (electrophilic addition, addition polymers);
  - alcohols (production, oxidation, elimination)
- Analytical & instrumental: organic tests; mass spectrometry; infrared spectroscopy

**Skills**
- Required practicals 1–6:
  - volumetric solution and acid–base titration;
  - enthalpy change;
  - rate vs temperature;
  - test-tube ion tests (Group 2 ions, NH₄⁺, halides, OH⁻, CO₃²⁻, SO₄²⁻);
  - distillation of a product;
  - tests for alcohol, aldehyde, alkene and carboxylic acid [S10]
- CPAC practical endorsement (runs over 2 years) [S10]
- Quantitative: at least 20% of marks at Level 2 maths or above; uncertainties; logarithms introduced for pH in Y13 [S10]
- Models: skeletal and displayed formulae; mechanisms; 3D shapes [S10]
- Inquiry: evaluating methods; measurement uncertainty; identifying variables [S10]

### Year 13 (Upper Sixth) — outside 7–12, shown for context (typical)
- Thermodynamics (Born–Haber cycles, entropy, Gibbs free energy); rate equations and orders; Kp; electrode potentials and cells; acids and bases (pH, Ka, buffers)
- Period 3 oxides; transition metals (complexes, colour, catalysis); ions in aqueous solution
- Optical isomerism; carbonyls; carboxylic acids and derivatives; aromatics; amines; condensation polymers; amino acids, proteins and DNA; organic synthesis; NMR; chromatography
- Required practicals 7–12 [S10]; OCR Modules 5–6 [S11]

## 3. Terminology and notation conventions
- **Nomenclature:** the KS3 programme of study requires "IUPAC chemical nomenclature" [S1].
  - GCSE uses systematic names for simple organics (ethanol, ethanoic acid, ethene; not acetic acid or ethylene) [S8].
  - A level uses full IUPAC locants (propan-2-ol, but-2-ene, E/Z) [S10].
  - Roman numerals for variable-oxidation-state ions (iron(II), iron(III)) [S12].
  - Common names survive for everyday substances (limewater, bleach, crude oil) [S8].
- **Spellings:** sulfur, sulfate, sulfuric acid (f-spelling throughout; older "sulphur" appears only in legacy material); aluminium; caesium; litre; "fibre" [S8][S22].
- **Units:**
  - volume in cm³ and dm³ in England (not mL or L at GCSE);
  - concentration in g/dm³ and mol/dm³ (written mol dm⁻³ at A level);
  - Scotland writes l and **mol l⁻¹** [S8][S10][S22];
  - energy in kJ and kJ/mol; temperature in °C (K at A level);
  - gas molar volume 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (20 °C, 1 atm) [Sep][HT] [S8].
- **Symbols:**
  - Ar and Mr for relative atomic and formula mass;
  - Avogadro constant 6.02 × 10²³ per mole [S8];
  - Rf; ΔH is negative for exothermic reactions;
  - nuclide notation (mass number on top) [S22].
- **Equations:**
  - word and balanced symbol equations with state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq), required at KS4 [S3];
  - reversible reactions use ⇌;
  - ionic equations and electrode **"half equations"** at GCSE [HT]; Scotland calls these **"ion-electron equations"** [S22];
  - electrons shown as e⁻.
- **Electronic structure:** 2,8,1 (commas) at GCSE; 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ at A level.
- **Periodic table labels:** "Group 1, Group 7, Group 0" at GCSE; A level uses "Group 7(17)" [S8][S10].
- **Tier and track vocabulary:** Foundation / Higher tier; Combined Science (Trilogy, Synergy) / "triple" or "separate" science; "required practicals" (AQA), "PAGs" (OCR), "core practicals" (Pearson).
