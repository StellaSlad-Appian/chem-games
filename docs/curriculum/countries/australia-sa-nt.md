# South Australia and Northern Territory (SACE Board): Stage 1 and Stage 2 Chemistry, with organic chemistry, kinetics, energetics and equilibrium

**Purpose:** the reference curriculum for South Australia and the Northern Territory, for the organic
chemistry and energetics games planned in
[`../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md`](../../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md). The other states and territories
are compared in [`australia-overview.md`](../australia-overview.md). Years 7–10 are covered by
[`australia-curriculum-v9-7-10.md`](./australia-curriculum-v9-7-10.md).
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent, from primary sources).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches SACE or NTCET Chemistry.

Primary sources: the SACE Board's Stage 1 and Stage 2 Chemistry subject outlines (Word files,
text extracted in full), the Chemistry data sheet and periodic table, the 2025 Stage 2 Chemistry
examination paper, the 2025 Chemistry Subject Assessment Advice, the SACE subject renewal pages,
and the SACE NTCET page. Full URL list at the end. Anything not confirmed from a primary source is
marked **(unverified)**.

## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **Organic chemistry in SA/NT is two blocks a year apart.** Hydrocarbon naming, structural
  isomers, alkene addition and addition polymers are in **Stage 1 (usually Year 11), Topic 3**.
  Every functional group (alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, esters, amides)
  is in **Stage 2 (Year 12), Topic 3 "Organic and biological chemistry"**. Stage 2 leads into
  carbohydrates, triglycerides and proteins. A game should tag content "Stage 1 3.3" or
  "Stage 2 3.x", not only a year.
- **Stage 1 is not guaranteed coverage.** Teachers choose "a selection of concepts" from the six
  Stage 1 topics. A 10-credit student may skip Topic 3 entirely. So Stage 2 games cannot assume
  hydrocarbon naming was taught; they need a short refresher.
- **The naming limits are explicit and small.** Main chain of **up to eight carbons**, side
  chains of **at most two carbons** (methyl, ethyl). Multiple groups of the same kind are allowed
  ("one or more hydroxyl groups", "one or two carboxyl groups"). Esters are named only as
  **methyl and ethyl esters**. Amines are named only if **primary**. Amides are drawn but have no
  naming requirement. A naming generator for SA must respect these limits.
- **Several framework skills are absent in SA/NT.** There are no haloalkanes, no substitution
  reactions, no cis/trans or E/Z isomerism, no optical isomers, no IR/NMR/mass spectrometry of
  organic compounds, no percentage yield or atom economy, and no Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.
  These games must be hideable for SA/NT players.
- **Energetics is thin and practical.** SA/NT covers exo/endo, energy profile diagrams,
  calorimetry with **Q = mcΔT** and the ΔH/Q/n relationship on the data sheet, enthalpy of
  solution, and fuels compared **per mole, per gram and per litre**. There is **no Hess's law,
  no ΔfH°, no bond-enthalpy calculation, and no entropy or Gibbs energy**. The strongest
  energetics game here is a fuel-comparison or calorimetry game, not a Hess's-law puzzle.
- **Mind the marking language.** The 2025 assessment advice says students wrongly wrote that
  particles "overcome activation energy". It also rejects up/down arrows for "increase" and
  "decrease", and undefined abbreviations such as "ROR" (rate of reaction). Game feedback text
  should model the accepted wording.
- **The subject outlines will not change before 2028 at the earliest.** Chemistry is in the
  SACE Board's subject renewal "group 2". No accreditation date has been announced for group 2
  (see section 0).

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 The certificates

- **SACE (South Australian Certificate of Education)**:
  - 200 credits in total
  - compulsory:
    - Stage 1 *Exploring Identities and Futures* (10 credits, Year 10 or 11)
    - literacy: an English subject, 20 credits
    - numeracy: a mathematics subject, 10 credits
    - Stage 2 *Activating Identities and Futures* (AIF, 10 credits; formerly the Research
      Project)
  - at least 60 credits at Stage 2
  - pass grades: C or better at Stage 1, C– or better at Stage 2
- **NTCET (Northern Territory Certificate of Education and Training)**: the same 200-credit
  model, administered by the SACE Board.
  - The SACE NTCET page lists: literacy 20 credits, numeracy 10 credits, Exploring Identities and
    Futures 10 credits, and "at least 60 credits at Stage 2".
  - It says "In order to gain an Australian Tertiary Admission Rank (ATAR), students are
    required to successfully complete 90 credits of Stage 2 Tertiary Admissions Subjects."
- **ATAR:** calculated by SATAC from 90 credits of Stage 2 subjects (secondary sources).
  Chemistry is a Tertiary Admission Subject **(unverified: not checked on the SATAC list)**.
- **Credits and time:** 10 credits is one semester and 20 credits is a full year. The hours per
  credit were not confirmed from a SACE source **(unverified)**. The Stage 2 outline recommends
  "a minimum of 16–20 hours of class time involves practical activities" for a 20-credit subject.

### 1.2 Stage 1 Chemistry (usually Year 11; can start in Year 10)

- 10 credits (code **1CEM10**) or 20 credits (**1CEM20**).
- Six topics:
  1. Materials and their atoms
  2. Combining atoms (listed as "Combinations of atoms" in the topic list)
  3. Molecules
  4. Mixtures and solutions
  5. Acids and bases
  6. Redox reactions
- **10 credits:** "a selection of concepts from at least three of these topics".
  **20 credits:** "a selection of concepts from all six topics".
  "Topics can be studied in their entirety or in part."
- **Assessment is 100% school-based.** There are two assessment types:
  - *Investigations Folio*: practical investigations plus a "science as a human endeavour" (SHE)
    investigation
  - *Skills and Applications Tasks*
  - Each type is weighted at least 20%.
  - 10 credits: three or four assessments. 20 credits: six or seven assessments.
- Grades A–E against the performance standards.

### 1.3 Stage 2 Chemistry (Year 12)

- 20 credits (code **2CEM20**). Four compulsory topics, in any order:
  1. Monitoring the environment
  2. Managing chemical processes
  3. Organic and biological chemistry
  4. Managing resources
- **Assessment:**
  - **School assessment, 70%:**
    - Assessment Type 1, *Investigations Folio* (30%): one or two practical investigations and
      one SHE investigation. Reports are at most 1500 words or 9 minutes.
    - Assessment Type 2, *Skills and Applications Tasks* (40%): three or four tasks. At least
      two are supervised, each at most 90 minutes.
  - **External assessment, 30%:** Assessment Type 3, *Examination*. It is marked by
    SACE-appointed markers.
  - In total, six or seven assessments, including the exam.
- **The examination:** "Students undertake a 130-minute examination." Stage 2 content "from all
  topics may be assessed".
  - The 2025 paper had two question booklets. Booklet 1 had questions 1–3 (59 marks, about 65
    minutes). The total was 120 marks.
  - Students write in the booklet and may use approved calculators.
- **Data sheet:** "a sheet containing a periodic table, standard SI prefixes, symbols of common
  quantities, some mathematical relationships, and a table showing the relative activities of a
  number of metals". The version in use says it "will be provided in Chemistry examinations from
  November 2019". Contents are in section 4.
- **Assessment design criteria:**
  - Investigation, Analysis and Evaluation (IAE1–IAE4)
  - Knowledge and Application (KA1–KA4)
  - Grades A–E. School and exam grades are combined into a final A+ to E– result.
- **Moderation:** school assessment is quality-assured by the SACE Board.

### 1.4 The three strands

Both stages integrate:

- *science inquiry skills*
- *science as a human endeavour* (key concepts: Communication and Collaboration, Development,
  Influence, Application and Limitation)
- *science understanding*

Each subtopic has two columns:

- **"Science Understanding"**: the mandatory content, a statement followed by assessable verb
  dot points
- **"Possible contexts"**: suggestions only, "neither comprehensive nor exclusive"

In section 2, content from the second column is marked *(context)*.

## 2. Content by stage and topic

### Stage 1 (Year 11)

**Topic 1: Materials and their atoms.**

- 1.1 Properties and uses of materials, including nanomaterials; separation (filtration,
  distillation, evaporation)
- 1.2 Atomic structure: spectra, isotopes, subshell configurations for the first 38 elements
- 1.3 Quantities of atoms: the mole
- 1.4 The periodic table: trends in radius, valency and electronegativity; s/p/d/f blocks

**Topic 2: Combining atoms.**

- 2.1 Types of materials: metallic, ionic, covalent network, molecular
- 2.2 Bonding between atoms. **The energetics seed is here:** "Energy is released when bonds are
  formed. Energy is required to break bonds." Also: electron-dot diagrams, bond polarity,
  structural formulae.
- 2.3 Quantities of molecules and ions: percentage composition, n = m/M, empirical formulae

**Topic 3: Molecules. This is the Stage 1 organic block.**

- **3.1 Molecule polarity:** VSEPR shapes (one central atom, up to eight valence electrons) and
  molecular polarity.
- **3.2 Interactions between molecules:** dispersion forces ("Their strength depends on the size
  and shape of the molecules"), dipole–dipole forces, hydrogen bonding (HF, H₂O, NH₃).
  - *(context)* compare the viscosity of propan-1-ol, propane-1,2-diol and propane-1,2,3-triol.
- **3.3 Hydrocarbons:**
  - "Carbon forms hydrocarbon compounds, including alkanes and alkenes. The physical properties
    of hydrocarbons depend on the size of the molecules."
  - Compare melting and boiling points.
  - "Write equations for the complete combustion of hydrocarbons."
  - "The chemical reactions of hydrocarbons are determined by the functional groups present."
    Dot point: "Predict the product of an addition reaction of an alkene."
  - *(context)* compare cyclohexane and cyclohexene with bromine or iodine solution.
  - Formula types: "Hydrocarbons can be represented by empirical formulae, molecular formulae,
    and structural formulae, including extended, condensed, and skeletal representations."
  - "Hydrocarbons can exist as different structural isomers."
  - Naming (quoted dot point): "Identify, name systematically, and draw structural formulae of
    hydrocarbons containing: up to eight carbon atoms in the main chain, with side chains limited
    to a maximum of two carbon atoms; one or more alkene groups."
  - Determine empirical and molecular formulae from composition and molar mass.
  - "Organic molecules have a hydrocarbon skeleton and can contain functional groups." A note
    says teachers "may consider introducing some of the functional groups included in Stage 2".
  - *(context)* ester preparation, and the boiling points of methanol, ethanol and propanol.
  - *(context)* IUPAC nomenclature as an example of an international scientific protocol.
- **3.4 Polymers:** repeating units; addition polymerisation from alkene monomers (one C=C);
  properties depend on the interactions between chains. Condensation polymers are deferred to
  Stage 2.

**Topic 4: Mixtures and solutions.**

- 4.1 Miscibility and solutions: polar vs non-polar solvents; solubility from polarity and size;
  emulsifiers.
- 4.2 Solutions of ionic substances: ion–dipole interactions, precipitation.
- 4.3 Quantities in reactions: c = n/V, mass concentration, stoichiometry.
- **4.4 Energy in reactions. This is the Stage 1 energetics block.**
  - Endothermic = "gain of energy" and exothermic = "loss of energy". These are explained "in
    terms of the Law of Conservation of Energy and the breaking and forming of bonds".
  - Dissolving ionic substances: dissociation requires energy, hydration releases energy. Write
    thermochemical equations for dissolving.
  - "Enthalpy changes for solution reactions can be determined experimentally." Students explain
    and calculate with two relationships. They are embedded as equation images; decoded, they
    are **Q = mcΔT** and a relationship between **ΔH, Q and n**. The same pair is on the exam
    data sheet (see section 4).
  - Identify a reaction as exothermic or endothermic "given a thermochemical equation or the
    value of its enthalpy change".
  - *(context)* hand warmers, cold packs, enthalpy of solution of sodium thiosulfate or
    ammonium chloride.

**Topic 5: Acids and bases.**

- 5.1 Brønsted–Lowry, conjugate pairs, indicators, monoprotic/polyprotic. A note says conjugate
  pairs "may be used as an introduction to reversible reactions".
- 5.2 Reactions of acids and bases: "Neutralisation is an exothermic reaction."
- 5.3 The pH scale: pH = −log[H⁺]; acid rain.

**Topic 6: Redox reactions.**

- 6.1 Concepts of oxidation and reduction.
  - *(context)* discuss "why oxidation number is not used in organic chemistry, and why the
    concept of gain and loss of oxygen and hydrogen is more useful".
- 6.2 Metal reactivity: the activity series.
- 6.3 Electrochemistry: galvanic cells.

### Stage 2 (Year 12)

**Topic 1: Monitoring the environment.**

- 1.1 Global warming and climate change; ocean acidification ("Explain, using equilibrium
  principles, the impact of altering ocean pH on the formation of carbonates").
- 1.2 Photochemical smog: NOx, ozone, **catalytic converters**.
- 1.3 Volumetric analysis: concentration units mol L⁻¹, g L⁻¹, %w/v, ppm, ppb; titration.
- **1.4 Chromatography:** TLC, GC, HPLC, ion chromatography.
  - Predict relative rates of movement from polarity and charge.
  - "Calculate and apply RF values and retention times".
  - Ion exchange explained with equilibrium principles.
- **1.5 Atomic spectroscopy:** flame tests and AAS (atomic absorption spectroscopy);
  calibration graphs. Configurations for the first 38 elements, including ions.

**Topic 2: Managing chemical processes. Kinetics and equilibrium are here.**

- **2.1 Rates of reactions:**
  - "The rates of a reaction at different times can be compared by considering the slope of a
    graph of quantity or concentration of reactant or product against time." Draw and interpret
    such graphs.
  - "Predict and explain, using collision theory, the effect on rates of reaction due to changes
    in: concentration; temperature; pressure (for reactions involving gases); surface area; the
    presence of a catalyst." Catalysts include "inorganic and biological catalysts (enzymes)".
  - "Energy profile diagrams can be used to represent the relative enthalpies of reactants and
    products, the activation energy, and the enthalpy change for a chemical reaction." Dot point:
    "Draw and interpret energy profile diagrams."
  - *(context)* marble chips + HCl mass-loss graph; designing rate experiments (concentration,
    particle size, temperature or pH of an enzyme reaction); nanoparticle catalysts.
  - **Not in the outline:** rate laws, reaction order, rate constants, the Arrhenius equation,
    and the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. The Science Inquiry Skills table does list
    "calculating mean values and rates of reaction, where appropriate".
- **2.2 Equilibrium and yield:**
  - "Chemical systems may be open or closed." Reversible reactions in a closed system at fixed
    temperature "eventually reach a state of chemical equilibrium". Graph concentration changes.
  - Kc:
    - "Write Kc expressions that correspond to given reaction equations for homogeneous
      equilibrium systems."
    - "Undertake calculations involving Kc and initial and/or equilibrium quantities of
      reactants and products for homogeneous equilibrium systems."
  - "Predict and explain, using Le Châtelier's principle, the effect on the equilibrium position
    of a system of a change in the: concentration of a reactant or product; overall pressure of a
    gaseous mixture; temperature of an equilibrium mixture for which the ΔH value for the forward
    or back reaction is specified."
  - Reverse inference: from the observed shift, deduce the change that occurred or whether the
    reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
  - *(context)* CrO₄²⁻/Cr₂O₇²⁻ and CoCl₂/CoCl₂·6H₂O colour equilibria, NO₂/N₂O₄, Fe(SCN)²⁺,
    haemoglobin/CO, SO₂ in wine.
- **2.3 Optimising production:**
  - "Designing chemical-synthesis processes involves constructing reaction pathways that may
    include more than one chemical reaction."
  - Flow charts (raw materials, by-products, waste products).
  - "Explain how certain reaction conditions represent a compromise that will give maximum yield
    in a short time."
  - Costs and environmental impact of higher temperature and pressure.
  - "Explain how use of a catalyst may benefit both the manufacturer and the environment."
  - *(context)* ammonia, nitric acid and sulfuric acid production; Fritz Haber; the green
    chemistry principle of minimising energy.

**Topic 3: Organic and biological chemistry. This is the Stage 2 organic block.**

- **3.1 Introduction:**
  - "Determine the molecular formula of an organic compound given its extended, condensed, or
    skeletal structural formula."
  - "Organic compounds are named systematically to provide unambiguous identification."
  - "Condensation reactions occur when two organic molecules combine to form a larger molecule,
    also releasing another small molecule, such as water."
  - "Predict, explain, and compare the melting points, boiling points, and solubilities in water
    and in non-polar solvents of organic compounds, given their structural formulae."
  - *(context)* uses of propanone and butanone as solvents; esters as flavours; ethyl ethanoate
    to decaffeinate.
- **3.2 Alcohols:**
  - "Alcohols are classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary."
  - Name and draw: up to 8 C in the main chain, side chains of at most 2 C, "one or more hydroxyl
    groups".
  - "Describe how primary and secondary alcohols can be distinguished from tertiary alcohols by
    their reaction with acidified dichromate solution."
  - "Predict the structural formula(e) of the product(s) of oxidation of a primary or secondary
    alcohol".
- **3.3 Aldehydes and ketones:**
  - Produced by oxidising primary and secondary alcohols respectively. Same naming limits, with
    "one or more aldehyde or ketone groups".
  - "Aldehydes can be readily oxidised; ketones cannot." Draw the oxidation product "in either
    acidic or alkaline conditions".
  - "Describe how acidified dichromate solution and Tollens reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate
    solution) can be used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones."
- **3.4 Carbohydrates:** polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones; ring–chain equilibrium; condensation
  to disaccharides and polysaccharides; identify the monomer from a polysaccharide section.
- **3.5 Carboxylic acids:**
  - Name and draw: up to 8 C, "one or two carboxyl groups".
  - Weak acids. Reactions with hydroxides, carbonates and hydrogencarbonates to form carboxylate
    salts.
  - Why Na and K carboxylates are more soluble than the parent acids.
- **3.6 Amines:**
  - Classified primary, secondary or tertiary, but naming is required only for **primary amines**
    (up to 8 C, "one or more amino groups").
  - Amines act as bases. Draw the protonated form, and explain why it is more soluble.
- **3.7 Esters:**
  - "Identify, name systematically, and draw structural formulae of methyl and ethyl esters of
    acids containing up to eight carbon atoms in the main chain, with side chains limited to a
    maximum of two carbon atoms."
  - Draw the ester from an acid and an alcohol, or the reverse; draw a polyester from its
    monomers, or the reverse.
  - "Condensation reactions are slow at 25°C." "Explain the use of heating under reflux, and the
    use of a trace of concentrated sulfuric acid in the laboratory preparation of esters."
  - "Esters may be hydrolysed under acidic or alkaline conditions."
- **3.8 Amides:** condensation of a carboxylic acid with an amine; polyamides; acidic or alkaline
  hydrolysis (*(context)* paracetamol, Kevlar, nylon). **No naming requirement.**
- **3.9 Triglycerides:**
  - Esters of "propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol)" and carboxylic acids; hydrolysis.
  - Saturated vs unsaturated: "Describe and explain the use of a solution of bromine or iodine to
    determine the degree of unsaturation of a compound. Draw the structural formula of the
    reaction product."
  - Melting points; catalytic hydrogenation; alkaline hydrolysis to carboxylate ions and
    micelles.
- **3.10 Proteins:**
  - General formula of amino acids; self-ionisation (zwitterion); condensation to protein chains
    ("peptide links").
  - Secondary interactions; function affected by pH and temperature.
  - *(context)* α-amino acids.

**Topic 4: Managing resources. This is the Stage 2 energetics block.**

- **4.1 Energy:**
  - Photosynthesis and respiration equations.
  - Fossil fuels vs biofuels. Production of bioethanol and biodiesel, "including the writing of
    chemical equations".
  - "Write thermochemical equations for the complete combustion of fuels in which the only
    products are carbon dioxide and water."
  - "Explain why incomplete combustion is more likely with longer-chain carbon-based fuels".
  - "Use experimental data to determine the enthalpy of combustion of a fuel." "Undertake
    thermochemical calculations involving enthalpy changes and temperature changes of a
    specified mass of water given the necessary data."
  - "Calculate the quantities of heat evolved per mole, per gram, and per litre (for liquids) for
    the complete combustion of fuels." "Compare fuels given appropriate data."
  - *(context)* spirit-burner enthalpy of combustion of an alcohol, with error analysis.
  - Photovoltaic cells vs fuel cells (including flow cells); electrode half-equations; hydrogen
    from fossil fuels, biomass or water.
- 4.2 Water: flocculation, zeolites, reverse osmosis, desalination, the chlorine/hypochlorous
  acid equilibrium and pH.
- 4.3 Soil: N/P/K, eutrophication, cation exchange on silicates as an equilibrium.
- **4.4 Materials:**
  - **Polymers:** addition vs condensation, identifying the repeating unit, thermoplastic vs
    thermoset, cross-linking, fossil vs renewable feedstocks, biodegradability, green chemistry.
  - **Metals:** extraction; reduction method from the activity series; electrolytic cells.
  - **Recycling:** composites.

## 4. Conventions and notation

**Nomenclature (from the outlines, the 2025 exam and the 2025 assessment advice):**

- **Locants go immediately before the suffix they refer to:** *propane-1,2,3-triol*,
  *propane-1,2-diol*, *propan-1-ol* (outline), *ethane-1,2-diamine* (2025 exam). Not
  "1-propanol".
- Systematic names are used alongside trivial names in the outline: *ethanal*, *ethanoic acid*
  (with "vinegar"), *propanone (acetone)*, *butanone*, *ethyl ethanoate*,
  *propane-1,2,3-triol (glycerol)*.
- Esters are named alkyl alkanoate (*ethyl ethanoate*), and only methyl and ethyl esters are
  required.
- Isomer vocabulary is "structural isomers" only. There is no cis/trans or E/Z.
- The 2025 advice: "Teachers should not be using the typical general formula of CxH2yOy as part of
  the definition of a carbohydrate as not all carbohydrates obey this ratio."

**Formula representations:**

- Required: empirical, molecular, and structural formulae "extended, condensed, and skeletal".
- The 2025 advice on drawing:
  - Full structures must show every hydrogen. Students "who chose to draw a skeletal formula did
    not always do so successfully".
  - A polymer section needs open bonds at each end, with dots or a wavy line. Square brackets
    were "not required" and were penalised when misused.
  - "If carbons are drawn, the correct number of bonded hydrogens also need to be shown."

**Energetics notation:**

- **Q** for heat energy and **c** for specific heat capacity. **c** is also used for molar
  concentration; the data sheet lists both.
- **Thermochemical equations** (2025 advice, Q7(b)(iii)):
  - The coefficient of the fuel is kept at 1, with fractions if needed (e.g. ½ O₂).
  - States are written as subscripts.
  - "Correct ∆H with sign is recorded at the end of the equation", e.g.
    H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l), ∆H = −286 kJ mol⁻¹.
  - If the equation is doubled, the value doubles and is written in kJ: "∆H = –572 kJ (Note that
    it is an error to have 'per mol' here.)"
- Units are written with negative indices: **kJ mol⁻¹**, **mol L⁻¹**, **g L⁻¹**. The 2025 exam
  gave "Enthalpy of combustion of methane = −890 kJ mol⁻¹".
- The outline writes the Le Châtelier condition as "the ΔH value for the forward or back
  reaction" (the Δ is a symbol glyph in the source).

**Data sheet (in use since November 2019):**

- **Metal activity** series, most to least reactive: K, Ca, Na, Mg, Al, Zn, Cd, Co, Ni, Bi, Cu,
  Hg, Ag, Au. It has no Fe, Sn or Pb.
- **SI prefixes**: tera to pico, including deci and centi.
- **Symbols of common quantities**:
  - n: amount of substance
  - m: mass
  - c: molar concentration
  - ΔH: change in enthalpy
  - M: molar mass
  - V: volume
  - Q: heat energy
  - c: specific heat capacity
  - T: temperature
- **Mathematical relationships**: n = m/M, c = n/V, **Q = mcΔT**, **ΔH = Q/n**, pH = −log[H⁺].
  - The ΔH relationship is printed with no minus sign. How the sign is handled (stated from
    context) was not checked in a marking guide **(unverified)**.
- **Periodic table**: relative atomic masses to 4 significant figures (e.g. C 12.01, O 16.00,
  H 1.008), with Australian spellings (*aluminium*, *sulfur*, *caesium*).
- **Not on the sheet:** bond enthalpies, ΔfH° values, specific heat of water, IR data, reduction
  potentials, and the gas constant. These are given in the question when needed (e.g. the molar
  mass and enthalpy of combustion in the 2025 exam).

**Named reagents and tests:**

- acidified dichromate (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ / acidified potassium dichromate solution)
- Tollens reagent (ammoniacal silver nitrate), the silver mirror
- bromine or iodine solution for unsaturation
- concentrated sulfuric acid (trace) with heating under reflux for esterification
- acidified permanganate appears only in Stage 1 redox contexts (MnO₄⁻, Cr₂O₇²⁻, H₂O₂, Fe²⁺)

**Kinetics wording (2025 advice):**

- Particles do **not** "overcome activation energy". The accepted idea is that more collisions
  have energy equal to or greater than Ea.
- A higher rate does **not** increase yield.
- On a catalysed concentration–time curve, the curve must level off at the same final value.
- Do not use arrows for increase/decrease, or undefined abbreviations (e.g. "ROR").

**Other notation:**

- Australian spelling throughout: *sulfur*, *sulfuric*, *aluminium*, *caesium*,
  *vaporisation* (assessment advice), *polymerisation*, *ionise*, *hydrogencarbonate* (one word
  in the outline).
- Le Châtelier is written with the circumflex.
- **Significant figures** are marked: "the inaccurate use of significant figures, scientific
  notation, and rounding will result in the loss of marks". Units must be written even when the
  question does not specify them.
- "Write an equation for …" expects a "Balanced, formulae equation". "As soon as one formula is
  incorrect, chemical equations are awarded no marks."

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

No SACE-wide glossary of command terms for Chemistry was found on the SACE site. The Chemistry
resources page lists none, and a site search found only VET glossaries **(unverified that none
exists)**.

The working vocabulary is:

1. the verbs that start each Science Understanding dot point
2. the performance-standard descriptors for the two assessment design criteria

The meanings below are paraphrased from how the outline and the 2025 assessment advice use them.

| Verb (as used) | What it asks for (paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| Identify | Recognise and name a feature, type or species (e.g. primary/secondary/tertiary; addition vs condensation polymer) |
| Name systematically | Give the IUPAC name within the stated limits |
| Draw (structural formula) | A complete, correct structure; every H shown on drawn carbons unless skeletal |
| Write (equation) | A balanced formula equation with correct formulae (states when thermochemical) |
| Describe | Give an account of a procedure, change or effect (e.g. how dichromate distinguishes alcohols) |
| Explain | Give reasons with the relevant concept; the advice expects the causal link (e.g. heating raises rate, reflux prevents loss of volatiles) |
| Predict (and explain) | State the expected outcome from principles, then justify it (collision theory, Le Châtelier) |
| Compare | Point out similarities and differences against given data |
| Determine / Calculate / Undertake calculations | Show working with units and significant figures; marks are given for each correct step |
| Interpret | Extract meaning from graphs, flow charts or energy profiles |
| Discuss / Evaluate | Weigh advantages and disadvantages or the validity of claims or procedures |
| Deconstruct (a problem) / Design (an investigation) | The IAE1 inquiry skills: break down an open problem and justify a method |

Performance-standard qualifiers run from A to E (e.g. "Critically deconstructs … with detailed
justification" at A, down to "Attempts a simple deconstruction" at E). "Critically",
"systematically", "logically", "highly effectively" and "coherently" mark the top bands.
