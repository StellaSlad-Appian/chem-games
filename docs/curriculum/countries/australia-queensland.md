**Purpose:** the reference curriculum for the organic chemistry and energetics games planned in
[`../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md`](../../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md), for Queensland. The other
Australian states and territories are compared in [`australia-overview.md`](../australia-overview.md).
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent, from primary sources).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches in Queensland.

Primary sources: the QCAA *Chemistry 2025 v1.3 General senior syllabus* (Jan 2026), the QCAA *Chemistry 2025
formula and data book*, the QCAA *Chemistry 2019 v1.3* syllabus (predecessor), the QCAA glossary of cognitive
verbs, QCAA memo 043/23 (Australian Curriculum v9.0 schedule), the QCAA Years 7–10 Science v9.0 sequence, the

## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **Organic chemistry is a Year 12 topic in Queensland.** It sits almost entirely in **Unit 4** ("Structure,
  synthesis and design", Year 12, second half), which the external exam covers. Year 11 only touches
  hydrocarbons: alkanes/alkenes/benzene properties and the bromine-water test. Tag organic games "Year 12 /
  Unit 4". Year 10 (Australian Curriculum v9) has **no organic chemistry at all**.
- **Energetics and kinetics are Year 11, and not in the external exam.** Enthalpy, bond enthalpies and
  calorimetry are in **Unit 1**; rates, collision theory and Maxwell–Boltzmann are in **Unit 2**. Units 1–2 are
  school-assessed only (reported as S/U). They come back in Year 12 only through equilibrium: forward/reverse
  activation energies, the ΔH sign deciding the temperature effect, and collision theory for the forward and
  reverse reactions. Kinetics and thermo games are therefore Year 11 revision tools, with an equilibrium
  bridge in Year 12.
- **Thermodynamics stays at the enthalpy level.** There is no entropy, no Gibbs energy and no formation
  enthalpy (ΔfH°) table. **Hess's law appears only as a suggested investigation**, not as a science
  understanding dot point. Bond enthalpy calculations *are* in scope, and the data book has a full table.
  Maxwell–Boltzmann *is* in scope. Rate laws and orders are excluded in so many words ("Order of reaction is
  not required").
- **The organic content is broad for Australia.** It covers:
  - naming up to C10 with methyl/ethyl branches;
  - cis/trans **and optical isomers / chiral carbons**;
  - Markovnikov's rule, haloalkanes and elimination;
  - reaction pathways with reagents and conditions;
  - IR and mass spectra;
  - polymers, including tacticity, PLA, nylon and polyester;
  - amino acids, tripeptides, electrophoresis and sugars.

  A reaction-pathway game maps directly onto the **"Reaction pathways" diagram printed on page 1 of the QCAA
  formula and data book**.
- **Naming boundaries are precise.** Students must *name* alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, alcohols, aldehydes,
  ketones, carboxylic acids, haloalkanes and esters. For **amines and amides they only draw formulas** (these
  two are missing from the IUPAC naming list). Use modern locant style (propan-2-ol, pentan-3-one), as in the
  2024 exam. Use **cis/trans**, not E/Z.
- **Named tests to model:** bromine water (alkane vs alkene), and **acidified potassium dichromate(VI)** and
  **potassium manganate(VII)** (primary/secondary/tertiary alcohols). Tollens and Fehling are not in the
  syllabus.
- **Assessment shape suits short, data-rich games.** IA1 is a 60-minute data test (Unit 3). The external exam is
  two 90-minute papers of multiple choice plus short response, all with the QCAA data book. Games that give a
  data set and ask the player to "determine", "deduce" or "identify" mirror QCAA's cognitive verbs.

## 0. What applies in 2026

- **Syllabus in force: *Chemistry 2025 v1.3 General senior syllabus*** (QCAA, January 2026). Version history
  printed in the syllabus:

  | Version | Date | Note |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1.0 | January 2024 | Released for familiarisation and planning (with implementation starting in 2025) |
  | 1.1 | July 2024 | Released for implementation with minor updates |
  | 1.2 | October 2024 | ISBN removed and minor updates |
  | 1.3 | January 2026 | File metadata changes to support the new Syllabuses application |

  v1.3 changes metadata only, so the content is the same as v1.1/1.2.
- **Timeline (confirmed):** the 2025 General syllabuses were implemented with students starting **Units 1–2 in
  2025** and finishing **Units 3–4 in 2026**. The QCAA handbook text quoted in search results reads "students
  commence their study of the subject with Unit 1 and Unit 2 in 2025 and complete their study of the subject
  with Units 3 and 4 in 2026". QCAA memo 043/23 also says General subjects are revised "from 2025" for
  Year 11. So in the **2026 school year**:
  - Year 11 is on 2025-syllabus Units 1–2 (second cohort).
  - Year 12 is on 2025-syllabus Units 3–4 (**first cohort**).
  - The **first external exam under the 2025 syllabus is in 2026**. QCAA timetable (May 2026): Chemistry Paper 1
    and Paper 2 are both on **Friday 30 October 2026**, one each side of the day. The whole external block runs
    from 26 October to 17 November 2026 (Chemistry dates read from a school-hosted copy of the QCAA timetable
    PDF).
- **Predecessor: *Chemistry 2019 v1.3 General senior syllabus*.** It was first implemented with Year 11 in 2019
  and had its first external exam in 2020. The **last cohort finished in 2025**: the QCAA "Chemistry subject
  report, 2025 cohort" (January 2026) is the last one under that syllabus. Later minor versions of the 2019
  syllabus may exist; only v1.3 was read **(unverified)**.
- **Differences between the 2019 and 2025 syllabuses that matter for games** (from reading both):
  - Added in 2025:
    - optical isomers and chiral carbon atoms
    - Maxwell–Boltzmann *sketching* (already present in 2019)
    - polymer tacticity (syndiotactic/isotactic/atactic polypropene), PLA
    - electrophoresis and isoelectric points
    - reduction of alkynes
  - Removed in 2025: nitriles (2019 had KCN substitution and nitrile reduction), and protein
    secondary/tertiary structure as science understanding (now SHE context only). Amine/amide *naming*
    dropped out of the IUPAC list.
  - 2019 had "structural formulas (condensed and extended)". 2025 says "empirical, molecular and structural
    formulas".
  - Perusal time fell from 10 to 5 minutes per paper.
- **No replacement announced.** Nothing found about a post-2025 Chemistry syllabus. QCAA syllabuses run on a
  multi-year cycle, so 2026–2028 cohorts should all be on the 2025 syllabus **(unverified beyond "no
  announcement found")**.

**Years 7–10 in Queensland:** the Australian Curriculum (AC) v9.0 applies (QCAA memo 043/23, 11 July 2023).
- For Years 7–10, schools choose their own transition approach during 2023–2026, and "**All learning areas
  must be fully implemented**" by **2027**. The memo says: "all Queensland schools will be teaching all
  Australian Curriculum v9.0 learning areas by the end of 2027".
- For Prep–Year 6, Science was familiarisation in 2025 and implementation in 2026.
- So in 2026, a Queensland Year 9/10 class may be on AC v8.4 or v9.0 depending on the school. From 2027 it is
  v9.0 everywhere.

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 Course and units (syllabus "Course structure")

- Chemistry is a **General** senior subject (the ATAR-eligible type) with **four units, each "a notional time of
  55 hours of teaching and learning, including assessment"**.
- Students "should complete Unit 1 and Unit 2 before beginning Units 3 and 4". **Units 3 and 4 are studied as
  a pair.** In practice, Units 1–2 are Year 11 and Units 3–4 are Year 12.

| Unit | Title | Topics (notional hours) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chemical fundamentals — structure, properties and reactions | T1 Properties and structure of atoms (20 h); T2 Properties and structure of materials (5 h); T3 Chemical reactions — reactants, products and energy change (20 h) |
| 2 | Molecular interactions and reactions | T1 Intermolecular forces and gases (13 h); T2 Aqueous solutions and acidity (22 h); T3 Rates of chemical reactions (10 h) |
| 3 | Equilibrium, acids and redox reactions | T1 Chemical equilibrium systems (25 h); T2 Oxidation and reduction (20 h) |
| 4 | Structure, synthesis and design | T1 Properties and structure of organic materials (30 h); T2 Chemical synthesis and design (15 h) |

- Each topic has three kinds of subject matter:
  - **Science understanding:** examinable. For Units 3–4, "can be assessed in the external assessment".
  - **Science as a human endeavour (SHE):** in Units 3–4, "may be assessed in the internal assessments" only.
  - **Science inquiry:** suggested practicals and investigations.

### 1.2 Assessment

- **Units 1 and 2:** school-devised, with 2–4 assessments across the two units. Results go to QCAA as
  **Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory** only. **Year 11 content is never externally examined.**
- **Units 3 and 4:** three internal assessments (IAs) plus one external assessment (EA). QCAA confirms the
  internal marks and combines them with the external result into a mark out of 100 and an A–E grade.

| Instrument | Unit | Weight | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| IA1 Data test | 3 | 10% | Supervised exam. Perusal 5 min, working 60 min. 2–4 unseen datasets from Unit 3 practicals. Mark split ~30% "unknown scientific quantities" (objective 2), ~30% "trends, patterns, relationships, limitations or uncertainty" (obj. 3), ~40% "conclusions" (obj. 4). QCAA-approved calculator and **QCAA Chemistry formula and data book** allowed. |
| IA2 Student experiment | 3 | 20% | Modify a Unit 3 practical or simulation. Written report up to 2000 words or multimodal up to 11 min. About 10 h class time. |
| IA3 Research investigation | 4 | 20% | Evaluate a claim using secondary sources. Up to 2000 words or 11 min multimodal. About 10 h. |
| EA Examination (combination response) | 3 + 4 | **50%** | **Two papers, each 5 min perusal + 90 min working.** Multiple choice, single words, sentences/paragraphs, calculations, unseen stimulus. Science-understanding subject matter from Units 3 and 4 only. QCAA provides the formula and data book. |

- For reference, the **2024** external exam (2019 syllabus, same two-paper shape) had:
  - Paper 1: Section 1 with 20 multiple-choice questions (20 marks) and Section 2 with nine short-response
    questions (38 marks).
  - Paper 2: 52 marks of short response.

  The 2026 mark split under the 2025 syllabus was not confirmed **(unverified)**.

### 1.3 Certificate and ATAR

- The certificate is the **Queensland Certificate of Education (QCE)**. The ATAR is calculated by **QTAC** from
  General subject results, with inter-subject scaling. Credit rules (e.g. how many QCE credits a General
  subject earns) were not read in the QCE and QCIA policy and procedures handbook **(unverified)**.
- There are no prerequisites in the syllabus beyond the Units 1–2 → 3–4 order. Many schools recommend Year 10
  science and mathematics results for entry **(unverified; school-level)**.

### 1.4 QCAA Chemistry formula and data book (2025 edition, 18 pages)

What is printed, in page order, with the parts relevant to games in bold:

1. **Physical constants:**
   - NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; F = 96 485 C mol⁻¹; R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
   - Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 298 K
   - **molar volume at STP 22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹, with STP = 273 K and 100 kPa**
   - **specific heat capacity of water cw = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹**
   - 1 dm³ = 1 L
2. **"Reaction pathways" diagram (page 1).** A map linking: alkane, alkene, haloalkane, dihaloalkane,
   poly(alkene), alcohol (primary/secondary), aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid, ester, amine, amide.
   **This is effectively the board for a pathway game.** It shows the links only, not the reagents; the
   syllabus asks students to supply reagents and conditions.
3. **Formulas by topic:**
   - uncertainty and percentage error
   - **Q = mcΔT**; **ΔH = ΣH(products) − ΣH(reactants)**; **ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed)**
   - n = m/M; percentage yield; PV = nRT; c = n/V
   - **rate = Δ[P]/Δt or −Δ[R]/Δt**
   - Q and Kc expressions; Ksp, Kw, pH, pOH, Ka, Kb, Kw = Ka × Kb
   - E°cell = E°(reduction) − E°(oxidation); q = n(e⁻)F; q = It
   - RF
4. **List of elements; periodic table** (IUPAC groups 1–18, relative atomic masses to 2 decimal places).
5. Atomic and ionic radii; electronegativities and first ionisation energies.
6. Solubility table (s / p / i) for 16 cations × 9 anions.
7. **Average bond enthalpies at 298 K** (kJ mol⁻¹):
   - single bonds among H, C, N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I (e.g. C–H 414, C–C 346, O–H 463, C–O 358)
   - multiple bonds C=C 614, C≡C 839, C=O 804, O=O 498, N≡N 945, C=N 615, C≡N 890, N=N 470
8. Reactivity series of metals (C and H₂ included for comparison).
9. Standard electrode potentials at 298 K. Includes **Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ and MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺**, the oxidants used in the
   alcohol tests.
10. **Common amino acids** (20, with structures and isoelectric pH).
11. **Glucose and fructose**, straight-chain and α-ring forms.
12. **Acid–base indicators** (8, with pKa, pH range and colours).
13. **Infrared data**: characteristic absorption ranges for C–X, C–O, C=C, C=O, C≡C, O–H (alcohol and acid,
    hydrogen-bonded), C–H and N–H.
14. Polyatomic ion formulas, including "acetate (ethanoate)" and "hydronium" H₃O⁺.

**Not in the data book:** no ΔfH° or ΔcH° tables, no heats of combustion of fuels, no entropy data, no NMR data.

## 2. Content by year / unit

Dot points are paraphrased unless in quotation marks. Quoted text is verbatim from the 2025 v1.3 syllabus.
The PDF text layer drops the Δ sign, so formulas are restored by hand.

### Year 11, Unit 1: Chemical fundamentals: structure, properties and reactions

**Topic 1: Properties and structure of atoms (20 h)**
- atomic structure, isotopes, and electron configurations to Z = 36 (Cr and Cu as exceptions)
- successive ionisation energies
- mass spectrometry of elements
- flame tests and AAS, emission spectrum of hydrogen
- periodic trends
- Lewis structures
- IUPAC names of ionic and molecular compounds; empirical vs molecular formula

**Topic 2: Properties and structure of materials (5 h)**
- ionic, metallic and covalent bonding and properties; allotropes of carbon
- **First organic seed:** "Explain that hydrocarbons, including alkanes (saturated), alkenes (unsaturated) and
  benzene, have different chemical properties that are determined by the nature of the bonding within the
  molecules."
- Suggested practical: "tests to distinguish alkanes and alkenes".

**Topic 3: Chemical reactions: reactants, products and energy change (20 h). The main thermochemistry block:**
- state symbols; balancing many reaction types
- heat vs temperature ("temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles")
- endo/exothermic reactions related to "the law of conservation of energy and the breaking and reforming of
  bonds"
- "Sketch enthalpy level diagrams for exothermic and endothermic reactions."
- Analyse enthalpy level diagrams and thermochemical equations for relative stability and the **sign of ΔH**.
- "Explain, in terms of average bond enthalpies, why reactions are exothermic or endothermic."
- Know the **limitations of average bond enthalpies**.
- **Q = mcΔT**. ΔH for a reaction from temperature change, quantities and mass of water.
- Analyse data for **heat of combustion, heat of neutralisation** and reactions in aqueous solution.
- Mole concept, limiting reactant, percentage and theoretical yield.
- SHE:
  - industrial energy saving
  - respiration as an exothermic reaction
  - "Evaluate fuels, including fossil fuels and biofuels, in terms of their energy output, their suitability
    for purpose, and the nature of products of combustion."
- Suggested investigation: "the enthalpy change of a reaction, e.g. calorimetry or Hess's Law". **This is the
  only place Hess's law appears.**

### Year 11, Unit 2: Molecular interactions and reactions

**Topic 1: Intermolecular forces and gases (13 h)**
- VSEPR (linear, bent, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, pyramidal), polarity
- "Explain the relationship between vapour pressure, melting point, boiling point and solubility, and the
  nature and strength of intermolecular forces (e.g. dispersion forces, dipole-dipole attractions, and hydrogen
  bonding)"
- paper/TLC chromatography and RF values
- kinetic theory, PV = nRT, molar volume at STP

**Topic 2: Aqueous solutions and acidity (22 h)**
- water's properties, molarity, precipitation and solubility rules, solubility curves
- pH scale, Arrhenius model, reactions of acids

**Topic 3: Rates of chemical reactions (10 h). The kinetics block:**
- Factors: temperature, surface area, pressure (gases), concentration, catalyst.
- Apply **collision theory** to the effects of concentration, temperature, pressure and surface area.
- "**Sketch Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curves for reactions with and without catalysts.**"
- "Describe activation energy (Ea)."
- Relate bond strength and number to the size of Ea and to rate.
- **Sketch and analyse energy profile diagrams** with and without catalysts, "to determine the enthalpy change
  and activation energy".
- How catalysts affect rate.
- **Rate = increase in [P] / time, or decrease in [R] / time.**
- Analyse graphs of concentration, volume or mass against time. "(Order of reaction is not required.)"
- SHE: enzymes and Ea, history of collision theory, corrosion. Suggested investigation: factors affecting rate.

### Year 12, Unit 3: Equilibrium, acids and redox reactions (externally examined)

**Topic 1: Chemical equilibrium systems (25 h)**
- "Discriminate between open or closed chemical systems." (Isolated systems are not mentioned.)
- Reversibility; equilibrium arrow; dynamic equilibrium in a closed system; position of equilibrium.
- "Explain the reversibility of chemical reactions by considering the activation energies of the forward and
  reverse reactions." (**The kinetics–equilibrium bridge.**)
- Concentration–time graphs to find the position of equilibrium.
- Temperature effect "by considering the enthalpy change for the forward and reverse reactions".
- Collision theory applied to the forward and reverse reactions.
- **Le Châtelier** for temperature, concentration, pressure "and the addition of a catalyst", both on the
  **position** of equilibrium and on **the value of the equilibrium constant**.
- Kc and Ksp:
  - expressions for homogeneous and heterogeneous systems
  - extent of reaction from the size of Kc
  - calculate Q
  - **calculate Kc and equilibrium concentrations**, with the small-change assumption stated
  - Ksp calculations
  - predict shifts by comparing Q and Kc
- Acids and bases:
  - Brønsted–Lowry, conjugate pairs, amphiprotic species
  - strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄); "carboxylic and carbonic acids as weak acids and ammonia and amines as
    weak bases"
  - Kw and pH/pOH for strong acids and bases
  - Ka, Kb, pKa, pKb calculations
  - buffers qualitatively ("Buffer calculations are not required.")
  - indicators, where colour change happens at pH = pKa
  - conductometric and acid–base titration curves
- SHE: wine chemistry; oceans and CO₂; Eunice Newton Foote; superacids.

**Topic 2: Oxidation and reduction (20 h)**
- Oxidation states (sign before number, e.g. +2); Roman numerals in transition-metal names.
- Half-equations in acidic conditions only.
- Galvanic and electrolytic cells, standard hydrogen electrode, E°cell, spontaneity.
- Electrolysis (molten and aqueous), Faraday calculations.
- SHE: **breath-alcohol tests** (a context for alcohol oxidation), fuel cells.

### Year 12, Unit 4: Structure, synthesis and design (externally examined). **The organic unit**

**Topic 1: Properties and structure of organic materials (30 h)**

*Structure of organic compounds*
- Identify alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, haloalkanes, esters,
  amines and amides.
- "Discriminate between class and functional groups, e.g. for OH, hydroxyl is the functional group and alcohol
  is the class."
- "Describe the features of a homologous series." Saturated vs unsaturated.
- "Discriminate between empirical, molecular and structural formulas."
- Determine molecular and structural formulas **up to C10, "including simple methyl and ethyl branched
  chains"**. This covers:
  - alkanes/alkenes/alkynes
  - alcohols (primary, secondary, tertiary)
  - aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids
  - **amines and amides**
  - haloalkanes (primary, secondary, tertiary)
  - esters
- **"Apply IUPAC rules in the nomenclature of organic compounds, up to C10, including simple methyl and ethyl
  branched chains"**. The list is the same as above **minus amines and amides**.
- "Identify structural and stereoisomers, including geometrical (cis and trans) and optical isomers."
- Deduce isomers of non-cyclic alkanes up to C6, cis/trans isomers of non-cyclic alkenes, and optical isomers.
  Sketch and name isomers of non-cyclic alkanes and straight-chain alkenes up to C6.
- "Identify chiral carbon atoms."
- Empirical and molecular formula and percentage composition of organic compounds from data.

*Physical properties and trends*
- Trends in melting point, boiling point, volatility and solubility (water and organic solvents) within and
  between homologous series (alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids), explained by dispersion forces,
  dipole–dipole interactions and hydrogen bonds.
- Analyse such data.

*Organic reactions and reaction pathways*
- Reaction equations for:
  - alkanes + X₂
  - haloalkanes + X₂, NaOH, NH₃
  - alkenes + H₂O, X₂, H₂, HX
  - alcohols + HX
  - carboxylic acid + alcohol → ester; carboxylic acid + amine → amide
- Also:
  - oxidation of alcohols
  - combustion of alkanes and alcohols
  - addition polymerisation to poly(alkenes)
  - reduction of alkynes and alkenes to alkanes
  - **elimination** of haloalkanes to alkenes
- "Identify reactions as addition, elimination, substitution or redox (oxidation-reduction). (Reaction
  mechanism for substitution and elimination reactions are not required.)"
- Classify carbon atoms in haloalkanes and alcohols as primary, secondary or tertiary.
- Acid–base properties of carboxylic acids and amines. "Explain that esterification is a reversible reaction."
- **Tests:** discriminate "alkanes and alkenes using bromine water" and "primary, secondary and tertiary
  alcohols using acidified potassium dichromate (VI) and potassium manganate (VII)". Interpret these tests.
- "Apply Markovnikov's rule to determine the products for addition reactions of alkenes with hydrogen halides
  (HX) and water."
- "**Determine reaction pathways, including reagents, condition and chemical equations, given the starting
  materials and the product/s formed.**"

*Organic materials: structure and function*
- Amino acids, tripeptides, monosaccharides, disaccharides.
- LDPE/HDPE, polypropene (syndiotactic, isotactic, atactic), PTFE, PLA, nylon, polyester.
- How structure relates to strength, density and biodegradability. Zwitterions of 2-amino acids.

*Analytical techniques*
- Paper/TLC chromatography of amino acids (RF); **electrophoresis** (buffer pH, isoelectric point).
- "Analyse data from spectra, including mass spectroscopy and infrared to determine the identity and structure
  of organic molecules." **No NMR.**

*SHE (internal assessment only)*
- computer modelling of proteins; enzymes; triglycerides as esters
- green polymers; **atom economy** ("the higher the atom economy, the 'greener' the process")
- organochlorine insecticides (DDT)

**Topic 2: Chemical synthesis and design (15 h)**
- Reagents and conditions chosen to optimise yield and rate: **Haber process** and **contact process**.
- Equations for:
  - Haber and contact processes
  - **ethanol by fermentation vs hydration of ethene**
  - the hydrogen fuel cell (acidic and alkaline)
- Yield and limiting reagent calculations; data on how conditions affect yield and rate.
- Addition and condensation polymers from monomers (polysaccharides, PLA, polyamides incl. proteins and nylon,
  polyester).
- Construct and name tripeptides from amino acid symbols; glycosidic bonds.
- SHE:
  - green chemistry and **atom economy calculations**
  - biofuels from algae, oil seeds and waste
  - industrial enzymes, including "lipase-catalysed transesterification to produce biodiesel"
  - molecular manufacturing

## 4. Conventions and notation

**Verified from QCAA primary sources:**
- **Locants before the suffix** (current IUPAC): the 2024 external exam uses *propan-2-ol*, *pentan-1-ol*,
  *pentan-2-one*, *pentan-3-one*, *2-methylpropane*, *ethanoic acid*, *butanoic acid*.
  - The same paper also uses *butanone* and *butanol* without locants, plus trivial names in context
    (*methylamine*, *phenol*, *glycerol*, *bioethanol*).
  - The 2019 syllabus guidance still wrote "1-bromopropane" and "1-butylamine", so older resources use the
    older style.
- **cis/trans**, not E/Z: "geometrical (cis and trans) isomers". Also "optical isomers", "chiral carbon atoms".
- **Reagent names as printed:** "acidified potassium dichromate (VI)", "potassium manganate (VII)", "bromine
  water".
  - The data book and syllabus use Stock notation with Roman numerals (the syllabus says oxidation numbers
    "represented as roman numerals" in transition-metal names).
  - Oxidation *states* are written sign-before-number: "+2".
- **Class vs functional group** is a named distinction: *hydroxyl* is the group, *alcohol* is the class.
- **"Haloalkanes"** (not halogenoalkanes), **"2-amino acids"**, **"poly(alkenes)"**, **"polyethene"**,
  **"polypropene"**, **"polytetrafluorethene"** (spelled without the second "o" in the syllabus), "polylactic
  acid (PLA)".
- **Units and symbols:**
  - kJ mol⁻¹ for ΔH (2019 syllabus guidance: "The enthalpy change (ΔH) for chemical reactions is indicated in
    kJ mol⁻¹").
  - The data book uses dm³ and mol² dm⁻⁶ in constants. The syllabus uses mol L⁻¹, g L⁻¹ and ppm, and square
    brackets for concentration.
  - STP = 273 K and 100 kPa; 22.7 dm³ mol⁻¹.
  - cw = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹.
  - The 2019 guidance said to assume dilute aqueous solutions have the same specific heat capacity as water.
- **Formula as QCAA writes it:** ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed). Endothermic is positive.
- **"hydronium"** for H₃O⁺ (data book ion list); "acetate (ethanoate)".
- **Australian spelling:** sulfur, sulfate, aluminium, caesium ("Cesium" in the data book element list),
  ionisation, vapour, colour.
- **Significant figures and uncertainty rules** are spelled out in the syllabus: decimal places for
  addition/subtraction, significant figures for multiplication/division, absolute uncertainty of the mean, and
  percentage error.

**Game-relevant data-book facts:** the bond enthalpy table, reaction-pathway map, IR table, amino-acid table,
indicator table and E° table (with dichromate and permanganate) can be copied into games as "what the
student has in the exam". Do not supply ΔfH° or fuel energy values as if QCAA provided them.

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

- QCAA uses one **Glossary of cognitive verbs** for all General syllabuses; the 2025 syllabus links to it. The
  copy read is dated January 2018; a newer revision may exist **(unverified)**.
- The syllabus objectives are built from six verbs: *describe, apply, analyse, interpret, evaluate,
  investigate*.
- The Chemistry dot points use mainly *identify, discriminate, determine, explain, apply, analyse, sketch,
  calculate, deduce, describe, state, compare*.

Paraphrased definitions:

| Verb | Meaning (paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| analyse | break down to find the essential elements, features or structure and their relationships |
| apply | use knowledge or a procedure in a given situation |
| calculate | find a numerical answer by mathematical processes, showing the working |
| classify / categorise | arrange into classes by shared characteristics |
| compare | show similarities and differences and why they matter |
| contrast | show how things differ, referring to all items |
| deduce | reach a conclusion that must be true given the assumptions and information |
| describe | give an account of the features or characteristics of something |
| determine | establish or ascertain after consideration, observation or calculation |
| discriminate | note or recognise a difference; distinguish as different |
| distinguish | recognise as different; make the difference between items clear |
| evaluate | weigh strengths, implications and limitations and judge against criteria |
| explain | make an idea clear by describing it in more detail or giving the relevant facts |
| extrapolate | infer beyond the known data by assuming trends continue |
| identify | recognise and name; pick out from possibilities |
| infer | conclude from evidence and reasoning rather than from explicit statements |
| interpret | use knowledge to recognise trends and draw conclusions from given information |
| justify | give reasons or evidence that show an answer is right or reasonable |
| predict | give an expected result based on the available information |
| recall / recognise | remember facts; identify features from knowledge |
| sketch | draw simply, showing the essential features without detail |
| symbolise | represent by symbols (as in "Symbolise equilibrium equations") |

- The IA1 specification maps verbs to objectives:
  - objective 2: *calculate, determine, identify, use*
  - objective 3: *categorise, classify, compare, contrast, identify, organise, sequence*
  - objective 4: *deduce, determine, draw (a conclusion), extrapolate, infer, interpolate, justify, predict*
- These make good labels for game task types.
