## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **Levels 9–10 contain no organic chemistry at all.** Organic chemistry first appears in
  **VCE Unit 1 AoS 2** (families, IUPAC names to C8, structural isomers to C5, addition polymers)
  and returns in full in **Unit 4 AoS 1–2** (more families, skeletal formulas, reactions,
  pathways, spectroscopy). Every organic game is `Senior`; a Year 10 organic game would be
  enrichment only.
- **VCE energetics is enthalpy-and-fuels, not thermodynamics.** Unit 3 AoS 1 covers exo/endo
  with ΔH, energy profile diagrams, combustion (complete and incomplete), stoichiometry at SLC,
  calorimetry with a calibration factor, fuel and food energy, and efficiency. **Hess's law,
  ΔfH°, entropy and Gibbs energy are not in the study design.** A Hess/Gibbs game has no
  Victorian home.
- **The 2026 Data Book changed things games depend on.** Version 2 (Feb 2026) **removed the
  average bond enthalpy tables** that the 2025 book and the 2024 sample exam used, and
  **removed the `-amide` row** from the nomenclature table. Games should take their numbers
  (molar enthalpies of combustion, heats of combustion, IR/NMR tables, constants) from the 2026
  book and should not ask for bond-enthalpy ΔH calculations as VCE practice.
- **Equilibrium is quantitative and graph-heavy; kinetics is qualitative.** Unit 3 AoS 2 asks
  for Kc with units, the reaction quotient Q, Le Chatelier and **concentration–time graphs**;
  kinetics is collision theory, rate factors and catalysts on energy profile diagrams, with no
  rate law. Maxwell–Boltzmann is not named in the study design but was examined (2024 NHT).
- **The exam rewards pathway flow charts, percentage yield and atom economy.** The June 2024
  sample exam has a pathway flow chart with blanks; the 2024 exam compares yield and atom
  economy of two routes. The Synthesis Router mechanic matches this, but its reaction table
  needs trimming (Section 8).
- **Answer checking must accept both naming styles.** VCAA accepts "propan-1-ol" and
  "1-propanol" as IUPAC-conformant, and its own sample exam writes "2-methyl-2-propanol" and
  "1-hexene". Unsymmetrical alkene additions give **both** isomers in VCAA questions (no
  Markovnikov major/minor). Geometric isomers are **cis/trans**, not E/Z.
- **Version stability:** the study design is in force for 2026 with no replacement announced.
  VCAA now lists it as "accreditation from 2023 / 2024" with no end date, so the old
  "2023–2027" label can no longer be relied on (Section 0).

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 Years 7–10 (Victorian Curriculum F–10 v2.0 Science)

- Three strands: **Science Understanding** (biological, chemical, Earth and space, physical
  sciences), **Science as a Human Endeavour** and **Science Inquiry**.
- Each band has content descriptions ("Students learn that: …"), optional elaborations and an
  achievement standard.
- No state-set hours and no external assessment in Years 7–10. The VCAA does not prescribe
  time allocations for Science **(unverified)**.

### 1.2 VCE Chemistry, Units 1–4

- **Units:**
  1. *How can the diversity of materials be explained?*
  2. *How do chemical reactions shape the natural world?*
  3. *How can design and innovation help to optimise chemical processes?*
  4. *How are carbon-based compounds designed for purpose?*
- **Hours:** "at least 50 hours of scheduled classroom instruction" per unit.
- **Entry:** no prerequisites for Units 1, 2 or 3; Units 3 and 4 must be taken as a sequence.
  Units 1–2 are normally taken in Year 11 and Units 3–4 in Year 12. This is common practice;
  the study design says only that Units 1–4 are at the standard of the final two years
  **(year mapping unverified)**.
- **Practical work:** at least 10 h of class practical per unit across AoS 1–2. Add 7 h for the
  Unit 1 and Unit 2 AoS 3 investigations and 10 h for the Unit 4 AoS 3 investigation. A
  **logbook** is compulsory in every unit.
- **Key science skills** (a table common to all VCE sciences) apply across all four units and
  are examinable. They include significant figures, graphing, and accuracy/precision/validity
  terms that are defined in the study design.
- **Sustainability thread:** the UN SDGs (nine named), **seven green chemistry principles**
  (atom economy, catalysis, design for degradation, design for energy efficiency, designing
  safer chemicals, prevention of wastes, renewable feedstocks) and linear vs circular economy.
  All of these are reprinted in the data book.

### 1.3 Assessment

- **Units 1–2:** school-based. Results are reported only as S/N (satisfactory / not
  satisfactory).
- **Study score contributions:**
  - Unit 3 School-assessed Coursework (SAC): **20%**. Outcomes 1 and 2, 40 marks each.
  - Unit 4 SAC: **30%**. Outcomes 1, 2 and 3, 40 marks each. Outcome 3 is the student-designed
    investigation presented as a **scientific poster** (maximum 600 words) plus logbook.
  - End-of-year exam: **50%**.
- **SAC task types** (each used only once across Units 3 and 4):
  - comparison of findings from at least two practicals
  - data analysis
  - problem-solving with calculations
  - analysis of an innovation, case study or media text
  - Each task takes about 50–70 min written, or 10 min oral.
- **Exam** (specifications "From 2024"):
  - 15 min reading and 2 h 30 min writing.
  - **Section A:** 30 multiple-choice questions, 30 marks.
  - **Section B:** short- and extended-answer questions, 90 marks. The number of questions
    varies from year to year.
  - Total **120 marks**, all questions compulsory.
  - A data book is provided. Approved materials: normal stationery and **one scientific
    calculator**.
  - All Unit 3–4 key knowledge and the key science skills are examinable. Questions may
    integrate several areas of study.
- **Certificate and ATAR:** results count towards the **VCE**. The 0–50 study score comes from
  the SACs and the exam. The ATAR is calculated from study scores by VTAC **(VTAC process
  unverified here)**.

### 1.4 The Data Book (2026, Version 2): what matters for games

The study design calls the Data Book "an integral part of the study design", to be used in
teaching as well as in the exam. The 2026 contents are listed below. Items marked (★) are
directly usable as game data.

1. Names of selected elements
2. Electrochemical series (E⊖ at SLC)
3. **Chemical relationships** (★): n = m/M, n = cV, n = V/Vm, pV = nRT, ΔH = H(products) − H(reactants), q = mcΔT, molar ΔH = q/n, Q = It, calibration factor for general and for reaction calorimetry, E⊖cell, **% atom economy**, **% yield**, **Kc expression**
4. **Physical constants** (★): N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, F = 96 500 C mol⁻¹, R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, **Vm = 24.8 L mol⁻¹ at SLC (25 °C, 100 kPa)**, c(water) = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹, ΔHvap(H₂O) = +44.0 kJ mol⁻¹ (25 °C) and +40.7 kJ mol⁻¹ (100 °C)
5. Unit conversions
6. Metric prefixes
7. Acid–base indicators
8. **Colours of selected oxidising agents and their conjugate reducing agents** (★): e.g. dichromate orange → Cr³⁺ green; permanganate intense purple → Mn²⁺ colourless (dilute); bromine reddish-brown → bromide colourless; starch–iodine blue-black
9. Formulas and charges for selected ions
10. Qualitative solubilities
11. **Energy content of biomolecules in food** (★): fats and oils 37, proteins 17, carbohydrates 16 kJ g⁻¹
12. **Molar enthalpies of combustion** (★), in kJ mol⁻¹. The book notes that "∆H values are equation‑dependent" and gives the ethane example at ½ and 1 scale:
    - H₂ −286; CH₄ −890; C₂H₆ −1560; C₃H₈ −2220; C₄H₁₀ −2877; C₈H₁₈ −5470
    - CH₃OH −726; C₂H₅OH −1367; C (graphite) −394; glucose −2803
13. **Heats of combustion of selected fossil fuels** (★), in kJ g⁻¹ and kJ mL⁻¹: diesel, kerosene, natural gas, petrol
14. **Heats of combustion of selected biofuels** (★): biodiesel ≈ 37, bioethanol 29.7, biogas 14–24 kJ g⁻¹
15. Periodic table (with electronegativities)
16. **Formats for representing organic molecules** (★), using butanoic acid: molecular, structural, semi-structural (condensed) `CH3CH2CH2COOH` or `CH3(CH2)2COOH`, skeletal
17. **Functional group and substituent nomenclature** (★), in priority order:
    - carboxylic acid `-oic acid` > ester `-oate` > aldehyde `-al` > ketone `-one` > alcohol (`hydroxy-` / `-ol`) > amine (`amino-` / `-amine`) > alkene `-ene`
    - halogen prefixes fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo-, and alkyl prefixes methyl- to octyl-
    - Prefixes for the higher groups are marked "beyond the scope of VCE Chemistry"
18. Formulas of selected fatty acids (trivial names: lauric, palmitic, oleic, linoleic …)
19. 2-amino acids (structures)
20. Selected biomolecules (glycerol, α-D-glucose, amylose, amylopectin, glycogen)
21. **IR absorption ranges** (★)
22. **¹³C-NMR shifts** (★)
23. **¹H-NMR shifts** (★)
24. Sustainability (SDGs, green chemistry principles, types of economy)
25. References

**Changes from the 2025 Data Book** (compared against the copy printed in the 2025 exam):
- **Removed:** "Average bond enthalpies at 25 °C – single bonds" and "– multiple bonds" (2025 items 10–11). The 2024 sample exam and the 2025 NHT report used them.
- **Removed:** the **amide / `-amide`** row of the nomenclature table.
- **Renamed:** "Heats of combustion of selected blended fuels" became "… selected fossil fuels"; "Colours of selected conjugate redox reagents" became "Colours of selected oxidising agents and their conjugate reducing agents".
- **Updated values:** the electrochemical series now uses E⊖ at SLC (e.g. F₂ +2.89 V, previously +2.87 V).
- **New:** a references section.
- The reason for dropping bond enthalpies is not stated. Whether bond-enthalpy calculations are
  still examinable (with values given in the question) is **(unverified)**.

## 2. Content by level / unit

### 2.1 Levels 7–8 (brief)

- **VC2S8U05:** particle and kinetic theories explain properties (melting point, boiling point,
  density, viscosity, diffusion …).
- **VC2S8U06:** pure substances vs mixtures; separation techniques, including distillation and
  chromatography.
- **VC2S8U07:** elements, compounds and mixtures; symbols and chemical formulas.
- **VC2S8U08:** physical vs chemical change. The indicators include "a temperature change". Gas
  tests for O₂, CO₂ and H₂.
- **VC2S8U09:** renewable vs non-renewable resources.

### 2.2 Levels 9–10 (Victorian Curriculum v2.0): the whole chemistry content

- **VC2S10U06:** changing models of the atom; natural radioactive decay.
- **VC2S10U07:** periodic table organisation and trends (periods, metallic character, atomic
  size, reactivity). Elaborations: Bohr model, electron shells, flame tests.
- **VC2S10U08** (verbatim): "chemical reactions are described by the Law of Conservation of
  Mass and involve the rearrangement of atoms; they can be modelled using a range of
  representations, including word and simple balanced chemical equations". Elaborations include
  closed and open systems, and **green chemistry principles** (minimising waste and energy use).
- **VC2S10U09** (verbatim): "chemical reactions include synthesis, decomposition and
  displacement reactions and can be classified as exothermic or endothermic; reaction rates are
  affected by factors including temperature, concentration, surface area of solid reactants, and
  catalysts". Elaborations:
  - hot and cold packs
  - metal + acid, neutralisation and precipitation (all as displacement)
  - rate factors
  - First Peoples' chemistry: fermentation to ethanol, pyrolysis to charcoal, calcination,
    detoxifying cycad seeds by raising temperature and surface area
- **VC2S10U10:** the carbon cycle, including "the combustion of fossil fuels".
- **VC2S10U11:** climate change.
- **VC2S10U15** (physics): conservation of energy and efficiency.
- **Achievement standard** (Levels 9–10, chemistry sentences):
  - "They demonstrate the Law of Conservation of Mass in chemical reactions, and write word and
    balanced chemical equations for these reactions."
  - "They classify energy changes in chemical reactions as exothermic or endothermic."
  - "They predict the products of reactions and the effect of changing reaction conditions."
- **Not present at Levels 9–10:**
  - hydrocarbons, organic families, naming, polymers
  - moles
  - collision theory and activation energy
  - ΔH, energy diagrams
  - reversibility and equilibrium
  - acids and bases as a dot point (they appear only in elaborations)
- **Difference from ACARA v9.0:** Victoria names "exothermic or endothermic" in the 9–10 content
  description. ACARA v9 has only "indicators of energy change" in Year 8. Victoria also bands
  Years 9 and 10 together where ACARA splits them (AC9S9U07 vs AC9S10U07).

### 2.3 VCE Unit 1: *How can the diversity of materials be explained?*

- **AoS 1** *How do the chemical structures of materials explain their properties and
  reactions?*:
  - periodic trends (electronegativity, first ionisation energy)
  - Lewis, structural and molecular formulas for 11 small molecules, **including methane,
    ethane and ethene**
  - VSEPR shapes (linear, bent, pyramidal, tetrahedral; "excluding bond angles")
  - polarity
  - **intramolecular bonds vs intermolecular forces (dispersion, dipole–dipole, hydrogen
    bonding)** and boiling and melting points
  - diamond and graphite
  - metals and reactivity series; ionic compounds, formulas and names including seven
    polyatomic ions; precipitation equations with states
  - **chromatography with Rf values**
- **AoS 2** *How are materials quantified and classified?*:
  - relative isotopic and atomic mass (mass spectrometry, no instrument detail)
  - **Avogadro's constant and moles from mass**, empirical and molecular formulas
  - **Families of organic compounds** (verbatim): "the grouping of hydrocarbon compounds into
    families (alkanes, haloalkanes, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids) based upon similarities
    in their physical and chemical properties, including general formulas and general uses based
    on their properties"
  - **Representations and naming** (verbatim): "representations of organic compounds
    (structural formulas, semi-structural formulas) and naming according to the International
    Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) systematic nomenclature (limited to non-cyclic
    compounds up to C8, and structural isomers up to C5)"
  - plant biomass as a feedstock
  - **Polymers:** addition vs condensation; addition polymers from alkene monomers;
    thermoplastic vs thermosetting; designing linear addition polymers (monomer choice, chain
    length, branching); HDPE, PVC, LDPE, PP and PS vs PLA, Bio-PE and Bio-PP; recycling
    (mechanical, chemical, organic); condensation polymers and hydrolysis in a circular economy
- **AoS 3:** a student research investigation on the sustainable production or use of a
  material.

### 2.4 VCE Unit 2: *How do chemical reactions shape the natural world?*

- **AoS 1** *How do chemicals interact with water?*:
  - hydrogen bonding and water's anomalous properties
  - **specific heat capacity of water** and latent heat of vaporisation
  - Brønsted–Lowry acids and bases, polyprotic and amphiprotic species
  - strong/weak vs concentrated/dilute
  - pH of strong acids and bases using Kw
  - redox half-equations (including in acidic conditions)
- **AoS 2** *How are chemicals measured and analysed?*:
  - concentration units (mol L⁻¹, g L⁻¹, %(m/v), %(v/v), ppm)
  - solubility curves
  - titrations (excluding back titrations)
  - greenhouse gases
  - **SLC = 25 °C, 100 kPa**, pV = nRT, gas stoichiometry
  - salinity; colorimetry and UV-visible spectroscopy with calibration curves
- **AoS 3:** a student-adapted or student-designed quantitative investigation.

### 2.5 VCE Unit 3: *How can design and innovation help to optimise chemical processes?* (energetics, kinetics, equilibrium)

**AoS 1 *What are the current and future options for supplying energy?***

*Carbon-based fuels*
- fossil fuels vs biofuels and renewability; food fuels in kJ g⁻¹
- photosynthesis, respiration, fermentation to bioethanol (equations given)
- The central dot point (verbatim): "comparison of exothermic and endothermic reactions, with
  reference to bond making and bond breaking, including enthalpy changes (∆H) measured in kJ,
  molar enthalpy changes measured in kJ mol-1 and enthalpy changes for mixtures measured in
  kJ g-1, and their representations in energy profile diagrams"
- limiting reactants
- **complete and incomplete combustion**, written as balanced **thermochemical equations** with
  states

*Measuring changes in chemical reactions*
- combustion stoichiometry (mass–mass, mass–volume, volume–volume) at SLC, including
  greenhouse-gas masses and volumes
- **specific heat capacity of water** to estimate the heat from fuel and food
- **solution calorimetry**: calibration factor, heat loss, and **temperature–time graphs**
- **energy transformation efficiency** (%)
- energy values of foods

*Galvanic and fuel cells*
- oxidation numbers; half-equations in acidic and basic conditions
- the electrochemical series; Faraday's laws
- fuel cells and green chemistry
- (not a focus of this framework)

**AoS 2 *How can the rate and yield of chemical reactions be optimised?***

*Rates* (verbatim):
- "factors affecting the frequency and success of reactant particle collisions and the rate of
  a chemical reaction in open and closed systems, including temperature, surface area,
  concentration, gas pressures, presence of a catalyst, activation energy and orientation"
- "the role of catalysts in increasing the rate of specific reactions, with reference to
  alternative reaction pathways of lower activation energies and represented using energy
  profile diagrams"

*Extent of reactions*
- reversible vs irreversible; **rate vs extent**
- dynamic **homogeneous** equilibria (aqueous or gas) shown by equations and
  **concentration–time graphs**
- changes in temperature, species or volume (concentration or pressure), also shown on
  concentration–time graphs
- **Le Chatelier's principle** for yield
- **Kc calculations "including units"**, and the dependence of Kc on temperature and on the
  equation as written
- **the reaction quotient Q**
- the rate-vs-yield **temperature compromise**, linked to the green chemistry principles of
  catalysis and energy efficiency

*Electrolysis*
- products predicted from the electrochemical series (molten or aqueous)
- commercial cells; secondary (rechargeable) cells
- green hydrogen (PEM electrolysis, artificial photosynthesis); Faraday's laws
- (not a focus of this framework)

**Suggested practicals in the study design:** rates by mass, gas volume and time; iron(III)
thiocyanate equilibrium; H₂O₂ decomposition with organic and inorganic catalysts; calorimetry of
methane, alcohols, waxes and foods.

**Not in the study design text:** Hess's law, standard enthalpies of formation, bond-enthalpy
calculations (only the qualitative "bond making and bond breaking"), entropy, Gibbs energy, the
Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, rate laws/orders/rate constants, Arrhenius, Kp, heterogeneous
equilibria, and Ksp. **But:** the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve and a bomb calorimeter both appear in
the 2024 NHT exam report (Q28, Q24), so examiners treat Maxwell–Boltzmann as covered by
"frequency and success of … collisions".

### 2.6 VCE Unit 4: *How are carbon-based compounds designed for purpose?* (organic)

**AoS 1 *How are organic compounds categorised and synthesised?***

*Structure, nomenclature and properties* (verbatim):
- "molecular, structural and semi-structural (condensed) formulas and skeletal structures of
  alkanes (including cyclohexane), alkenes, benzene, haloalkanes, primary amines, primary
  amides, alcohols (primary, secondary and tertiary), aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and
  non-branched esters"
- "the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) systematic naming of organic
  compounds up to C8, with no more than two functional groups for a molecule, limited to
  non-cyclic hydrocarbons, haloalkanes, primary amines, alcohols (primary, secondary and
  tertiary), aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and non-branched esters"
- So **amides, benzene and cyclohexane are drawn but not named.** Diversity of carbon
  compounds (valence, bond strength, unsaturation, structural isomers). Trends in boiling point,
  melting point and **viscosity** within and between homologous series.

*Reactions of organic compounds* (verbatim list): "organic reactions and pathways, including
equations, reactants, products, reaction conditions and catalysts (specific enzymes not
required)":
- "synthesis of primary haloalkanes and primary alcohols by substitution"
- "addition reactions of alkenes"
- "the esterification between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid"
- "hydrolysis of esters"
- "pathways for the synthesis of primary amines and carboxylic acids"
- "transesterification of plant triglycerides using alcohols to produce biodiesel"
- hydrolysis of proteins, carbohydrates, fats and oils; condensation to proteins, starch,
  glycogen and lipids

*Also in AoS 1*
- "**calculations of percentage yield and atom economy of single-step or overall reaction
  pathways**"
- green chemistry principles: renewable feedstocks, catalysis, designing safer chemicals

**Outcome 1** (verbatim): "analyse the general structures and reactions of the major organic
families of compounds, **design reaction pathways for organic synthesis**, and evaluate the
sustainability of the manufacture of organic compounds used in society".

**Oxidation of alcohols** is not a separate dot point. It enters through "pathways for the
synthesis of … carboxylic acids" and through the hydroxyl test (AoS 2). VCAA's sample Unit 4
plan asks students to "Classify a range of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols by testing
with acidified potassium dichromate solution". The June 2024 sample exam (Q7) and the 2025 exam
(tertiary linalool is not oxidised by acidified dichromate) both examine it.

**AoS 2 *How are organic compounds analysed and used?***
- **qualitative tests for C=C, hydroxyl and carboxyl groups**
- melting point; simple and fractional distillation
- **degree of unsaturation with iodine**
- redox titrations (no back titrations)
- **MS:** molecular ion, simple fragments
- **IR:** bond identification
- **¹³C-NMR:** low resolution
- **¹H-NMR:** low and high resolution, integration, splitting with the n+1 rule, no coupling
  constants
- **HPLC:** retention times and calibration curves
- structure deduction from combined MS, IR and NMR
- **Medicinal chemistry:** solvent extraction and distillation of plant compounds; functional
  groups in medicines; "significance of isomers and the identification of chiral centres (carbon
  atom surrounded by four different groups)"; enzyme structure (primary–quaternary),
  denaturation, zwitterions; competitive inhibition (lock and key)

**AoS 3:** the student-designed investigation and scientific poster (energy, chemicals, or
organic analysis or synthesis). It may run across Units 3 and 4.

## 4. Conventions and notation

**Naming**
- IUPAC with locants before the suffix is standard: *propan-1-ol*, *propan-1-amine*,
  *1-chloro-2-methylpropan-2-ol* (all from 2024–2025 exams and reports).
- The older style is **also accepted**. The 2024 report says of propan-1-ol and 1-propanol:
  "Both of these follow IUPAC nomenclature conventions". VCAA's own June 2024 sample exam
  writes *2-methyl-2-propanol*, *1-hexene* and *2-hexene*. Games must accept both forms and
  should display the locant-before-suffix form by default.
- Esters use `-oate` (Data Book), so *ethyl ethanoate* style **(the exact ester names used in
  exams unverified)**. "Non-branched esters" only.
- Halogen and alkyl substituents are prefixes (fluoro- … iodo-; methyl- … octyl-). The
  prefixes of higher-priority groups (e.g. carboxy-, oxo-) are "beyond the scope of VCE
  Chemistry".
- Trivial names are used for fatty acids (lauric, palmitic, oleic …), amino acids (Data Book)
  and biomolecules.

**Formulas**
- Four representations are named in the Data Book: molecular, structural, semi-structural
  (condensed), skeletal.
- Condensed forms may use brackets: `CH3(CH2)2COOH`.
- The 2024 report rejects `OHCH2CH2CH2CH2CH3` for a terminal hydroxyl group; write `…CH2OH`.
- Lewis (electron dot) structures are used for small molecules in Unit 1.

**Isomers**
- Structural isomers; **cis/trans** (not E/Z); chiral centres; enantiomers.

**Energetics notation**
- ΔH in **kJ** for an equation as written, **kJ mol⁻¹** for molar enthalpy, **kJ g⁻¹** for
  mixtures and fuels.
- Exothermic ΔH is negative.
- **"Energy released" is reported as a positive quantity.** The 2024 report criticises answers
  that carry the negative sign into "energy released".
- SLC is 25 °C and 100 kPa; Vm = 24.8 L mol⁻¹; c(water) = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- Calibration factor: CF = E/ΔT (general) or nΔH/ΔT (reaction).
- Energy profile diagrams have "reaction progress" on the x-axis and are "diagrams, not
  graphs" (2024 NHT report).

**Equilibrium notation**
- `⇌`, Kc with square-bracket concentrations and **units**, and the reaction quotient Q.

**Concentration**
- mol L⁻¹, and the Data Book also writes "1.00 M". Also g L⁻¹, %(m/v), %(v/v) and ppm.

**Significant figures**
- The study design gives explicit rules, e.g. "400 mL" has three significant figures.
- Uncertainty is treated only qualitatively.

**Named reagents and observations** (Data Book item 8, reports)
- acidified dichromate: orange → green (Cr³⁺)
- acidified permanganate: intense purple → colourless or very pale pink (Mn²⁺). The 2025 report
  corrects answers that describe the colour change the wrong way round.
- bromine: reddish-brown → colourless
- iodine for unsaturation; starch–iodine blue-black
- UV light for alkane halogenation
- H₃PO₄ catalyst for hydration (sample exam: H₂O, H₃PO₄, 400 °C)
- conc. H₂SO₄ for esterification

**Spelling**
- Australian: *sulfur*, *aluminium*, *caesium*, *oxidising*, *colour*, *vaporisation*.

**Missing edge:** transesterification of a triglyceride with methanol (KOH/NaOH catalyst) to
biodiesel + glycerol is a verbatim dot point **(catalyst detail unverified)**. It suits a Level 4
or 5 "renewable feedstock" order and scores well on the green chemistry principle.

**Markovnikov (open question 2): out, and model both products instead.**
- Neither the study design nor any report checked mentions Markovnikov's rule.
- In the June 2024 sample exam, a linear C4 alkene with steam gives "two different organic
  products, Q and S … structural isomers of each other" (butan-2-ol and butan-1-ol). MC Q20
  treats 1-hexene + HCl as a route that "could be used" to make 2-chlorohexane.
- VCAA therefore expects students to know that an unsymmetrical alkene gives **both** isomers.
  It does not expect a major/minor prediction.
- **Recommendation:** at Level 5, unsymmetrical additions output both isomers (lower yield for
  the wanted one, or a separation step). Do not teach "major product" rules as VCE content.
