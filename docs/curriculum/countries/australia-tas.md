## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **Tasmania has one pre-tertiary chemistry course: Chemistry Level 4 (CHM415115).** It is
  150 hours with a single end-of-course exam, and is usually taken in Year 12 after Physical
  Sciences Level 3 in Year 11 **(unverified for "usually")**. There are no Units 1–4. Tag games
  by **criterion** (5 electrochemistry, 6 thermochemistry/kinetics/equilibrium, 7 organic and
  inorganic matter, 8 quantitative), not by unit.
- **Hydrocarbon naming is taught in Year 11 in Physical Sciences Level 3, not in Chemistry.**
  PSC315118 has IUPAC naming of branched and unbranched alkanes, alkenes, alkynes and cyclic
  compounds, with stems of up to **10 carbons**, halogen substituents, isomers, and simple
  reactions including the bromine test. Chemistry Level 4 then adds the functional groups.
- **Tasmania has the richest energetics of SA/NT/TAS.** It includes:
  - calorimetry with an electrically **calibrated calorimeter** (calibration factor
    Cf = VIt/ΔT) and change-of-state heat
  - **bond-energy calculations** and **Hess's law**
  - an **energy distribution diagram** for kinetics (Maxwell–Boltzmann in all but name)
  - a qualitative mention of **entropy**: equilibrium as a balance between "minimum enthalpy and
    maximum entropy"

  There is no Gibbs energy and no ΔfH° requirement **(ΔfH° unverified)**.
- **The organic reaction map is printed on the exam information sheet.** The sheet has a
  flowchart of "some organic reactions of aliphatic compounds", general formulae, stem names,
  worked example names, and a three-band IR table. A pathway or "synthesis map" game that mirrors
  this sheet fits Tasmania very well. Exam organic molecules are limited to **two functional
  groups**.
- **Tasmania has extras that SA/NT lack, and gaps of its own.**
  - In: alkyl halides and **substitution**, alkynes, benzene structure, asymmetric addition and
    the resulting isomers, **mass spectrometry and IR**, X-ray crystallography, percentage yield,
    gases (the ideal gas equation).
  - Out: cis/trans or E/Z, optical isomers, NMR, and biomolecules beyond amino acids.
- **Notation to copy:**
  - heat is **E = mcΔT** (not q), with c(water) = 4.184 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹
  - ΔH is written after a thermochemical equation in kJ
  - **Kc is written with units** (the 2025 exam: "about 20 mol⁻¹ L")
  - "Ek" for kinetic energy
  - "LCP" is an accepted abbreviation for Le Chatelier's Principle
- **Stable through at least 2026.** Accreditation was renewed "from 1 January 2026". A minor
  amendment was approved on 16 Sept 2026. Chemistry is **not** on the Years 9–12 list of courses
  revised in 2026 for 2027 delivery. No replacement course is announced.

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 The certificate and ATAR

- **TCE (Tasmanian Certificate of Education):**
  - at least two years of education and training after Year 10
  - **120 credit points**, of which **80 at Level 2 or higher**
  - the three "everyday adult" standards: reading, writing and communication; mathematics; use of
    computers and the internet
- **Chemistry CHM415115** carries **15 TCE credit points** and meets the **mathematics standard**
  ("Mathematics standard: Yes"). It does not meet the reading/writing or computers standards.
- **ATAR:** set by the University of Tasmania as Tasmania's tertiary admissions centre.
  - From 2025 it uses "an eligible student's best results in Level 3 and 4 courses over two years
    of senior secondary education". The previous rule that three results come from the final
    year has been removed.
  - Eligibility includes a TCE and "at least a Satisfactory Achievement or better in at least
    four TASC Level 3 or 4 courses".
  - Chemistry Level 4 counts.
- **Awards:** EA (Exceptional), HA (High), CA (Commendable), SA (Satisfactory), PA (Preliminary)
  Achievement.

### 1.2 Chemistry Level 4 (CHM415115)

- **Size and time:** "a design time of 150 hours". About 40 hours are recommended for practical
  work.
- **Access:** "It is highly recommended that learners studying Chemistry have successfully
  completed Physical Sciences Level 3, and, as a minimum, have studied or are currently studying
  General Mathematics Level 3 or equivalent." Completing Physical Sciences Level 3 "provides an
  essential preparation".
- **Content: four compulsory topics, in any order:**
  1. Fundamental principles and theories of electrochemistry (Criterion 5)
  2. Principles of thermochemistry, kinetics and equilibrium (Criterion 6)
  3. Properties and reactions of organic and inorganic matter (Criterion 7)
  4. Application of logical processes to solve quantitative chemical problems (Criterion 8)
- **Criteria:**
  1. personal skills to plan, organise and complete activities
  2. develop, interpret and evaluate chemistry experiments
  3. collect, process and communicate information
  4. understanding of the application and impact of chemistry in society
  5. electrochemistry (external and internal)
  6. thermochemistry, kinetics and equilibrium (external and internal)
  7. organic and inorganic matter (external and internal)
  8. quantitative problems (external and internal)
- **Criteria-based assessment:**
  - Each criterion gets a rating of A, B or C. "t" means below C and "z" means no evidence.
  - Criteria 5–8 are rated both internally and externally, giving 12 ratings in all (8 internal
    + 4 external).
  - Award rules:
    - EA needs 10 A and 2 B, with 3 A and 1 B from the exam
    - HA needs 4 A, 5 B and 3 C
    - CA needs 6 B and 5 C
    - SA needs 10 C, 3 of them from the exam
    - PA needs 6 C
- **External exam (External Assessment Specifications v1.2, Feb 2022):**
  - "The written exam is THREE hours", plus 15 minutes of preparation time.
  - **Four sections, A–D**, one per criterion 5–8. Each is about 45 minutes and **45 marks**,
    with five to seven compulsory questions.
  - "All criteria are assessed numerically with marks out of 45", then converted to A/B/C/t/z.
  - Marks equal the recommended minutes.
  - The question types are routine and non-routine contexts, real-world scenarios, short and
    extended responses, and closed and open-ended questions.
  - Allowed: a TASC-approved calculator and "The current year's External Exam Information Sheet
    *Chemistry*".
- **Moderation and quality assurance:** TASC QA of scope and sequence, assessment matrix and
  student work. DECYP publishes Common Assessment Tasks.

### 1.3 Physical Sciences Level 3 (PSC315118): the Year 11 lead-in

- A combined physics and chemistry course, 15 credit points, all content compulsory.
- Criteria 7 (chemical structures and properties) and 8 (chemical reactions and reacting
  quantities) are the chemistry half.
- It has an external exam: criteria 4–8 are marked with an asterisk, and the award is from 13
  ratings (5 of them external).
- There is no thermochemistry, kinetics or equilibrium in it. It has only "all physical changes
  are reversible, whereas only some chemical reactions are reversible".
- Physical Sciences – Foundation (Level 2, PSC215118) is a lower-level alternative. It covers
  energy conservation and particle ideas but no organic naming **(based on a keyword scan of the
  course page)**.

## 2. Content by course

### 2.1 Physical Sciences Level 3 (Year 11), chemistry threads

**Criterion 7: Chemical structures and properties.**

- The periodic table: trends, groups 1, 2, 17 and 18.
- Bonding (metallic, ionic, covalent); intra- vs intermolecular forces.
- Polarity is taught with water only: polar molecules have higher melting and boiling points.
- Allotropes of carbon; ion identification; solubility-table precipitation.
- **Carbon compounds. The first organic block:**
  - "carbon forms aliphatic hydrocarbon compounds including alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, cyclic
    alkanes and cyclic alkenes"
  - "structure and naming of organic compounds using IUPAC nomenclature. The stem to contain a
    maximum of 10 carbon atoms. This is limited to branched and unbranched alkanes, alkenes,
    alkynes and cyclic organic compounds containing one or more atoms of F, Cl, Br and I (NO other
    functional group chemistry)"
  - "the concept of an isomer, writing structural formulae for a given molecular formula"
  - saturated vs unsaturated
  - "the distinctions between empirical, molecular and structural formulae (structural formula
    must include all constituent atoms and bonds)"
  - simple reactions:
    - complete and incomplete combustion to given products
    - "substitution reactions with X₂"
    - "addition reactions with H₂, X₂ and HX"
    - "test for unsaturation using bromine solution"

**Criterion 8: Chemical reactions and reacting quantities.**

- The mole: n = N/N_A and n = m/M; percentage composition; empirical formulae.
- Concentration c = n/V; acids and bases (Brønsted–Lowry, "equations should use hydrogen ions and
  not hydronium ions"); strong/weak vs dilute/concentrated; simple titrations.

### 2.2 Chemistry Level 4 (usually Year 12)

**Criterion 5: Electrochemistry.**

- Oxidation states (including hydrides and peroxides); the electrochemical series; balancing with
  H⁺ and H₂O.
- Galvanic cells and cell diagrams (anode left, cathode right); E° of cells.
- Electrolysis of molten salts and aqueous solutions; Faraday's laws.
- Corrosion of steel and its prevention (paint, coatings, sacrificial anodes, applied EMF).

**Criterion 6: Thermochemistry.**

- "understand the concepts of heat content and enthalpy for chemical reactions"
- "understand that forming bonds releases, and that breaking bonds absorbs energy"; exothermic
  vs endothermic
- "construct enthalpy/potential energy diagrams including activation energy"
- Thermochemical equations: "write the heat of reaction as a reactant or product or write
  thermochemical equations using ΔH notation, and kJ for the reaction"
- Heat energy for a known mass of reactant or product
- **Calorimetry:**
  - "calibrate a calorimeter"
  - heat for a measured temperature change of a known mass
  - heat for a change of state (solid → liquid, liquid → gas)
  - "use the techniques of calorimetry to measure the heat of reaction"
- **Bond energy:** "define bond energy"; "identify the bonds that must be broken and formed in the
  course of a chemical reaction and calculate the heat of reaction using a table of bond
  energies"
- **Hess' Law:** "use Hess' Law of the additivity of heats of reaction to calculate ΔH for
  unknown reactions by summing thermochemical equations"

**Criterion 6: Kinetics.**

- Rate defined as the rate at which product forms or reactant is consumed.
  - Average rate = Δn(product)/t, or Δc(product)/t (course web page notation).
- Collision theory for:
  - concentration ("pressure for gases")
  - surface area
  - temperature
  - "nature of reactants i.e. more bonds to be broken the slower the reaction rate, complexity of
    the reaction (e.g. precipitation)"
  - catalysts
- **Temperature:**
  - "construct a diagram to show the distribution of kinetic energy for molecules at a particular
    temperature"
  - relate the rise in rate "to the increase in the proportion of molecules with sufficient
    kinetic energy to react"
- **Catalysts:** potential energy diagram showing the lower activation energy via "a different
  reaction pathway"; enzymes as biological catalysts.
- **Not present:** rate laws, order, rate constants, Arrhenius.

**Criterion 6: Chemical equilibrium.**

- Open and closed systems; reversibility.
- "understand that an equilibrium state represents a compromise (or balance) between the two
  naturally occurring driving forces towards minimum enthalpy and maximum entropy"
- Dynamic equilibrium: equal forward and reverse rates. Reversibility is explained by forward and
  reverse activation energies. Temperature effects are explained by forward and reverse enthalpy
  changes.
- Effects predicted from the rates of the competing reactions:
  - "changing the concentration of a species in solution"
  - "adding a catalyst"
  - "changing the volume of a container of gaseous reactants and products"
  - "changing the partial pressure of one gaseous reactant"
  - "adding an inert substance"
- Le Chatelier's Principle, and "Le Chatelier's principle has limitations in its application".
- The equilibrium law:
  - define K from the coefficients
  - concentrations of pure substances are incorporated into K
  - the size of K measures the extent of reaction
  - calculations
  - the reaction quotient Q gives the direction of change
- Acids:
  - Ka for monoprotic acids; Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C
  - Brønsted–Lowry (H⁺ or hydronium)
  - pH of strong and weak monoprotic acids and of strong bases

**Criterion 7: Properties and reactions of organic and inorganic matter.**

- **Gases:** Boyle's and Charles' laws, the ideal gas equation, kinetic theory, real gases.
- **Organic molecules:**
  - "Organic molecules have a hydrocarbon skeleton and can contain functional groups, including
    alkyl halides, alcohols, carboxylic acids, and esters"
  - "name and draw structural formulae of organic molecules including those with functional
    groups (amines and amides - simple only)"
  - amino acids as organic acids with an amine group
- Reaction classes: "addition, substitution, acid-base and oxidation reactions". "The organic
  compounds will be limited to only 2 functional groups for external assessment."
- **Alkyl halides:**
  - compare physical properties with the parent alkanes (polarity, dipole–dipole and dispersion
    forces)
  - they undergo substitution more readily than alkanes
  - "write equations for the reactions of alkyl halides to form primary, secondary and tertiary
    alcohols"
- **Benzene:** a flat symmetrical ring with bonds intermediate between single and double, all the
  same length, drawn as a hexagon with a circle.
- **Alcohols:**
  - hydrogen bonding → higher melting and boiling points and solubility in water vs alkanes
  - distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary
  - polyalcohols have much higher boiling points
  - reactions with sodium metal and with carboxylic acids
  - primary → aldehyde "when heated in the presence of copper catalysts or when treated with
    limited acidified dichromate solution"
  - primary → acid with "stronger oxidisers such as acidified permanganate solution"
  - secondary → ketone
  - "understand why tertiary alcohols do not undergo oxidation reactions"
- **Synthesis (addition, substitution, condensation):**
  - compare alkanes and alkenes with halogens (alkanes substitute; alkenes and alkynes add)
  - combustion with excess or insufficient oxygen
  - "write equations for the complete reactions of alkenes and alkynes with asymmetric molecules,
    including hydrogen halides and steam"
  - "identify the possible isomers resulting from an asymmetric molecule reacting with an
    unsaturated hydrocarbon" (Markovnikov's rule is not named)
  - addition polymers from alkenes; "polyesters are formed from polyalcohols and polyacids"
- **Aldehydes and ketones:** aldehydes oxidise to acids; aldehydes are less soluble than the
  parent alcohols; ketones cannot be oxidised further.
- **Carboxylic acids:**
  - weak acids; monoacids and polyacids
  - their solutions have a higher pH than strong acids of the same concentration
  - reactions with carbonates, NaHCO₃, metal oxides, hydroxides, reactive metals and alkalis
  - esterification with primary alcohols
- **Fuels:** "fuels can be synthesised from organic or sources" by addition, oxidation and
  esterification ("brief treatment only"). The word before "sources" is missing in the course
  text. The Australian Curriculum original reads "organic and inorganic".
- **Esters:** insoluble, distinctive smells; hydrolysis "including alkaline hydrolysis".
- **Amines and amides (simple):** hydrogen bonding → higher melting and boiling points and
  solubility.
- **Structure determination:**
  - "mass spectroscopy, including the determination of the molecular ion peak, relative
    molecular mass and identification of simple molecular fragments"
  - "Infra-red (IR) spectroscopy, including use of characteristic absorption bands to identify
    some functional groups"
  - "X-ray crystallography to identify simple molecular structures"
  - "often requires evidence from more than one technique"
  - Excludes instrumentation.
- **Periodic table:**
  - successive ionisation energies; emission and absorption spectra (qualitative)
  - the Bohr model; shells, subshells and orbitals
  - s/p/d configurations; isoelectronic species
  - valence theory and trends (electronegativity, core charge, shielding, radii)
  - mass spectrometry of isotopes

**Criterion 8: Quantitative problems.**

- Mass calculations; gravimetric analysis, including **"%-purity and %-yield"** and water of
  crystallisation.
- Primary standards, dilution, limiting reagents.
- Acid–base and redox titrations.
- **Chemical synthesis:** choosing reagents and conditions "to maximise yield and purity"; "calculate
  the yield of a chemical synthesis reaction by comparing stoichiometric quantities with actual
  quantities".
- Gas-law calculations (STP and SLC molar volumes).
- "Criteria 5, 6 and 7 calculations are examined externally as part of criterion 8" (e.g.
  Faraday).

## 4. Conventions and notation

**Nomenclature (course, info sheet, 2025 exam and report):**

- **Locants go before the suffix:** *propan-1-ol*, *pentan-3-one*, *butan-2-amine* (info sheet
  examples). Also *butanoic acid*, *ethanal*, *ethanamide*, *methyl propanoate*,
  *1-chlorobutane*, *butanamide*, *pentyl propanoate* (2025 exam).
- **Halo prefixes are the English forms** *bromo, chloro, fluoro, iodo*. Side chains on the info
  sheet are *methyl, ethyl, propyl*.
- Stem names meth- to dec- (1–10 C) and multiplying prefixes mono- to deca- are on the info
  sheet.
- **"Alkyl halides" is the course term** (not "haloalkanes"). The info sheet has a row "alkyl
  halides R–F, R–Cl, R–Br, R–I, CnH2n+1X".
- **General formulae on the info sheet:**
  - alkanes CnH2n+2
  - alkenes CnH2n
  - alkynes CnH2n−2
  - cyclic alkanes CnH2n
  - cyclic alkenes CnH2n−2
  - benzene C6H6
  - alcohols CnH2n+2O
  - aldehydes and ketones CnH2nO
  - carboxylic acids and esters CnH2nO2
  - amines CnH2n+3N
  - amides CnH2n+1ON
- Isomers are structural only. There is no cis/trans or E/Z anywhere in the TASC documents read.

**Organic reaction flowchart (info sheet page 7; paraphrased from the text layer, layout
approximate):**

- alkene + H₂ → alkane
- alkene + HX(g) → alkyl halide
- alkene + H₂O(g) → alcohol
- alcohol → alkene by loss of H₂O, using H₂SO₄(l). This dehydration is on the sheet but not in
  the course text.
- alkyl halide + dilute NaOH(aq) → alcohol
- alkyl halide + NH₃(g) → amine
- alcohol + active metal (Na) → alkoxide + ½H₂
- primary alcohol [O]:
  - with milder oxidisers, e.g. acidified dichromate → aldehyde
  - with an excess of a stronger oxidiser, e.g. acidified permanganate → carboxylic acid
- secondary alcohol [O] → ketone
- carboxylic acid + base, active metal or carbonate → salt
- carboxylic acid + alcohol with conc. H₂SO₄ → ester; hydrolysis with a catalyst reverses it
- alkene → polymer by addition polymerisation with a catalyst

The 2025 report notes the Cu catalyst was "the only reactant not on the formula sheet". It also
required the charges on the alkoxide (–) and sodium (+) ions.

**Energetics and kinetics notation (course and info sheet):**

- **E = mcΔT** (E in J; c in J g⁻¹ K⁻¹; ΔT in K), with **c(water) = 4.184 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹**.
- **Cf = VIt/ΔT** and **Cf = E/ΔT** (calibration factor in J K⁻¹); E = VIt.
- ΔH is written in thermochemical equations, "using ΔH notation, and kJ for the reaction".
  - The exam specs example is "ΔH = −910 kJ".
  - The 2025 exam wrote "ΔH = −5.6 kJ mol⁻¹" after an equation (the Δ was lost in text
    extraction).
  - Both kJ and kJ mol⁻¹ appear, so a game should accept both where appropriate
    **(unverified whether markers distinguish them)**.
- In the 2025 report, ΔH was accepted in **kJ g⁻¹** for a fuel of unknown molar mass.
- The kinetic-energy symbol **Ek** is used in the 2025 marking guide.
- The report lists "L.C.P" and "S.T.P" as acceptable standard abbreviations. All others must be
  defined first.

**Equilibrium notation:**

- The info sheet gives Kc = [R]ᶻ[S]ʷ/[P]ˣ[Q]ʸ "where none of components P, Q, R or S is a solid".
- It gives Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² L⁻² at 25 °C, pH = −log[H⁺] and [H⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ, with
  the note [H⁺] = [H₃O⁺].
- **Kc carries units** (e.g. "mol⁻¹ L" in the 2025 exam; "mol² L⁻²" for Kw).

**Conditions and constants (info sheet):**

- S.T.P. = 0 °C (273 K) and 101.3 kPa, molar volume 22.4 L mol⁻¹.
- S.L.C. = 25 °C (298 K) and 101.3 kPa, molar volume 24.5 L mol⁻¹.
- R in four unit sets: 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, 8.31 L kPa K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, 0.0821 L atm K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, and
  62.4 L mm Hg K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
- N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; F = 96 500 C mol⁻¹.

**Info sheet (v2, Feb 2026) contents:**

- constants, as above
- unit prefixes
- formulae: n = m/M, N = nN_A, c = n/V, combined and ideal gas equations, n(e⁻) = q/F = It/F,
  E = mcΔT, E = VIt, Cf, density
- tables of common cations and anions
- a solubility table
- equilibrium formulae
- coloured ions (e.g. dichromate orange, permanganate purple, chromate yellow)
- the organic section (general formulae, stems, side chains, functional-group examples, reaction
  flowchart)
- IR stretching frequencies:
  - O–H (alcohol) 3650–3200 cm⁻¹, strong and broad
  - O–H (carboxylic acid) 3300–2500 cm⁻¹, strong and very broad
  - C=O 1780–1650 cm⁻¹, strong
- standard reduction potentials
- a relative atomic mass table, a periodic-trends diagram and a periodic table (masses to 4
  significant figures)
- **No bond-energy table and no ΔfH° data.**

**Reagents:**

- acidified dichromate ("limited", for aldehyde)
- acidified permanganate (a stronger oxidiser)
- copper catalyst (heated, for primary alcohol → aldehyde)
- sodium metal
- concentrated sulfuric acid (esterification; dehydration on the flowchart)
- bromine solution (unsaturation, PS3)
- dilute NaOH (alkyl halide → alcohol)
- NH₃ (alkyl halide → amine)

**Spelling and units:** Australian spelling (*sulfur*, *aluminium*, *caesium*, *ionisation*); the
2025 report writes "sulphate" once. Units use negative indices (mol L⁻¹, J g⁻¹ K⁻¹). Physical
Sciences asks for equations with **H⁺ rather than H₃O⁺**; Chemistry accepts either.

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

The Chemistry course document has a **Glossary (Appendix 2)**. The command-type terms are
paraphrased below. Some courses amended for 2027 are moving their glossaries out of the course
document; Chemistry is not among them, so its glossary stays in the document.

| Term | Meaning (paraphrase of the TASC course glossary) |
|---|---|
| Analyse | Examine in detail to find meaning or relationships and to identify patterns, similarities and differences |
| Critically analyse | Examine the parts of an issue or information, e.g. the premise of an argument, its plausibility, and faulty reasoning or conclusions |
| Critically evaluate | Evaluate while weighing the important factors and available evidence, to reach a justifiable critical judgement |
| Communicates | Conveys knowledge or understanding to others |
| Demonstrate | Give a practical exhibition as an explanation |
| Describe | Give an account of characteristics or features |
| Evaluate | Examine in detail and give a substantiated judgement of merit, significance or value |
| Explain | Give additional information that shows understanding of reasoning and/or application |
| Identify | Establish or indicate who or what something is |
| Research | Locate, gather, record, attribute and analyse information to build understanding |
| Solve | Work out a correct solution to a problem |
| Complex / Familiar / Unfamiliar | Qualifiers used in the standards: many interconnected parts; previously met in learning; not previously met |

Other terms:

- **Standards verbs step up across the ratings,** for example identifies (C) → describes (B) →
  explains (A) in Criteria 5 and 6, and "routine" (C) → plain (B) → "complex" or "unfamiliar" (A)
  calculations.
- **The 2025 Assessment Report adds exam command terms:** "state", "explain", "label",
  "determine", "outline", "calculate", "compare". It says answers "need to reflect the command
  terms in the question".
- **The report also sets these marking rules:**
  - numerical answers need context and units in every section
  - contradictory statements lose marks
  - half-marks are used
