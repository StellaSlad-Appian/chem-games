# Chemistry curriculum: France (Years 7–12)

**Purpose:** input for the cross-country concept/year map for chem-games.
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches in France.
**Key sources:** the national programmes published in the *Bulletin officiel* (BO): cycle 4 physique-chimie (BO n°31, 30 July 2020), 2nde and 1re (BO spécial n°1, 22 Jan 2019), Terminale (BO spécial n°8, 25 July 2019), enseignement scientifique (BO n°25, 22 June 2023); bac and brevet exam notes for session 2027 (BO spécial n°4, 17 Sept 2026); timetable orders on Légifrance. Full list in §6.

## Summary for game design

- **Chemistry is never a subject of its own before university.** From 5e (Year 7) to Terminale (Year 12) it is taught as half of **physique-chimie** (physics-chemistry), by one teacher, in one timetable slot. Before that, 6e (Year 6) and primary cover matter only inside integrated *sciences et technologie*.
- **The collège programme is set per cycle, not per year.** Cycle 4 covers 5e, 4e and 3e (Years 7–9). The 2020 text only gives end-of-cycle expectations plus *repères de progressivité* (guidance on sequencing). The year placements in §2 for Years 7–9 therefore follow that guidance, and some are common practice only. Games should treat Years 7–9 as one band with soft internal ordering.
- **Big topics land late.** The mole, electron configurations, ions and Lewis structures first appear in **2nde (Year 10)**. The *tableau d'avancement* (reaction table), redox, titration, Beer–Lambert, polarity, intermolecular forces and first organic chemistry come in **1re (Year 11)**. Acids and bases by Brønsted with pH = −log[H₃O⁺], kinetics, equilibrium (Qr and K), cells and electrolysis, and multi-step organic synthesis come in **Terminale (Year 12)**.
- **The track split comes at Year 11.** Everyone takes the same physique-chimie in 2nde (3 h per week). In 1re, *spécialité physique-chimie* (4 h) is optional; general-track students choose 3 spécialités. In Terminale the spécialité is 6 h, and students keep 2 of their 3. All general-track students also take ***enseignement scientifique*** (common core), which carries only light chemistry: nucleosynthesis, radioactivity, crystals, atmospheric chemistry and climate. Technological series (STL, STI2D, ST2S) have their own chemistry programmes.
- **Nothing changes in 2026/27 for chemistry content in Years 7–12.** New cycle 4 physique-chimie programmes, set year by year, are in draft. The 6e programme changes from rentrée 2027, and 5e presumably not before rentrée 2028 (unverified). The June bac *épreuve de spécialité* (session 2027) covers the **whole Terminale programme**. Year 11 content may be drawn on, but it cannot be the main thrust of a question.
- **Notation that games must respect:**
  - decimal comma (6,02 × 10²³);
  - units written as mol·L⁻¹ (never "M" or "molar");
  - "concentration en masse" (g·L⁻¹) is kept distinct from "concentration en quantité de matière" (mol·L⁻¹);
  - "HO⁻" at lycée, although the collège text writes "OH⁻";
  - "ion oxonium H₃O⁺";
  - di- prefixes for elemental gases: dioxygène, dihydrogène, diazote;
  - "transformation" (what is observed) is kept separate from "réaction" (the model);
  - "espèce chimique" (macroscopic substance) is kept separate from "entité chimique" (particle);
  - "gaz nobles", "schéma de Lewis", "formule semi-développée", "pont hydrogène".
- **Surprises for an English-style map:**
  - subshell electron configurations (1s 2s 2p 3s 3p) come in Year 10, before any shell-number (2,8,8) stage;
  - no gas laws beyond Mariotte (Boyle) in Year 11 and PV = nRT in Year 12;
  - no enthalpy notation (ΔH) at all; energetics goes through bond energies and "énergie molaire de réaction";
  - the "vitesse de réaction" (rate as d(advancement)/dt) is explicitly excluded; only volumetric rates of formation or disappearance are taught;
  - nuclear chemistry sits inside chemistry ("Constitution et transformations de la matière").
- **Practical work is examined.** The bac has a 1-hour practical, the *ECE* (évaluation des compétences expérimentales), worth 20 % of the spécialité mark. Python programming (*capacités numériques*) is written into the chemistry programme.

Technological series [S12] (hours per week):
- **STL** (*sciences et technologies de laboratoire*): *physique-chimie et mathématiques* 5 h in 1re and Tle. ### Reforms in force or being phased in (2026/27)

- **Cycle 4 physique-chimie (Years 7–9): unchanged in 2026/27.** The in-force text is the cycle 4 programme consolidated in BO n°31 of 30 July 2020 [S1]. Its history: first published 2015, applied from rentrée 2016, adjusted in 2020 to stress climate change, biodiversity and sustainable development [S17].
  - The *Conseil supérieur des programmes* (CSP) published a draft **year-by-year** programme (separate 5e / 4e / 3e content) in July 2025 [S15][S9b].
  - Other cycle 4 subjects (physique-chimie, SVT, technologie, history-geography) were then postponed indefinitely; only French and maths moved ahead [S9c].
  - A new national consultation on the physique-chimie draft ran **18 May – 19 June 2026** [S9a].
  - No arrêté for a new cycle 4 physique-chimie programme had been found as of 24 Sept 2026.
  - SNES-FSU (a teachers' union) "hopes" the 5e programme will apply at **rentrée 2028** [S9a]. By extension, 4e would follow in 2029 and 3e in 2030 **(unverified; staggered roll-out agreed in principle only)**.
- **Cycle 3 sciences et technologie (6e, Year 6): new programme adopted.** Arrêté of 5 June 2026, BO n°24 of 11 June 2026 [S8]. It applies to CP and CM1 from 2026/27, and to CE1, CE2, CM2 and **6e from 2027/28**. The draft has a strand *La matière, les mouvements et les signaux*: states, mixtures, dissolving, conservation of mass [S16].
- **Lycée physique-chimie (Years 10–12): no reform.** The 2019 programmes (2nde and 1re from 2019/20, Tle from 2020/21) are unchanged [S2][S3][S4]. *Enseignement scientifique* was revised in BO n°25 of 22 June 2023 (1re from 2023/24, Tle from 2024/25) [S5][S6].
- **Exams for session 2027** (i.e. students in 3e and Terminale in 2026/27) are redefined in BO spécial n°4 of 17 Sept 2026 [S7]:
  - new notes for the DNB, the bac spécialité exams, the épreuves anticipées (French, maths) and the technological series;
  - common "maîtrise de la langue" (language quality) marks: 2 of 20 points in the physique-chimie spécialité written paper.
- **Épreuve anticipée de mathématiques:** a maths exam at the end of 1re, general and technological tracks. First sitting was June 2026; it is redefined for 2027 [S7]. Not chemistry, but it makes quantitative skills (proportionality, powers of ten, logs) examined in Year 11 for all students.
- **DNB:**
  - since session 2026 the final mark is 60 % final exams and 40 % 3e year averages [S7];
  - the brevet is *not* a condition for entering 2nde; a plan to require it was withdrawn in 2025 [S19] (partly verified).

### National exams that shape content

- **DNB, end of 3e (Year 9)**, *épreuve de sciences* [S7]:
  - 1 h, coefficient 2;
  - two of the three disciplines (physique-chimie, SVT, technologie), 10 points each, announced 2 months before the exam;
  - covers the 3e programme "if in force", otherwise the cycle 4 programme. For 2027 that means the whole cycle 4 physique-chimie content.
- **Bac général, épreuve de spécialité physique-chimie, June of Terminale (Year 12)** [S7]:
  - written paper 3 h 30: 3 exercises, contextualised, with document analysis and "questions with initiative" (open problems); calculator per paper;
  - practical ECE 1 h, drawn from a national bank, with at least 2 chemistry-dominant and 2 physics-dominant situations;
  - final mark = 0.8 × written + 0.2 × ECE;
  - scope: **the whole Terminale programme**, plus 1re notions "known and usable but not the main thrust";
  - the partial exclusion lists used for the March 2022/2023 sittings no longer apply; exams moved to June from session 2024 [S18].
- **Spécialité dropped at the end of 1re:** it is assessed by contrôle continu (school grades), not a national exam (not re-checked this session).
- **Enseignement scientifique:** contrôle continu. Separate "évaluations ponctuelles" apply to some candidates [S18b].
- **Technological bacs:** STL, STI2D and ST2S have their own June exams, redefined for session 2027 [S7]; not analysed.

**Typical interval:**
- Major revisions come about every **7–10 years**. Collège: 2008 → 2015/16 → expected 2028+. Lycée: 2000–02 → 2010–12 → 2019–20.
- Revision is **ad hoc and reform-driven**: it follows ministerial or presidential reform projects (collège 2016, bac 2021, "choc des savoirs" 2023–24). There is **no statutory review cycle**.
- The CSP drafts on request, followed by public consultation and a vote of the *Conseil supérieur de l'éducation*. Ministerial turnover has repeatedly paused drafts; see the 2025 postponement [S9c].

**Smaller updates:**
- Exam-definition *notes de service* are reissued every 2–4 years: 2011, 2020, 2021, 2026.
- Timing changes (March vs June) have happened three times in 2021–2024.
- Targeted *aménagements* or "consolidated" versions appear every few years: 2017, 2020, 2023.
- The ECE practical bank is refreshed annually. It is a national bank chosen from by académies [S7]; there is no per-year content narrowing since 2024.

**Announced or in progress now:**
- New 6e sciences from rentrée 2027 (adopted).
- New annual cycle 4 physique-chimie programmes: draft consulted in 2026; start in 5e possibly **rentrée 2028**, then 4e and 3e in later years (unverified).
- The brevet sciences exam will switch to "3e programme" content once that exists [S7].
- **No lycée physique-chimie revision announced** (none found).

**Stability rating for game content: moderate churn.** Lycée chemistry (Years 10–12) has been stable since 2019 and has no announced replacement. Collège content (Years 7–9) is stable for 2026/27, but a year-by-year replacement is likely from about 2028. That will move topics between 5e, 4e and 3e; per the draft, the periodic table and equations go into 3e [S9b]. Exam rules also change every 2–3 years.

## 2. Year-by-year concepts and skills

Cycle 4 (Years 7–9) is one programme, and its topics can be spread across the three years [S1]. Items below are placed in a year only where the *repères de progressivité* say so ("from 5e", "from 4e", "reserved for 3e"). Otherwise they are placed by **common practice (unverified)** and marked (cp). Physics-only topics (motion, forces, electricity, light, sound) are omitted.

### Year 7 (5e)

**Concepts** [S1]
- *Matter & particles:* the states of matter (solid, liquid, gas); changes of state of a pure substance (*corps pur*); conservation of mass but **not** volume during a change of state; change-of-state temperature used to identify pure substances; water as the main example; melting ice and climate. A **particle model** "can be developed from 5e".
- *Mixtures & separation:* *espèce chimique*, *corps pur* vs *mélange*; solubility and miscibility "from the start of the cycle"; dissolving gases (CO₂) in water; composition of air (cp); water treatment (decontamination, purification, desalination).
- *Chemical reactions:* experimental examples of chemical transformations "from 5e", linked to history of science and daily life (cp: combustion as a first example).
- *Energetics:* revisit cycle 3 energy sources and conversions; orders of magnitude of energies from 5e (physics-led).

**Skills** [S1]
- Practical: propose and carry out a protocol to study changes of state; heating curves; measure mass and volume; lab safety rules in chemistry.
- Quantitative: mass–volume proportionality for a substance (qualitative or proportional reasoning only; density as a quotient comes later).
- Models: first particle pictures of states and changes of state.
- Inquiry/communication: formulate hypotheses; design an experiment; record results; precise scientific vocabulary; distinguish knowledge from belief.

### Year 8 (4e)

**Concepts** [S1]
- *Matter & particles:* *masse volumique* (density, ρ = m/V) introduced "progressively from 4e"; its temperature dependence (sea-level rise); used to tell materials apart.
- *Chemical reactions & equations:* identify a chemical transformation experimentally; distinguish chemical transformation from mixing and from physical change; a transformation interpreted as a **redistribution of atoms**; conservation of mass; **equations "initiated in 4e in simple cases"**; using a given equation to describe an observed transformation.
- *Formulas & nomenclature:* molecules and atoms; interpret a formula in terms of atoms. Required formulas: O₂, H₂, N₂, H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O (protoxyde d'azote).
- *Periodic table:* used "from 4e" as a **classification and look-up tool** (name ↔ symbol ↔ atomic number), "without insisting on the notion of element".
- *Industrial, environmental & applied:* combustion in air and greenhouse gases produced (cp placement); carbon footprint; decarbonised energy, recycling, CO₂ capture.
- *Mixtures:* composition of air (cp; may be 5e).

**Skills** [S1]
- Practical: characteristic tests for species from a supplied bank (e.g. limewater for CO₂) (cp); determine the density of a liquid or solid by a self-designed protocol.
- Quantitative: quotient quantities with maths (ρ = m/V, rearranging); unit conversion.
- Models: particle/atom models of molecules; ball-and-stick representations (cp); moving between a word description and an equation.
- Inquiry: plan experimental tasks; use digital data-acquisition tools; bibliographic research.

### Year 9 (3e)

**Concepts** [S1]
- *Atomic structure:* constituents of the atom; nucleus (protons, neutrons = nucleons) and electrons. This is "can be reserved for 3e", together with powers of ten.
- *Acids, bases & salts:* acidic/basic character of a solution from **pH measurement**; acidity/basicity linked to **H⁺ and OH⁻ ions**; acid–base reactions; acid–metal reactions; acidification of the oceans. (cp: 3e; the programme lists these without a year.)
- *Formulas & nomenclature:* ions as particles, alongside molecules and atoms.
- *Redox (qualitative only):* corrosion of a metal (no electron-transfer model at this level).
- *Chemical reactions:* consolidation of equations; conservation of mass.
- *Inorganic/descriptive:* elements on Earth and in the Universe (H, He, heavy elements O, C, Fe, Si, rare earths); origin of matter; stars. This strand runs through the whole cycle.
- *Energetics:* forms of energy including *chimique* and *nucléaire*; energy conversions (e.g. devices emitting CO₂); energy balance of a simple system.

**Skills** [S1]
- Practical: pH measurement (paper or meter); characteristic ion/gas tests; safe handling of acids (pictograms, cp).
- Quantitative: powers of ten and scientific notation (atom sizes); reading data tables.
- Models: atom model (nucleus + electrons); interpreting ions as charged particles.
- Inquiry/communication: argue from experimental results; open questions requiring initiative (a DNB format) [S7]; civic reasoning on environment and health.

### Year 10 (2nde)

Common to all general and technological students. Topics come from the theme "Constitution et transformations de la matière" [S2].

**Concepts**
- *Matter & particles / Mixtures:* pure substances vs homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures; identifying a species by change-of-state temperatures, density, or chemical tests (H₂O, H₂, O₂, CO₂); mass composition of a mixture; volume composition of air.
- *Solutions & concentration:* aqueous solutions (solvent, solute); **concentration en masse** (g·L⁻¹); maximum concentration (solubility); preparation by **dissolution and dilution**; **calibration-curve assay** (*dosage par étalonnage*, colour scale or density).
- *Atomic structure:* the nucleus holds the atom's mass and identity; atom and nucleus size and mass orders of magnitude; Z, A, notation ᴬ_ZX; element; masses and charges of e⁻, p, n; elementary charge.
- *Periodic table:* **electron configuration (1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p) for Z ≤ 18**; position in the table (s and p blocks); valence electrons; chemical families; noble gases.
- *Bonding & structure:* stability of noble-gas configurations; **monatomic ions** (name and formula: H⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cl⁻, F⁻); molecules; **Lewis model** (given Lewis structures are interpreted; bonding and lone pairs); qualitative bond energy.
- *Formulas & nomenclature:* molecule / atom / anion / cation; ionic compounds from electroneutrality.
- *Stoichiometry & the mole:* counting entities; mass of an entity; **definition of the mole** (exactly 6.022 140 76 × 10²³ entities); amount of substance (mol) in a sample.
- *Chemical reactions & equations:* modelling a transformation by a reaction; writing and **balancing equations**; spectator species; stoichiometry; **limiting reagent**. Required equations: combustion of carbon and methane, corrosion of a metal by acid, acid on limestone, HCl + NaOH.
- *Energetics:* physical transformations: equations for changes of state; exo/endothermic; specific latent energy (energy = m × L). Chemical transformations: exo/endothermic by temperature monitoring; energy depends on the amount of limiting reagent.
- *Organic / synthesis:* a species synthesised in the lab can be identical to a natural one; synthesis by **reflux**.
- *Analytical:* **thin-layer chromatography (CCM)**.
- *Nuclear chemistry:* isotopes; nuclear equations; energy in the Sun and nuclear power stations; classifying a transformation as physical, chemical or nuclear.

**Skills** [S2]
- Practical: choose glassware for dissolution and dilution; measure change-of-state temperature and density; TLC; reflux set-up with a labelled diagram; temperature monitoring of reactions.
- Quantitative: percentages and fractions; quotient quantities; proportionality; powers of ten and scientific notation; significant figures. Measurement and uncertainty: histogram, mean, standard deviation, **standard uncertainty** (*incertitude-type*) by a statistical approach; comparison with a reference value. Spreadsheet histogram.
- Models: macroscopic ↔ microscopic scale; given Lewis structures; symbolic equations for physical, chemical and nuclear change.
- Inquiry: the competences *s'approprier, analyser/raisonner, réaliser, valider, communiquer* (appropriate, analyse/reason, carry out, validate, communicate) run across the whole lycée. Python is the recommended language.

### Year 11 (1re)

**Concepts: [spécialité PC]** [S3]
- *Stoichiometry & the mole:* molar mass (from atomic molar masses); amount from mass; **molar volume of a gas**; amount of each species in a mixture.
- *Solutions & concentration:* **concentration en quantité de matière** (mol·L⁻¹); ionic dissolution equations with (s) and (aq); concentration of ions in solution.
- *Analytical & instrumental:* absorbance, UV-visible absorption spectrum, colour of a solution (complementary colours); **Beer–Lambert law**; standard series. **Colour-indicated titration**: equivalence as the change of limiting reagent. **Infrared spectroscopy** to identify functional groups.
- *Redox & electrochemistry:* oxidant, reductant, **Ox/Red couple**, **half-equations** (*demi-équations électroniques*), redox equations from given couples; redox as the basis for titrations; combustion modelled as redox.
- *Chemical reactions & equations:* initial state, **advancement x (mol)**, ***tableau d'avancement***, final state; x_final vs x_max; **total vs non-total transformation** (without mentioning equilibrium); stoichiometric mixtures.
- *Bonding & structure:* **writing Lewis structures** of molecules and mono- or polyatomic ions (O₂, H₂, N₂, H₂O, CO₂, NH₃, CH₄, HCl, H⁺, H₃O⁺, Na⁺, NH₄⁺, Cl⁻, HO⁻, O²⁻); *lacune électronique* (empty orbital, electron deficiency); **molecular geometry** from Lewis structures; **electronegativity** and its periodic trend; bond polarisation; polar/apolar molecules.
- *Bonding & structure (intermolecular):* cohesion of ionic and molecular solids; interactions between ions, polar and apolar entities, and ***pont hydrogène*** (hydrogen bond); solvation of ions; solubility and miscibility explained by interactions; **liquid–liquid extraction**; hydrophilic, lipophilic and amphiphilic species; **soaps and surfactants**.
- *Organic chemistry:* molecular and **semi-developed (condensed structural) formulas**; saturated carbon chains; functional groups and families: **alcohol, aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid**; name ↔ formula for molecules with one functional group.
- *Organic synthesis:* protocol steps: transformation, isolation, purification, analysis; **yield** (*rendement*).
- *Energetics:* organic fuels; complete combustion of an alkane or alcohol; **molar reaction energy estimated from bond energies** (gas phase); calorific value (*pouvoir calorifique massique*); fossil vs bio-sourced fuels; methanisation.
- *Gases [spécialité PC, in the "fluids" part]:* macroscopic description of a fluid (ρ, P, T) linked to particle behaviour; **Mariotte's law** (Boyle); pressure force F = P·S.

**Concepts: [enseignement scientifique]** (all general-track students) [S5]
- *Nuclear chemistry:* stellar nucleosynthesis and element abundances; fusion vs fission; random decay; **half-life** (number left after n half-lives; graphical determination); dating; medical uses; precautions.
- *Bonding & structure / Inorganic:* **the crystalline state**: NaCl as an ordered stack of ions; unit cell (*maille*) ↔ crystal ↔ mineral ↔ rock; polymorphism; amorphous solids (glass); biological crystals.
- *Biochemistry:* photosynthesis as solar-to-chemical energy conversion (SVT-led).

**Concepts: [STL] [STI2D] [ST2S]:** *physique-chimie et mathématiques*, and *physique-chimie pour la santé*. Content not analysed (see §5).

**Skills** [S3][S5]
- Practical: prepare a calibration series and measure absorbance; direct titration with a colour endpoint; reflux synthesis; isolate, purify (recrystallisation, cp) and analyse (TLC, melting point, cp); estimate the calorific value of a fuel; liquid–liquid extraction with a separating funnel; test Mariotte's law with a microcontroller.
- Quantitative: first-degree linear equations (reaction tables); yield; bond-energy sums; geometric sequences for decay [ES].
- Models: molecular models and visualisation software (geometry); Lewis structures; semi-developed formulas; 3D crystal representations [ES].
- Digital: **Python** to compute the final state of a total transformation.
- Inquiry/communication: justify the choice of separation or purification method from physical properties; scientific argument; sustainability and environmental debates.

### Year 12 (Terminale)

**Concepts: [spécialité PC]** (whole programme examined in June [S7]) [S4]
- *Acids, bases & salts:* **Brønsted acid/base, acid–base couple**, transfer of H⁺; couples of water, carbonic acid, carboxylic acids, amines; amphoteric species. **pH = −log([H₃O⁺]/c°)** with c° = 1 mol·L⁻¹. **K_A, K_e**, pK_A; strong vs weak acids and bases (strong = quasi-total reaction with water); common acid/base solutions with their dissolved species (HCl, HNO₃, CH₃COOH, NaOH, NH₃). **Predominance and distribution diagrams**; indicators; α-amino acids; **buffer solutions**.
- *Analytical & instrumental:* Beer–Lambert; **conductance/conductivity, Kohlrausch's law**; IR and UV-visible spectra for identification; mass fraction (*titre massique*) and relative density (*densité*) of a solution. **pH-metric and conductimetric titrations** (composition after each addition; slope interpretation from molar ionic conductivities).
- *Kinetics:* slow vs fast transformations; kinetic factors (temperature, concentration); **catalysis**; **volumetric rate** of disappearance or appearance; **half-reaction time**; **first-order rate law**. Microscopic side: **reaction mechanisms**, elementary steps, intermediates, **curly-arrow formalism**; effective collisions. (The "rate of reaction" as d(advancement)/dt is explicitly off-programme.)
- *Nuclear chemistry:* (N,Z) diagram; α, β⁻, β⁺ decay and γ emission; conservation laws; **decay law N(t)** (first-order differential equation); radioactive constant; activity; dating; medical applications; radiation protection.
- *Equilibrium:* the final state of a non-total transformation as **dynamic equilibrium**; **reaction quotient Qr** (dimensionless, only for dissolved, liquid or solid species, no partial pressures); **K(T)**; criterion of spontaneous evolution; final advancement ratio (*taux d'avancement final*).
- *Redox & electrochemistry:* spontaneous electron transfer, by contact or through an external circuit; **cells** (*piles*): half-cells, salt bridge or membrane, open-circuit voltage, electrode reactions, polarity, capacity (charge) of a cell; common oxidants and reductants (eau de Javel, O₂, Cl₂, ascorbic acid, H₂, metals; s-block metals as reductants). **Electrolysis**: forced evolution; amount formed from I·Δt; chemical energy storage (batteries, *accumulateurs*, photosynthesis).
- *Organic chemistry:* **skeletal formulas** (*formule topologique*); naming using supplied IUPAC rules; families: **esters, amines, amides, halogenoalkanes**; unsaturated and cyclic chains; **constitutional isomers**. Optimising rate and yield (excess reagent, removing a product). **Multi-step synthesis strategy** from a reaction bank: substitution, addition, elimination, acid–base, redox, polymerisation; **protection/deprotection**; **green (eco-responsible) synthesis**.
- *Polymers & materials:* identify the repeat unit; natural vs synthetic polymers and uses.
- *Gases [spécialité PC]:* **ideal-gas model and equation of state (PV = nRT)**, also used to find amounts; limits of the model. First law of thermodynamics (physics-led).

**Concepts: [enseignement scientifique]** [S6]
- *Industrial, environmental & applied:* evolution of the atmosphere (primitive N₂/CO₂/H₂O → today about 78 % N₂, 21 % O₂); O₂ sources and sinks (photosynthesis, respiration, combustion); **stratospheric ozone formation** (UV dissociation of O₂) and depletion; **greenhouse gases** (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O); carbon sinks (ocean, biosphere); fossil-fuel CO₂.
- *Energetics / applied:* future of energy: electricity generation, energy storage devices compared, energy choices and climate.

**Concepts: [STL-SPCL] [STI2D] [ST2S]:** not analysed. SPCL is the lab-intensive physical-chemistry option (see §5).

**Skills** [S4]
- Practical: pH-metric and conductimetric titrations; making up a solution from mass fraction and density data; building a cell and measuring its voltage and polarity; electrolysis; kinetic monitoring with a sensor (e.g. spectrophotometer or conductimeter); studying factors affecting yield and rate; eco-responsible synthesis. The **ECE practical exam** tests all of these [S7].
- Quantitative: **decimal logarithm** and its inverse; **quadratic equations** (weak-acid equilibrium); first-order linear differential equations (decay, first-order kinetics); derivatives (rates).
- Digital: **Python** to plot quantities vs titrant volume, concentration vs time and rate tests, acid–base distribution diagrams, and final advancement ratio.
- Models: microscopic (mechanisms, curly arrows) ↔ macroscopic (rate laws); Qr/K model; predominance diagrams.
- Inquiry/communication: problem solving with initiative (*résolution de problème*); critical reading of documents; environmental impact of syntheses. Language quality is marked (2/20) [S7]. The *Grand oral* (a 20-minute oral exam) may be on a physique-chimie topic.

## 3. Terminology and notation conventions

- **Nomenclature:** French IUPAC (UICPA) names are used from lycée: *éthanol, acide éthanoïque, propanone, méthanal*. Traditional names survive for everyday substances: *soude* (NaOH solution), *acide chlorhydrique*, *eau de Javel*, *ammoniac*, *calcaire*.
  - Elemental gases take di- prefixes at every level: *dioxygène*, *dihydrogène*, *diazote*, *dichlore*.
  - CO₂ is *dioxyde de carbone*; N₂O is *protoxyde d'azote* in the collège text.
  - In Terminale, students apply *supplied* nomenclature rules rather than memorised ones [S4].
- **Formulas:** *formule brute* (molecular), *formule développée* (displayed), *formule semi-développée* (condensed structural, from 1re), *formule topologique* (skeletal, Tle), *schéma de Lewis* with *doublets liants/non liants* and *lacune électronique*.
- **Key false friends and distinctions:**
  - *transformation* (the observed change of a system) vs *réaction* (the model written as an equation);
  - *espèce chimique* (macroscopic substance) vs *entité chimique* (molecule, ion, atom);
  - *corps pur* = pure substance;
  - *masse volumique* ρ (g·cm⁻³, kg·m⁻³) vs *densité* d (dimensionless relative density);
  - *concentration en masse* (g·L⁻¹, formerly "concentration massique") vs *concentration en quantité de matière* (mol·L⁻¹, formerly "concentration molaire"); "molarity" and "M" are **not** used;
  - *dosage* (any assay) vs *titrage* (assay by a reaction);
  - *quantité de matière* n (amount of substance);
  - *avancement* x and *tableau d'avancement* (the ICE-style table, in mol, not concentration), *avancement maximal* x_max, *taux d'avancement final* τ;
  - *réactif limitant*; *rendement* (yield);
  - *pile* (electrochemical cell or battery) vs *accumulateur* (rechargeable);
  - *pont salin* (salt bridge);
  - *pont hydrogène* (hydrogen bond);
  - *gaz nobles* (the current programmes use this, not *gaz rares*);
  - *classification / tableau périodique*.
- **Ions:**
  - *ion oxonium* H₃O⁺ in lycée (collège uses H⁺);
  - hydroxide is written **HO⁻** in lycée texts but OH⁻ in the collège text;
  - states are written as (s), (l), (g), (aq) from 1re;
  - couples are written **Ox/Red** (e.g. Cu²⁺/Cu) and acid/base (e.g. CH₃COOH/CH₃COO⁻).
- **Equations and constants:**
  - K and Qr are dimensionless, with c° = 1 mol·L⁻¹;
  - no ΔH notation; energies are "énergie molaire de réaction" or "énergie de liaison";
  - arrow conventions (→ for total, ⇄ for equilibrium) are the usual textbook practice (unverified in the programme text).
- **Numbers and units:**
  - decimal comma;
  - thin space as thousands separator;
  - SI units with a product dot or space (mol·L⁻¹, g·mol⁻¹, J·mol⁻¹);
  - *écriture scientifique* and *chiffres significatifs* are explicit skills;
  - uncertainty is written with *incertitude-type* u(x), standard uncertainty [S2].
- **Electron configuration:** subshell notation 1s² 2s² 2p⁶… for Z ≤ 18 from 2nde [S2]. No "2,8,8" shell notation is prescribed.
- **Brønsted:** spelled "Brönsted" in the official text.
