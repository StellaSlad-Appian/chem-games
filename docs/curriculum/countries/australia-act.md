# Australian Capital Territory chemistry curriculum (BSSS Chemistry T, Australian Curriculum 7–10): organic chemistry, kinetics, energetics, equilibrium

**Purpose:** the reference curriculum for the organic chemistry and energetics games planned in
[`../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md`](../../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md). The Australian states and
territories are compared in [`australia-overview.md`](../australia-overview.md).
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent, from primary sources).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches in the ACT.

Primary sources: the ACT Board of Senior Secondary Studies (BSSS) *Chemistry T* course document
("from 2023", read in full), the *BSSS Science Course Framework* (Board endorsed from 2024), the *ACT BSSS
Course Register 2026* (version 20.02.2026), and BSSS pages on qualifications, scaling, the ATAR and the ACT
Scaling Test. Full URL list at the end. Anything not confirmed from a primary source is marked
**(unverified)**.

## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **The ACT senior course is effectively the Australian Curriculum senior secondary Chemistry
  (ACARA Units 1–4).** Its content descriptions point to "the ACARA Australian Curriculum website" for
  elaboration, and the unit titles and dot points follow ACARA's text. A game tagged to the ACARA
  senior units therefore covers the ACT too.
- **There is no external exam and no data booklet.** Assessment is school-based and continuous, and the
  only common test is the ACT Scaling Test (AST), which is not a chemistry test. Games can serve as
  practice or formative tasks; there is no exam format to mimic. Colleges choose their own data values
  **(unverified in detail)**.
- **Energetics is thin.** The course has enthalpy, exo/endothermic reactions, thermochemical equations,
  energy profile diagrams and fuel comparisons (Unit 1–2). **Hess's law, bond-enthalpy calculations,
  enthalpies of formation, entropy and Gibbs free energy do not appear.** Hess and Gibbs games would be
  enrichment for the ACT.
- **Kinetics is qualitative and conceptual** (Unit 2): measuring rate from product formation or reactant
  loss, collision theory, activation energy, energy profiles, catalysts (enzymes, metal nanoparticles).
  There is no Maxwell–Boltzmann by name, no rate law and no Arrhenius equation.
- **Organic chemistry is framed as synthesis and analysis (Unit 4), not as a naming drill.**
  - Functional groups named: alcohols, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, amides.
  - Reaction classes: addition, condensation, oxidation.
  - Spectroscopy: IR, MS and X-ray crystallography, **but no NMR**.
  - Also covered: % yield, green chemistry, reaction pathways, polymers and biomolecules.
  - IUPAC naming is required only as "systematic nomenclature (using IUPAC conventions)", with no depth
    stated. Aldehydes, ketones, haloalkanes, isomer types and cis/trans are not named.
- **Units can be taught in any order**, and Year 11 and Year 12 students are graded against different
  achievement standards. A game cannot assume that Unit 1 energetics has been met before Unit 4 organic
  chemistry.

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 Units, hours and course patterns (Chemistry T, Appendix A)

| Unit (value 1.0 each; each can be split into two 0.5 units "a"/"b") | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Unit 1: Chemical Fundamentals** | Atomic structure, bonding, materials, **energy changes in reactions**, the mole |
| **Unit 2: Molecular Interactions and Reactions** ("Molecules") | Intermolecular forces, gases, aqueous solutions and acidity, **rates of reaction** |
| **Unit 3: Equilibrium, Acids and Redox Reactions** | **Chemical equilibrium**, acids and bases, redox, electrochemical cells |
| **Unit 4: Structure, Synthesis and Design** | **Organic structure, synthesis, analysis**, polymers, green chemistry |

- A standard 1.0 unit "is delivered over at least 55 hours", generally over one semester.
- **Course patterns:** a *Minor* needs at least 2 units; a *Major* needs at least 3.5 units.
- "Units in this course can be delivered in any order."
- **Prerequisites:** "Nil."
- "Teachers must deliver all content descriptions", but they may emphasise some over others and add
  content. Colleges write their own *program of learning*.
- **Mathematics:** assumes the F–10 Australian Curriculum: Mathematics. The course notes that logarithms
  for pH (Unit 3) may need to be taught.

### 1.2 Assessment: school-based and continuous

- There is **no external examination**. The course says students are "continually assessed throughout
  years 11 and 12, with both years contributing equally to senior secondary certification".
- **Assessment criteria:**
  - concepts, models and application
  - contexts
  - inquiry skills
- **Task rules:**
  - A 1.0 unit has **3 to 5 tasks**; a 0.5 unit has 2 to 3.
  - **No task may be weighted more than 45%** in a 1.0 unit.
  - Students must meet a variety of task types, both theoretical and practical.
  - A student-conceived investigation is recommended once in a minor and twice in a major.
- **Suggested task types include:** test/quiz, practical report, investigation, research report, response
  to stimulus, models, poster, essay, multimedia presentation, and others.
- **Grading:**
  - Each unit gets a **grade A–E** against the framework's achievement standards. There are separate
    **Year 11** and **Year 12** standards for T courses.
  - T units also get a numeric **unit score**, which feeds course scores (see 1.3).
  - Achievement standards "cannot be used as a rubric for an individual assessment task". Colleges write
    task rubrics.
- **Moderation:**
  - "structured, consensus-based peer review" of unit grades on Moderation Days, twice a year
  - statistical moderation of course scores via the AST

### 1.3 Certificate, AST and ATAR

- **ACT Senior Secondary Certificate (SSC):**
  - at least 17 standard units
  - at least four A/T/M/H/E courses from at least three course areas, including English
- **Tertiary Entrance Statement:**
  - at least 20 standard units
  - at least three majors and one minor classified T or H
  - the student must sit all components of the **AST**
- **ACT Scaling Test (AST):**
  - designed by ACER
  - measures "skills necessary for success at university", not subject knowledge
  - three papers:
    - short response, "19–25 questions", 2 h per the BSSS page (another BSSS source says 1 h 45 min)
    - multiple choice, 80 questions, 2 h 15 min
    - writing task, 600-word argumentative response, 2 h 30 min
  - held on the Tuesday and Wednesday of **week 6 of Term 3**, with a second sitting in week 2 of Term 4
- **Scaling:**
  - Colleges calculate each student's **course score** from their unit scores. The scaling page refers
    to "their best 80% unit scores".
  - Scores are compared within **scaling groups**.
  - **OCS (Other Course Score) scaling** adjusts course scores using the student's best four course
    scores and the AST. Scaling "does not alter the ranking of students".
  - The **aggregate** is "the best three scaled scores from major courses, and 0.6 of the next best
    course score (either major or minor)".
  - The aggregate is converted to an ATAR (the BSSS page mentions using NSW cohort data for the
    conversion).

## 2. Content by year / unit

Year 11 and Year 12 are not fixed to particular units, because units may be taught in any order. The
most common sequence is Units 1–2 in Year 11 and Units 3–4 in Year 12, as in ACARA's design
**(unverified for ACT colleges)**.

### 2.1 Years 7–10 (Australian Curriculum: Science)

- ACT schools use the Australian Curriculum F–10 (see section 0).
- The Version 9.0 Science content descriptions for chemical sciences in Years 9–10 could not be fetched;
  the ACARA page loads dynamically **(unverified)**.
- **Expected coverage from Version 9.0 (unverified):**
  - Year 9: reactions, including energy transfer and combustion
  - Year 10: the periodic table, types of chemical reactions and factors affecting reaction rates
  - Organic chemistry beyond simple hydrocarbons is not expected before Year 11.
- Cross-check against the national/AC section of [`australia-overview.md`](../australia-overview.md).

### 2.2 Unit 1: Chemical Fundamentals (the energetics unit)

**Unit goal:** "understand the concept of enthalpy, and apply this to qualitatively and quantitatively
describe and explain energy changes in chemical reactions".

**Properties and structure of atoms:**
- periodic trends, electron shells and energy levels
- flame tests and AAS, isotopes
- mass spectrometry for isotopic composition, relative atomic mass

**Properties and structure of materials:**
- pure substances vs mixtures, separation
- bonding: ionic, metallic, covalent molecular and network
- nanomaterials; carbon allotropes
- "carbon forms hydrocarbon compounds, including alkanes and alkenes, with different chemical properties
  that are influenced by the nature of the bonding within the molecules". This is **the only organic dot
  point before Unit 4**.

**Chemical reactions: reactants, products and energy change:**
- "all chemical reactions involve the creation of new substances and associated energy transformations"
- "endothermic and exothermic reactions can be explained in terms of the Law of Conservation of Energy
  and the breaking and reforming of bonds; heat energy released or absorbed can be represented in
  thermochemical equations"
- "fuels, including fossil fuels and biofuels, can be compared in terms of their energy output,
  suitability for purpose, and the nature of products of combustion"
- the mole concept and conservation of mass

**Inquiry skills:**
- "use of devices to accurately measure temperature change and mass". This implies calorimetry, although
  the word is not used.
- representations: "chemical equations and thermochemical equations"
- calculations: percentage composition and mole calculations

### 2.3 Unit 2: Molecular Interactions and Reactions (the kinetics unit)

**Intermolecular forces and gases:**
- properties (vapour pressure, mp, bp, solubility) from intermolecular forces
- VSEPR and polarity
- dispersion forces, dipole–dipole forces, hydrogen bonding
- **chromatography** (TLC, GC, HPLC) for composition and purity
- kinetic theory of gases, qualitatively

**Aqueous solutions and acidity:**
- properties of water; concentration in mol L⁻¹ and g L⁻¹
- ion tests
- solubility and temperature
- the pH scale; patterns in acid reactions

**Rates of chemical reactions:**
- "varying the conditions present during chemical reactions can affect the rate of the reaction and in
  some cases the identity of the products"
- "the rate of chemical reactions can be quantified by measuring the rate of formation of products or
  the depletion of reactants"
- "collision theory can be used to explain and predict the effect of concentration, temperature,
  pressure and surface area on the rate … by considering the structure of the reactants and the energy
  of particles"
- "the activation energy is the minimum energy required for a chemical reaction to occur and is related
  to the strength of the existing chemical bonds"
- "energy profile diagrams can be used to represent the enthalpy changes and activation energy"
- "catalysts, including enzymes and metal nanoparticles, affect the rate … by providing an alternative
  reaction pathway with a reduced activation energy, hence increasing the proportion of collisions that
  lead to a chemical change"

**Inquiry skills:**
- measuring pH and "the rate of formation of products"
- representations include "energy profile diagrams"
- mole calculations including gas volume at STP; n = cV

### 2.4 Unit 3: Equilibrium, Acids and Redox Reactions

**Chemical equilibrium systems:**
- open vs closed systems
- physical changes are reversible, only some chemical reactions are
- dynamic equilibrium in a closed system; "position of equilibrium"
- "the reversibility of chemical reactions can be explained by considering the activation energies of the
  forward and reverse reactions"
- the effect of temperature is explained through "the enthalpy changes for the forward and reverse
  reactions"
- the effects of concentration and pressure are explained through collision theory
- Le Chatelier's principle
- "equilibrium position can be predicted qualitatively using equilibrium constants"
- acids: monoprotic and polyprotic; strength via degree of ionisation and **Ka**; Brønsted–Lowry
- pH, **Kw**, indicators, volumetric analysis

**Oxidation and reduction:**
- redox as electron transfer; half-equations
- standard electrode potentials
- galvanic and electrolytic cells, fuel cells (metal nanoparticle catalysts), cell potentials

**Inquiry skills:**
- representations: "equilibrium constants and expressions", pH, oxidation numbers, cell diagrams
- calculations: cell potentials, volumetric analysis, "determining the yield of incomplete reactions",
  pH of strong acids and bases

**Not listed in Unit 3:** Ksp, numerical calculation of K from equilibrium concentrations (only
"qualitatively using equilibrium constants"), and a named industrial process such as Haber.

### 2.5 Unit 4: Structure, Synthesis and Design (the organic unit)

**Unit goals:**
- how functional groups and molecular structure relate to properties
- "understand addition, condensation and oxidation reactions, and predict the products of these
  reactions"
- how synthesis processes are designed and how analytical data give structural information

**Properties and structure of organic materials:**
- "organic molecules have a hydrocarbon skeleton and can contain functional groups, including alcohols,
  carboxylic acids, esters, amines and amides"
- "each class of organic compounds displays characteristic chemical properties and undergoes specific
  reactions based on the functional groups present; these reactions, including acid-base and oxidation
  reactions, can be used to identify the class of the organic compound"
- proteins, carbohydrates and synthetic polymers: strength, density and biodegradability explained by
  "primary, secondary or tertiary structures"
- "data from analytical techniques, including mass spectrometry, x-ray crystallography and infrared
  spectroscopy, can be used to determine the structure of organic molecules, often using evidence from
  more than one technique"

**Chemical synthesis and design:**
- choice of reagents for a product (pharmaceuticals, fuels, cosmetics, cleaning products)
- "constructing reaction pathways that may include more than one chemical reaction"
- choosing reagents and conditions "to maximise yield and purity"
- yield from stoichiometric vs actual quantities
- **green chemistry:** renewable feedstocks, fewer harmful solvents, minimising unwanted products
- "organic molecules, including polymers, can be synthesised using addition and condensation reactions"
- fuels (biodiesel, ethanol, hydrogen) "synthesised … using a range of chemical reactions including
  addition, oxidation and esterification"
- molecular manufacturing and nanotechnology

**Inquiry skills:**
- representations: "structural formulas, chemical equations, systematic nomenclature (using IUPAC
  conventions) and spectra"; models of primary, secondary and tertiary structures
- calculations: "multi-step reactions, and the percentage yield of synthesis reactions"
- "organic synthesis methods"

**Not listed in Unit 4:** homologous series by name, branched-alkane naming rules, aldehydes, ketones,
haloalkanes, isomer types, geometric or optical isomerism, substitution reactions, named test reagents,
and NMR.

## 4. Conventions and notation

- **Nomenclature:** the course requires only "systematic nomenclature (using IUPAC conventions)". **No
  style (propan-2-ol vs 2-propanol) is specified** and there is no common exam to show practice. The
  current IUPAC style (propan-2-ol, ethyl ethanoate) is recommended for consistency with NSW and the
  other states **(unverified for ACT classrooms)**.
- **Formula types named:** chemical formulae, "molecular structural formulae", "structural formulas",
  ionic formulae, electron dot diagrams, electron shell diagrams, thermochemical equations, half-equations,
  cell diagrams. Skeletal and condensed formulas are not named.
- **Units as written in the course** (lower case from the PDF extraction): "mol l-1", "g l-1", "ph".
  Standard practice is mol L⁻¹ and pH. Energy units (kJ mol⁻¹) are not specified in the course text
  **(unverified)**.
- **Spelling:** Australian: *ionisation*, *analyse*, *sulfate*-type spellings follow ACARA **(the words
  "sulfur" and "aluminium" do not appear in the course text)**.
- **Named reagents and tests:** none for organic chemistry. Unit 2 names ion identification by
  precipitation and acid–base reactions; Unit 1 names flame tests and AAS.
- **Data booklet:** none; there is no external exam. The AST is general reasoning, not chemistry.
  Colleges supply their own data in tests **(unverified)**.
- **Brønsted–Lowry** and **Le Chatelier's principle** are named in the course, as is VSEPR ("valence
  shell electron pair repulsion (vsepr) theory").

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

The Chemistry T course has its own **Appendix D – Glossary of Verbs** (standard across BSSS courses), plus
**Appendix C – Common Curriculum Elements**, which groups verbs by thinking type ("create, compose and
apply"; "analyse, synthesise and evaluate"; "organise, sequence and explain"; "identify, summarise and
plan"). Definitions below are paraphrased.

| Verb | Meaning (paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| analyse | consider in detail to find meaning or relationships, patterns, similarities and differences |
| apply | use in a particular situation |
| argue | give reasons for or against |
| assess | judge the value of |
| classify | sort into named categories |
| compare / contrast | note similarities and differences / emphasise differences |
| critically analyse | analysis that engages with criticism and existing debate |
| demonstrate | show by practical exhibition or explanation |
| describe | give characteristics or features |
| discuss | treat a topic taking different issues or ideas into account |
| evaluate | judge merit or significance |
| examine | determine the nature or condition of |
| explain | give information showing understanding of reasoning and/or application |
| extrapolate | infer from what is known |
| hypothesise | put forward a testable supposition |
| identify | recognise and name |
| interpret | draw meaning from |
| investigate | plan, inquire into and draw conclusions |
| justify | show how an argument or conclusion is right or reasonable |
| predict | suggest what might happen as a consequence |
| represent | convey meaning with words, images, symbols or signs |
| sequence | arrange in order |
| summarise | give a brief statement of the main points |
| synthesise | combine elements into a coherent whole |

The glossary also defines *compose*, *create*, *manipulate*, *plan*, *reflect*, *relate*, *reproduce*,
*respond*, *select*, *test*, *translate* and *visualise*. The achievement standards grade by verb level.
For example, an A-grade Year 11 T student "evaluates" where a C-grade student "explains" and an E-grade
student "identifies".
