# New South Wales chemistry curriculum (NESA): Science 7–10 and Stage 6 Chemistry (HSC), organic chemistry, kinetics, energetics, equilibrium

**Purpose:** the reference curriculum for the organic chemistry and energetics games planned in
[`../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md`](../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md). The Australian states and
territories are compared in [`australia-overview.md`](./australia-overview.md).
**Research date:** 24 Sept 2026 (desk research by an agent, from primary sources).
**Review status:** not yet checked by a teacher who teaches in NSW.

Primary sources: the NESA *Chemistry Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)* (Word version from nsw.gov.au, read in
full), the NESA Chemistry assessment and reporting page, the 2019 HSC Chemistry formulae sheet / data
sheet / periodic table (read as images), the 2025 HSC Chemistry exam paper, the NESA glossary of key
words, the NSW Curriculum site (curriculum.nsw.edu.au) for the *Science 7–10 Syllabus (2023)* and the
*Chemistry 11–12 Syllabus (2025)*, the *Science 7–10 Syllabus (2018)* Word file, and NSW Department of
Education implementation pages. Full URL list at the end. Anything not confirmed from a primary source is
marked **(unverified)**.

## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **In 2026 and 2027 the 2017 Stage 6 syllabus is the one that counts.** The new *Chemistry 11–12
  Syllabus (2025)* starts with Year 11 in Term 1 2028, and the first HSC under it is in 2029. Build
  NSW-tagged games against the 2017 modules (Module 1–8), but keep the tags flexible: the 2025 syllabus
  regroups everything into seven "focus areas".
- **Energetics is a Year 11 topic in NSW, and it is unusually deep.** Module 4 *Drivers of Reactions*
  has calorimetry, Hess's law with bond energy data, entropy, and **quantitative Gibbs free energy
  (ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°, printed on the formulae sheet)**. **The 2025 syllabus drops Hess's law, entropy and
  Gibbs entirely** (checked against the full list of content points). A Gibbs or Hess game is NSW-relevant
  only for HSC cohorts up to 2028.
- **Kinetics is qualitative and short.** Module 3 covers the factors affecting rate, collision theory,
  activation energy and orientation. There is no Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, no rate law, no order
  and no Arrhenius equation in either syllabus. Energy profile diagrams and catalysts sit in Module 4.
- **Organic chemistry is Year 12 Module 7, with analysis in Module 8.** Naming goes "up to C8" with
  "simple methyl and ethyl branched chains". It covers structural isomers (chain, position, functional
  group) but **not geometric isomers** (these arrive only in the 2025 syllabus). There is no chirality.
  Spectroscopy (IR, MS, ¹H and ¹³C NMR) is core, and the data sheet gives IR and ¹³C tables, so a
  spectroscopy game fits NSW well.
- **Use the exam's naming style:** locants before the suffix (*propan-1-ol*), esters as *ethyl ethanoate*
  or *propyl butanoate*, *ethanoic acid*, *sulfate*, *kJ mol⁻¹*. The data sheet gives the specific heat
  of water as **4.18 × 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**, so mass in q = mcΔT is in kg. The data sheet has **no bond
  energies and no enthalpies of formation or combustion**. Games must supply those values in the question,
  as the HSC does.
- **Stage 5 (Years 9–10) has only a light organic introduction.** The 2023 syllabus covers crude-oil
  fractions, IUPAC names of simple compounds (ethane, ethanol, ethanoic acid), straight-chain alkanes
  C1–C8, and complete vs incomplete combustion. Rate factors are also Stage 5. The 2023 syllabus is being
  phased in, so in 2026 Years 7 and 9 use it while Years 8 and 10 are still on the 2018 syllabus.
- **Question wording should use NESA's glossary verbs** (*explain*, *analyse*, *assess*, *evaluate*,
  *calculate*, *deduce*, *justify*). HSC long responses of 7–9 marks reward integrated explanations.
  This favours "explain why" follow-ups over pure recall.

## 0. What applies in 2026

NSW's school year is the calendar year. The HSC (Year 12) course traditionally **starts in Term 4 of
Year 11**, so "Year 12 content" is already being taught from October of the Year 11 calendar year. This
is visible in the 2025 syllabus timeline below.

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 Stage 6 Chemistry (2017): course structure

| Year | Modules | Indicative hours |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 11** (120 h) | **M1** Properties and Structure of Matter; **M2** Introduction to Quantitative Chemistry; **M3** Reactive Chemistry; **M4** Drivers of Reactions | 60 h for M1–2, 60 h for M3–4. **15 h depth study** within the 120 h. |
| **Year 12** (120 h) | **M5** Equilibrium and Acid Reactions; **M6** Acid/Base Reactions; **M7** Organic Chemistry; **M8** Applying Chemical Ideas | 60 h for M5–6, 60 h for M7–8. **15 h depth study** within the 120 h. |

- Each year has **at least 35 h of practical investigations**, including those done inside depth
  studies.
- **Working Scientifically** outcomes CH11/12-1 to -7 run across all modules: questioning and
  predicting, planning, conducting, processing, analysing, problem solving, communicating.
- The knowledge outcomes relevant here are:
  - **CH11-10:** reaction types, reactivity of metals, "the factors that affect the rate of chemical
    reactions"
  - **CH11-11:** "analyses the energy considerations in the driving force for chemical reactions"
  - **CH12-12:** equilibrium
  - **CH12-14:** "analyses the structure of, and predicts reactions involving, carbon compounds"
  - **CH12-15:** chemical systems used to design and analyse chemical processes
- The syllabus says Stage 6 includes the Australian Curriculum senior secondary content (ACSCH codes
  are cited next to many dot points) plus "additional descriptions that clarify the scope and depth".
- **Prerequisites:** none stated in the syllabus. Schools typically expect Stage 5 Science
  **(unverified)**.

### 1.2 Assessment

**School-based assessment (NESA Chemistry assessment and reporting page):**
- **Year 11:** mandatory components are skills in working scientifically (60%) and knowledge and
  understanding (40%). NESA recommends **3 tasks**, including a formal written exam and a depth study
  (or part of one). Each task is weighted 20–40%.
- **Year 12:** same 60/40 components. NESA recommends **4 tasks**, each weighted 10–40%.
  - The formal written exam is capped at **30%**.
  - The depth study is weighted **20–40%**.
- **Depth studies** must assess *Questioning and Predicting* and *Communicating*, plus at least two
  other Working Scientifically skills and at least one knowledge outcome.

**HSC examination:**
- One written paper, **100 marks**, **3 hours plus 5 minutes reading time**.
  - **Section I:** 20 marks of objective-response (multiple-choice) questions, about 35 min.
  - **Section II:** 80 marks, "20 to 25 items", with "at least two items worth 7 to 9 marks", about
    2 h 25 min. The 2025 paper had Questions 21–37.
- Coverage: "approximately equal weighting given to Modules 5 to 8", and "The Year 11 course is assumed
  knowledge for the Year 12 course". So Module 4 energetics can appear. The 2025 paper had a
  multiple-choice question on the signs of ΔH and ΔS for octane combustion, and an enthalpy profile with
  Ea1/Ea2 in Q35.
- **Provided:** "A data sheet, formulae sheet and Periodic Table" (see section 4.3). NESA-approved
  calculators are allowed.

**HSC mark and ATAR:**
- The HSC mark is **50% exam mark + 50% moderated school assessment mark**. NESA moderates school marks
  against the exam using the group's mean, top mark and (where possible) bottom mark.
- Results are reported in **Bands 1–6**.
- The ATAR is calculated by UAC, not NESA **(unverified here; not fetched)**.

## 2. Content by year / unit

### 2.1 Stage 4 (Years 7–8), 2023 syllabus: energy seeds only

- **Change → Chemical change:**
  - identify signs of a chemical change; one listed sign is that "the temperature changes"
  - "Investigate and observe energy changes in different chemical reactions"
  - write word equations (example: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide)
- **Change → Energy transfers:**
  - "Define open and closed systems" for energy transfer
  - the law of conservation of energy
  - chemical energy as a stored form of energy
- The Stage 4 pages fetched do not use the words *exothermic* or *endothermic*.

### 2.2 Stage 5 (Years 9–10), 2023 syllabus (Years 9 in 2026; Years 9–10 from 2027)

**Materials → Chemistry of organic compounds** (verbatim dot points):
- "Distinguish between organic and inorganic compounds"
- "Describe how hydrocarbons can be separated from crude oil and identify the uses of these products".
  The example given is fractional distillation.
- "Use International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) nomenclature to name simple organic
  compounds". The example given is "ethane, ethanol and ethanoic acid".
- "Identify and reproduce the structure of simple alkanes C1–C8". The example text defines them as
  "straight-chained, non-branched hydrocarbons with single bonds only".
- "Describe the differences between complete and incomplete combustion reactions of hydrocarbons …" and
  compare "the products of the chemical reaction and amount of energy released". The example is a Bunsen
  burner with the collar open and then closed.
- "Research the uses of hydrocarbon compounds and how this has changed over time"

**Materials → other content groups:**
- **Bonding:** valency, noble gas configuration, ionic/covalent/metallic bonds, diatomic elements, and
  formulas such as NaCl, MgO, H₂O and CO₂.
- **Polymers:** raw materials (polyethylene from ethene, LDPE/HDPE), properties, local polymer survey,
  biodegradability, microplastic bioaccumulation.
- **Materials in context:** alternatives to crude-oil materials.

**Reactions** (SC5-RXN):
- **Law of conservation of mass:** meaning, a practical demonstration, and closed systems.
- **Chemical reactions:**
  - IUPAC naming conventions for the formulas of ionic and covalent compounds
  - "writing word and balanced chemical equations with states", using the Data Book's activity series
    and solubility table
  - synthesis, decomposition, displacement and neutralisation reactions
  - the pH of common substances (vinegar is glossed as "ethanoic acid"); indicators
- **Rate of chemical reactions:**
  - "Investigate and explain how concentration, surface area, temperature and catalysts affect the rate
    of reactions". The example uses effervescent tablets and notes that catalysts "could include
    enzymes".
  - a hypothesis-driven practical, with the results graphed
- **Nuclear reactions:** Big Bang element formation, alpha/beta decay, half-life, fission and fusion,
  radioisotopes.
- **Reactions in context:** an industrial reaction, e.g. polymers, aspirin, sulfuric acid, ammonia.

**Not in the Stage 5 (2023) pages fetched:** collision theory, activation energy, *exothermic/endothermic*
as named terms, functional groups other than those in the three example names, isomers.

- **Stage 5 Energy** is about physics (energy transfer, circuits, energy sources). It has no chemical
  energetics.

### 2.3 Stage 5 (Years 9–10), 2018 syllabus (Year 10 in 2026 only)

- **Chemical World, CW3:**
  - combustion, acid reactions, corrosion, precipitation, neutralisation and decomposition, "and involve
    energy transfer"
  - word equations
  - conservation of atoms
- **Chemical World, CW4:**
  - "identify that chemical reactions involve energy transfer and can be exothermic or endothermic"
  - comparing combustion and respiration rates
  - "describe the effects of factors, eg temperature and catalysts, on the rate of some common chemical
    reactions"
- **Additional (optional) content:** balancing equations, activity series, flame tests.
- **No organic chemistry** in the 2018 Stage 5 Chemical World content.

### 2.4 Year 11 (2017 syllabus)

**Module 1: Properties and Structure of Matter**
- separation techniques, % composition
- "nomenclature of inorganic substances using … IUPAC naming conventions"
- isotopes, spdf configurations, flame tests, the Bohr and Schrödinger models, radioisotopes
- periodicity: atomic radius, first ionisation energy, electronegativity
- **bonding:** electronegativity and bond polarity, Lewis dot diagrams, molecular shapes, allotropes,
  network, molecular and metallic structures
- "the nature of intermolecular and intramolecular bonds" used to explain physical properties

**Module 2: Introduction to Quantitative Chemistry**
- conservation of mass, balancing equations
- the mole: n = m/MM, empirical formulae, limiting reagents
- c = n/V, dilutions, standard solutions
- the gas laws and PV = nRT

**Module 3: Reactive Chemistry.** This is where kinetics sits.
- **Chemical Reactions:**
  - indicators of chemical change
  - predicting products of synthesis, decomposition, combustion, precipitation, acid/base and
    acid/carbonate reactions
  - Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander detoxification of food
  - balanced equations
- **Predicting Reactions of Metals:**
  - activity series; oxidation numbers, half-equations
  - galvanic half-cells, the standard reduction potential table
  - "predict the spontaneity of redox reactions using the value of cell potentials"
- **Rates of Reactions**, inquiry question "What affects the rate of a chemical reaction?":
  - a practical to "collect data, analyse and report on how the rate … can be affected by" temperature,
    surface area, concentration and catalysts
  - "investigate the role of activation energy, collisions and molecular orientation in collision theory"
  - "explain a change in reaction rate using collision theory"

**Module 4: Drivers of Reactions.** All of the energetics.
- **Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions:**
  - practicals on endothermic and exothermic reactions, including combustion and dissolving ionic
    substances
  - "calorimetry and q = mcΔT", comparing results with secondary data and explaining differences
  - "construct energy profile diagrams to represent and analyse the enthalpy changes and activation
    energy"
  - "model and analyse the role of catalysts in reactions"
- **Enthalpy and Hess's Law**, inquiry question "How much energy does it take to break bonds, and how
  much is released when bonds are formed?":
  - enthalpy change from bond breaking and forming, and the law of conservation of energy
  - "investigate Hess's Law in quantifying the enthalpy change for a stepped reaction using standard
    enthalpy change data and bond energy data"; the example is C → CO → CO₂
  - "apply Hess's Law to simple energy cycles", including heat of combustion, photosynthesis and
    respiration
- **Entropy and Gibbs Free Energy:**
  - differences between entropy and enthalpy; modelling entropy changes
  - "predict entropy changes from balanced chemical reactions to classify as increasing or decreasing
    entropy"
  - spontaneity using Gibbs free energy, enthalpy and entropy
  - "solve problems using standard references and ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS° … to classify reactions as
    spontaneous or nonspontaneous"
  - "predict the effect of temperature changes on spontaneity"
- The syllabus glossary defines **enthalpy** as "the total heat content of a system" and **entropy** as
  "the degree of disorder or randomness in the system". A disorder-based entropy explanation is therefore
  the NSW-expected level.

### 2.5 Year 12 (2017 syllabus)

**Module 5: Equilibrium and Acid Reactions**
- **Static and Dynamic Equilibrium:**
  - reversibility practicals: CoCl₂ hydration, Fe³⁺/SCN⁻, burning Mg and steel wool
  - "model static and dynamic equilibrium and analyse the differences between open and closed systems"
  - "analyse examples of non-equilibrium systems in terms of the effect of entropy and enthalpy"
    (combustion, photosynthesis)
  - collision theory and rate applied to equilibrium
- **Factors that Affect Equilibrium:**
  - Le Chatelier (temperature, concentration, volume/pressure); examples are CoCl₂, NO₂/N₂O₄ and
    Fe(SCN)²⁺
  - explanation via collision theory
  - "examine how activation energy and heat of reaction affect the position of equilibrium"
- **Calculating the Equilibrium Constant (Keq):**
  - Keq expressions "for homogeneous reactions occurring in solution"
  - calculating Keq and concentrations and predicting direction
  - "qualitatively analyse the effect of temperature on the value of Keq"
  - Keq for the Fe/SCN system; Keq for dissociation of ionic solutions and of acids/bases
- **Solution Equilibria:**
  - dissolution; solubility rules
  - Ksp expressions and solubility from Ksp, predicting precipitation
  - Aboriginal detoxification of cycad fruit

**Module 6: Acid/Base Reactions.** Titles only, except:
- the practical to "measure the enthalpy of neutralisation" (a calorimetry context)
- Arrhenius vs Brønsted–Lowry
- pH/pOH, titration curves, Ka/pKa, buffers

**Module 7: Organic Chemistry.** The core organic module (outcome CH12-14).

- **Nomenclature:**
  - "investigate the nomenclature of organic chemicals, up to C8, using IUPAC conventions, including
    simple methyl and ethyl branched chains", for:
    - alkanes, alkenes, alkynes
    - alcohols (primary, secondary and tertiary)
    - aldehydes and ketones
    - carboxylic acids
    - amines and amides
    - halogenated organic compounds
  - "explore and distinguish the different types of structural isomers … including: chain isomers,
    position isomers, functional group isomers"
  - Esters are not in the nomenclature list, but the 2025 HSC paper names them (*propyl butanoate*,
    *ethyl ethanoate*).
- **Hydrocarbons:**
  - "construct models, identify the functional group, and write structural and molecular formulae for
    homologous series … up to C8" (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes)
  - properties within a homologous series explained by bonding
  - molecular shape around single, double and triple C–C bonds
  - alkane properties from intermolecular and intramolecular bonding
  - safe handling and disposal; environmental, economic and sociocultural implications of hydrocarbons
- **Products of Reactions Involving Hydrocarbons:**
  - unsaturated hydrocarbons "when added to" H₂, X₂, HX and H₂O
  - "reactions of saturated hydrocarbons when substituted with halogens"
- **Alcohols:**
  - structural formulae and properties of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols
  - intermolecular-force explanations
  - "measure and reliably compare the enthalpy of combustion for a range of alcohols"
  - "write equations, state conditions and predict products" for combustion, dehydration, substitution
    with HX and oxidation
  - production via haloalkane substitution and fermentation
  - "investigate the products of the oxidation of primary and secondary alcohols"
  - biofuels, including ethanol
- **Reactions of Organic Acids and Bases:**
  - structure and properties of alcohols, aldehydes/ketones, amines/amides and carboxylic acids
  - boiling point and solubility of acids, amines and amides from intermolecular forces
  - "the production, in a school laboratory, of simple esters"
  - organic acid vs organic base
  - soaps and detergents
  - "draft and construct flow charts to show reaction pathways for chemical synthesis, including those
    that involve more than one step"
- **Polymers:**
  - addition polymers "of ethylene and related monomers": PE, PVC, PS, PTFE
  - condensation polymers: nylon, polyesters
- The content focus also mentions proteins and carbohydrates (primary, secondary and tertiary structure),
  but there is **no biomolecule dot point**.

**Module 8: Applying Chemical Ideas**
- **Analysis of Inorganic Substances:**
  - cation and anion tests
  - gravimetric analysis, precipitation titrations
  - colourimetry, UV–vis, AAS
- **Analysis of Organic Substances:**
  - "conduct qualitative investigations to test for the presence in organic molecules of the following
    functional groups: carbon–carbon double bonds, hydroxyl groups, carboxylic acids". No reagents are
    named.
  - "proton and carbon-13 NMR, mass spectrometry, infrared spectroscopy"
- **Chemical Synthesis and Design:**
  - availability of reagents
  - reaction conditions
  - **yield and purity**
  - industrial uses
  - environmental, social and economic issues
  - The 2025 HSC asked for a **percentage yield** of propyl butanoate.

### 2.6 Chemistry 11–12 Syllabus (2025): what changes from 2028 (for forward planning)

Checked against the complete list of content points on the NESA pages for all seven focus areas.

**Year 11, Chemical reactions** has three content groups: *Classifying chemical reactions*, *Rates of
reactions*, *Energy changes*.
- **Rates:**
  - "Analyse how concentration, temperature, surface area and catalysts affect reaction rate using
    collision theory"
  - "Explain the roles of activation energy and molecular orientation, and the frequency of successful
    collisions"
  - "Analyse the effect of catalysts on activation energy … and provide an alternative lower-energy
    pathway"
  - a secondary-source task on the economics of an industrial catalyst
- **Energy changes:**
  - endothermic and exothermic reactions
  - "Relate heat energy changes to total bond energy changes and to the law of conservation of energy"
  - "Analyse endothermic and exothermic energy profile diagrams in terms of chemical potential energy,
    and bond breaking and formation"
  - q = mcΔT
  - "Solve problems involving enthalpy changes by using ΔH = −q/n"
  - calorimetry to calculate ΔH
- **Not present anywhere in the 2025 syllabus:** Hess's law, entropy, Gibbs free energy.
- Open and closed systems move to *Quantitative chemistry*.

**Year 12, Equilibrium:**
- Keq expressions for **homogeneous solution and gas systems**
- Keq, 1/Keq and **reaction quotient Q**
- Le Chatelier plus collision theory
- Ksp in g L⁻¹ and mol L⁻¹
- "manipulation of reaction conditions to ensure economical production of an industrial chemical"
- "Justify methods … used to increase yield"

**Year 12, Organic chemistry** has four content groups: Hydrocarbons, Alcohols, Organic acids, Esters.
- IUPAC names for straight and branched aliphatic hydrocarbons, alcohols, haloalkanes, organic acids and
  esters, all C1–C8
- **explicit formula types:** "molecular formulas, condensed structural formulas, expanded structural
  formulas and skeletal structures"
- chain isomers; **"position isomers and geometric isomers of alkenes and alkynes"**; functional group
  isomers of carboxylic acids and esters
- addition and substitution reactions
- alcohol dehydration and HX substitution
- "Account for aldehyde and ketone products in the oxidation of alcohols"
- esterification and purification
- flow charts for ester synthesis
- stoichiometry on organic reactions
- enthalpy of combustion vs chain length
- **Amines and amides are no longer named.** Aldehydes and ketones appear only as oxidation products.

**Year 12, Applying chemical ideas:**
- IR, MS, ¹H NMR (splitting patterns, peak areas, chemical shift) and ¹³C NMR
- petroleum refining
- the polymers PE, PVC, PTFE and polypropylene; ethene → chloroethene flow chart

## 4. Conventions and notation

### 4.1 Naming (verified from NESA documents)

- **Locant before the suffix it refers to:** *propan-1-ol* (2025 HSC Q25). No "1-propanol" form appeared
  in the 2025 paper.
- **Esters named alkyl alkanoate:** *ethyl ethanoate*, *propyl butanoate* (2025 HSC). The acid is
  *ethanoic acid*; the paper used substituted names such as *chloroethanoic acid* and
  *3-hydroxypropanoic acid*.
- **Trivial names still appear in places:**
  - 2017 syllabus: "addition polymers of ethylene", "polyethylene (PE)", "polyvinyl chloride (PVC)",
    "polystyrene (PS)", "polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE)", "acetate (CH₃COO⁻)"
  - 2025 syllabus: *ethene*, *chloroethene*, *polytetrafluoroethylene*
  - Stage 5 (2023) glosses vinegar as "ethanoic acid"
  - Games should use the systematic name first and allow common polymer names.
- Inorganic names use **Stock notation:** *cobalt(II) chloride*, *iron(III) thiocyanate*, *lead(II)
  iodide*.
- **Australian spelling in NESA text:** *sulfate*, *sulfur*, *ionisation*, *colourimetry*,
  *aluminium* (data-sheet periodic table), *analyse*.
- The syllabus uses the phrases "halogenated organic compounds" (2017) and **haloalkanes** (2025).
- **Isomer vocabulary:** chain, position, functional group isomers. "Geometric isomers" only from 2025,
  and the syllabus does not say cis/trans or E/Z **(unverified which one exams use)**.

### 4.2 Units and sign conventions

- ΔH in **kJ mol⁻¹**, with sign (e.g. "ΔH = −57.2 kJ mol⁻¹" and "ΔH = +52 kJ mol⁻¹" in the 2025 paper).
  Standard-state symbols use ° (ΔG°, ΔH°, ΔS°).
- **Formulae sheet (as printed):** n = m/MM; c = n/V; PV = nRT; q = mcΔT; ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°;
  pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; pKa = −log₁₀[Ka]; A = εlc = log₁₀(I₀/I).
- **Constants on the sheet:**
  - N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
  - molar gas volume at 100 kPa: 22.71 L at 0 °C (273.15 K) and 24.79 L at 25 °C (298.15 K)
  - R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
  - Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C
  - **specific heat capacity of water 4.18 × 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**
- Molar mass is written **MM** (g mol⁻¹) in the syllabus and on the formulae sheet.

### 4.3 HSC data sheet contents relevant to games

The file is named "hsc-exams-2019"; it is the current link on NESA's 2017-syllabus page. The 2025 paper
says a formulae sheet, data sheet and periodic table are provided.

| Table | What it contains | Game relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Solubility constants at 25 °C | Ksp for about 34 carbonates, hydroxides, phosphates, sulfates and halides of Ba, Ca, Cu(II), Fe(II/III), Pb(II), Mg, Ag | Equilibrium |
| **Infrared absorption data** | N–H (amines) 3300–3500; O–H (alcohols) 3230–3550 broad; C–H 2850–3300; O–H (acids) 2500–3000 very broad; C≡N 2220–2260; **C=O 1680–1750**; C=C 1620–1680; C–O 1000–1300; C–C 750–1100 (cm⁻¹) | Spectroscopy / functional-group ID |

- **Not on the data sheet:** bond energies, standard enthalpies of formation or combustion, entropies,
  a ¹H NMR table, K values other than Ksp. Ka values are given in questions.
- In the 2025 paper, "¹H NMR chemical shift data" was printed inside Q36. Games on Hess, bond energies
  or ¹H NMR must therefore supply their own data tables, as the exam does.

### 4.4 Named reagents and tests

- The 2017 syllabus names **no organic test reagents** (only the functional groups to be tested). Common
  NSW classroom practice is **bromine water** (C=C), **acidified potassium dichromate or permanganate**
  (oxidation / 1°–2° alcohols), and **sodium hydrogen carbonate or carbonate** (acid, effervescence)
  **(unverified from primary source)**.
- Reaction conditions are expected ("state conditions" for alcohol reactions). The typical ones taught
  are concentrated H₂SO₄ for dehydration and esterification, reflux, and Pt/Ni catalysts for
  hydrogenation **(unverified from primary source)**.

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

NESA's **Glossary of key words** is used across all HSC exams. Its *apply* and *critically
analyse/evaluate* entries were updated in April 2025 (effective immediately). The definitions below are
paraphrased.

| Key word | Meaning (paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| account / account for | give reasons for, report on / narrate a sequence of events |
| analyse | identify parts and how they relate; draw out and relate implications |
| apply | use in a different, new or unfamiliar situation |
| assess | judge value, quality, outcomes, results or size |
| calculate | work out from given facts, figures or information |
| classify | arrange into classes or categories |
| compare / contrast | show similarities and differences / show differences |
| construct | make, build, put together items or arguments |
| critically analyse / evaluate | analysis or evaluation with added depth, logic, questioning and reflection |
| deduce | draw conclusions |
| define | state meaning and essential qualities |
| demonstrate | show by example |
| describe | give characteristics and features |
| discuss | identify issues and give points for and/or against |
| distinguish | note differences between |
| evaluate | judge on criteria; determine the value of |
| examine | inquire into |
| explain | relate cause and effect; say how and/or why |
| extract / extrapolate | choose relevant details / infer from what is known |
| identify | recognise and name |
| interpret | draw meaning from |
| investigate | plan, inquire into and draw conclusions |
| justify | support an argument or conclusion |
| outline | give the main features in general terms |
