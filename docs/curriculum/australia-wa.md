**Purpose:** the reference curriculum for the organic chemistry and energetics games planned in
[`../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md`](../ORGANIC_GAMES_FRAMEWORK.md), for Western Australia. The other
Australian states and territories are compared in [`australia-overview.md`](./australia-overview.md).

## What this means for chem-games (summary)

- **Organic chemistry is split across two years.** Year 11 Unit 1 names **straight and simple branched alkanes
  and alkenes (C1–C8)** and covers hydrocarbon reactions, **including benzene substitution**. Year 12 Unit 4
  does functional groups, naming up to C8, isomers, alcohol oxidation, esters, polymers, proteins, soaps and
  synthesis. Only Units 3–4 are in the WACE exam.
- **The WA scope is narrower than Queensland's in several places a game might assume:**
  - no haloalkanes
  - no Markovnikov
  - no elimination
  - no optical isomers
  - no IR/MS of organic compounds
  - no Maxwell–Boltzmann
  - no Hess's law
  - no bond-enthalpy calculations
  - no quantitative Kc (equilibrium position is predicted "qualitatively" from K)

  Games should let the WA profile hide these topics.
- **It is wider than Queensland's in other places:**
  - amines and amides are *named* (IUPAC)
  - soaps and detergents (including hard water)
  - protein secondary/tertiary structure
  - saponification
  - biodiesel (base- and lipase-catalysed)
  - comparing fossil fuels and biofuels by energy output as a science-understanding dot point
- **Observation language is examined hard.** The 2024 exam asked for observations (e.g. bromine water, acidified
  permanganate). The examiners' report warns:
  - do not name chemicals in observations
  - do not say "clear" when you mean colourless
  - treat "colourless, odourless gas" as an inference, not an observation

  The **data booklet has a whole "Colours of selected substances" page** (Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange, MnO₄⁻ purple, Br₂(aq)
  orange, etc.). A "what would you see?" game using those colours fits WA exactly.
- **Energetics is qualitative and Year 11 only:**
  - exo/endo and thermochemical equations
  - energy profile diagrams with the transition state
  - fuels compared by energy output

  The data booklet has **no bond enthalpies and no specific heat capacity**. Kinetics is Year 11 Unit 2, but
  **collision theory is repeated as the first dot point of Year 12 Unit 3**, so rate factors and catalysts
  *are* examinable in the WACE exam. Temperature effects on equilibrium are shown on energy profile diagrams.
- **Naming style:** butan-1-ol, butan-2-one, butan-1-amine (2024 exam); "cis-trans isomerism"; "structural
  formulae (condensed or showing bonds)"; "ethanoate (acetate)"; "α-amino acids". The examiners asked for
  explicit functional groups in condensed formulae and "the latest IUPAC conventions".
- **A syllabus refresh is under way but looks non-content.**
  - New Year 11 syllabuses are "for teaching from 2026".
  - SCSA's "What's changing" notes cover only *Rationale and Aims* and *General capabilities*.
  - Year 12 notes also exist, which implies a Year 12 refresh, probably from 2027 **(inference; see §6)**.

## 1. Structure

### 1.1 Courses

- WA offers **Chemistry ATAR** (the university-entrance course with a compulsory external exam) and
  **Chemistry General** (no ATAR exam; externally set task).
- Each course is a Year 11 syllabus (Units 1–2) plus a Year 12 syllabus (Units 3–4). Each unit is "one semester",
  with **55 class contact hours per unit** (Year 11) or **110 hours for the pair** (Year 12).
- Year 11 units are "typically delivered as a pair". Year 12 units are "delivered as a pair".
- Year 11 content is "assumed knowledge" for Year 12. It is "recommended" that Units 1–2 are done before Units
  3–4.

| Unit | ATAR title |
|---|---|
| 1 (Y11) | Chemical fundamentals: structure, properties and reactions |
| 2 (Y11) | Molecular interactions and reactions |
| 3 (Y12) | Equilibrium, acids and bases, and redox reactions |
| 4 (Y12) | Organic chemistry and chemical synthesis |

- Each unit has three strands:
  - **Science Inquiry Skills**
  - **Science as a Human Endeavour** (a context paragraph per topic)
  - **Science Understanding** (dot points)
- Year 12 says the unit content "is the examinable content".

### 1.2 School-based assessment (ATAR)

| Type | Year 11 weight | Year 12 weight |
|---|---|---|
| Science inquiry (practical + investigation; at least one investigation) | 25% | 20% |
| Extended response | 10% | 10% |
| Test | 15% | 20% |
| Examination (school exams; Year 12 mirrors the design brief) | 50% | 50% |

Schools grade A–E from a ranked list.

### 1.3 WACE ATAR course examination (Year 12; examination design brief)

- **Reading time 10 minutes, working time 3 hours**, one paper. Up to three non-programmable calculators.
- **A Chemistry data booklet is provided.**

| Section | Weight | Questions | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|---|
| One: Multiple-choice | 25% | 25 | 50 min |
| Two: Short answer | 35% | 8–12 | 60 min |
| Three: Extended answer (scenario-based, multi-part) | 40% | 5–7 | 70 min |

- **Calculations are 15–20% of the exam** (Sections Two and Three). Answers need working, correct significant
  figures and units.
- 2024 statistics:
  - 4457 candidates, mean 59.89%.
  - Section means: Section One 72.87%, Section Two 55.29% (81 raw marks), Section Three 55.81% (87 raw marks).
- The final result combines the moderated school mark and the exam mark. The usual description is 50:50, but
  this was not read in the WACE Manual **(unverified)**.
- The **ATAR** is calculated by TISC; the certificate is the **WACE**.

### 1.4 Chemistry General (brief)

- **Year 11** (Units 1–2): practical, context-based chemistry: states, solubility, qualitative reaction rates
  ("time to complete"), food spoilage, sustainability and waste.
- **Year 12:**
  - **Unit 3:** crude oil, fractional distillation, "alkanes can be named using IUPAC conventions (C1–C8,
    straight chain only)", cracking, "alkenes can be named using IUPAC conventions (C2–C3 only)", polymers,
    vegetable oils (unsaturation tested "using bromine water or a dilute solution of iodine in ethanol"),
    biofuels, ethanol by fermentation.
  - **Unit 4:** either chemistry of metals or biochemistry (proteins, carbohydrates, ATP, enzymes).
- Assessment includes an **Externally Set Task (15%, 50 minutes, Term 2)** on Unit 3 content that SCSA names
  in advance. There is no ATAR exam.

### 1.5 Chemistry ATAR data booklet (2025 edition, 8 pages)

1. Periodic table (IUPAC 2018 data).
2. **Formulae:** n = m/M; n = cV; n = V/22.71 (gas at STP); PV = nRT; ppm = mg solute / kg solution;
   pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].
3. **Units:** L or mL; °C or K ("0.0 °C = 273.15 K"); "**Energy changes are given in kilojoules (kJ)**"; kPa;
   mol L⁻¹, g L⁻¹, ppm.
4. **Constants:** R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; NA = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; **STP is 0.0 °C and 100.0 kPa, 22.71 L**;
   Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C.
5. Solubility rules (soluble / insoluble / slightly soluble, with exceptions).
6. **Colours of selected substances:**
   - ionic solids (e.g. CuO black, PbI₂ yellow)
   - coloured aqueous ions: Cr³⁺ deep green, Co²⁺ pink, Cu²⁺ blue, Fe²⁺ pale green, Fe³⁺ pale brown, Mn²⁺
     pale pink, Ni²⁺ green, **CrO₄²⁻ yellow, Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange, MnO₄⁻ purple**
   - halogens as elements, in water (Cl₂(aq) pale yellow; **Br₂(aq) orange**; I₂(aq) brown) and in
     organic solvent
7. **α-amino acids** (20, with condensed structures).
8. **Standard reduction potentials at 25 °C.** Includes MnO₄⁻/Mn²⁺, Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺, H₂O₂, PbO₂/PbSO₄ and the
   CO₂/H₂C₂O₄ couple.

**Not in the booklet:** bond enthalpies, specific heat capacity of water, ΔH data, IR data and an indicator
table. None is needed, because the syllabus has no calculations that use them.

## 2. Content by year / unit

Quoted text is verbatim from the 2024 syllabuses; everything else is paraphrase.

### Year 11, Unit 1: Chemical fundamentals: structure, properties and reactions

*Properties and structure of atoms*
- electron configurations, periodic trends
- flame tests and AAS
- isotopes, relative atomic mass, mass spectrometry of elements

*Properties and structure of materials*
- pure substances and mixtures; nanomaterials (1–100 nm); separation techniques
- ionic, metallic and covalent (molecular and network) bonding and properties; carbon allotropes
- percentage composition
- **Organic block:**
  - "hydrocarbons, including alkanes, alkenes and benzene, have different chemical properties that are
    determined by the nature of the bonding within the molecules"
  - "molecular structural formulae (condensed or showing bonds) can be used to show the arrangement of atoms
    and bonding in covalent molecular substances"
  - "**IUPAC nomenclature is used to name straight and simple branched alkanes and alkenes from C1- C8**"
  - "alkanes, alkenes and benzene undergo characteristic reactions such as combustion, addition reactions for
    alkenes and substitution reactions for alkanes and benzene"

*Chemical reactions: reactants, products and energy change* (the energetics block)
- Balanced equations.
- "chemical reactions and phase changes involve enthalpy changes, commonly observable as changes in the
  temperature of the surroundings and/or the emission of light"
- Endo/exothermic reactions "can be explained in terms of the Law of Conservation of Energy and the breaking of
  existing bonds and forming of new bonds". Heat released or absorbed "can be represented in thermochemical
  equations".
- **Fossil fuels (coal, oil, petroleum, natural gas) and biofuels (biogas, biodiesel, bioethanol) "can be
  compared in terms of their energy output, suitability for purpose, and the nature of products of
  combustion"**. The SHE context adds carbon emissions.
- Mole concept; masses of reactants and products.
- Learning outcome: "understand the concept of enthalpy, and apply this to qualitatively and quantitatively
  describe and explain energy changes".
- Inquiry skills: "the use of devices to accurately measure temperature change and mass … and heat of
  reaction".
- There is **no q = mcΔT, calorimetry calculation, Hess's law or bond enthalpy calculation** in the dot points.

### Year 11, Unit 2: Molecular interactions and reactions

*Intermolecular forces and gases*
- VSEPR and Lewis structures, polarity
- dispersion forces, dipole–dipole forces and hydrogen bonding explaining vapour pressure, melting/boiling
  point and solubility
- **TLC, GC and HPLC** data for composition and purity
- kinetic theory (qualitative), gas volumes at STP

*Aqueous solutions and acidity*
- properties of water; concentration (mol L⁻¹, g L⁻¹, ppm)
- identifying ions by colour, flame tests and precipitation
- solubility and ion–dipole forces
- Arrhenius model, pH and indicators, reaction patterns of acids and bases, ionic equations

*Rates of chemical reactions* (the kinetics block)
- Varying conditions changes rate. Rate "can be quantified by measuring the rate of formation of products or
  the depletion of reactants".
- Collision theory explains concentration, temperature, pressure, catalysts and surface area.
- "the activation energy is the minimum energy required for a chemical reaction to occur and is related to the
  strength and number of the existing chemical bonds"
- "**energy profile diagrams, which can include the transition state and catalysed and uncatalysed pathways,
  can be used to represent the enthalpy changes and activation energy**"
- Catalysts, "including enzymes and metal nanoparticles", provide "an alternative reaction pathway with a
  reduced activation energy".
- SHE: industrial catalysts and **catalytic converters**.
- **No Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, no rate law or order.**

### Year 12, Unit 3: Equilibrium, acids and bases, and redox reactions (examinable)

*Chemical equilibrium systems*
- First dot point, **repeating the kinetics**: "collision theory can be used to explain and predict the effects
  of concentration, temperature, pressure, the presence of catalysts and surface area of reactants on the
  rates of chemical reactions".
- Systems "may be **open** … or **closed**" (no "isolated").
- Dynamic equilibrium in closed systems; position of equilibrium; characteristics in terms of rates.
- Reversibility explained by the activation energies of the forward and reverse reactions.
- Temperature effects predicted "by considering the enthalpy changes for the forward and reverse reactions;
  this can be represented on energy profile diagrams".
- Concentration and partial-pressure effects via collision theory.
- **Le Châtelier's Principle** for temperature, concentration, partial pressures, **total volume and the
  addition of a catalyst**.
- Equilibrium law expressions for homogeneous and heterogeneous systems.
- "the relative amounts of reactants and products (equilibrium position) can be **predicted qualitatively**
  using equilibrium constants (Kc)". **No Kc calculations, no Ksp.**
- SHE: ocean acidification and calcification.

*Acids and bases*
- Brønsted–Lowry; monoprotic and polyprotic acids; strength via degree of ionisation and **Ka**
  (qualitative).
- **Hydrolysis of salts**; buffers (qualitative, Le Châtelier); Kw; pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].
- Indicators; titration and volumetric calculations.

*Oxidation and reduction*
- Half-equations (acidic conditions); displacement and combustion as redox; oxidation numbers.
- Standard electrode potentials; galvanic and electrolytic cells; corrosion and its prevention; cell diagrams.
- Electrorefining of copper, silver electroplating.
- SHE: Leclanché, lead-acid and hydrogen fuel cells.

### Year 12, Unit 4: Organic chemistry and chemical synthesis (examinable). **The organic unit**

*Properties and structure of organic materials*
- Functional groups: "alkenes, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines and amides",
  defined as "groups of atoms or bonds within molecules which are responsible for the molecule's
  characteristic chemical properties". **Haloalkanes are not listed.**
- "structural formulae (condensed or showing bonds)" for all eight groups.
- Characteristic reactions: "addition reactions of alkenes, redox reactions of alcohols, and acid-base reactions
  of carboxylic acids". These "can be used to identify the functional group present".
- "**IUPAC nomenclature is used to name organic species, including those with a parent chain of up to 8 carbon
  atoms with simple branching and one of the following functional groups: alkenes, alcohols, aldehydes,
  ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines and amides**"
- "different types of isomerism include **chain and position structural isomerism and cis-trans isomerism**".
  Optical isomerism is not mentioned.
- Alcohols:
  - "all alcohols can undergo complete combustion"
  - "with oxidising agents, including **acidified MnO₄⁻ or Cr₂O₇²⁻** oxidation of primary alcohols produces
    aldehydes and carboxylic acids, while the oxidation of secondary alcohols produce ketones; these reactions
    have **characteristic observations**"
- Esters: alcohol + carboxylic acid "in a condensation reaction".
- Boiling point and solubility explained by dispersion, dipole–dipole and hydrogen bonding, "influenced by the
  nature of the functional groups".
- Empirical and molecular formulae by calculation; structure from reactions and "other analytical data".
  Specific spectroscopic methods are not named.
- Polymers:
  - addition polymers (polyethene, polytetrafluoroethene) and condensation polymers (polyamides, polyesters)
  - predict the polymer from the monomer and the monomer from the polymer
- **α-amino acids:** generalised structure, zwitterions, peptide bonds, polypeptides.
  - Protein **primary, secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet) and tertiary structure**, with named side-chain
    interactions (disulfide bridges, hydrogen bonding, dipole–dipole, dispersion, ionic).
- SHE: the Protein Data Bank.

*Chemical synthesis*
- Reaction sequences "with more than one chemical reaction", with reagents and conditions chosen "to optimise
  the rate and yield".
- Limiting reagent and **percentage yield**.
- Industrial conditions: **Haber process, Contact process, biodiesel (base-catalysed and lipase-catalysed)**.
- Enzymes as industrial catalysts: "fermentation to produce ethanol versus hydration of ethene".
- Worked sequence named in the syllabus: "the hydration of ethene to form ethanol and the subsequent reaction of
  ethanol with acetic (ethanoic) acid to produce ethyl ethanoate".
- **Saponification** of triglycerides to glycerol and soap. Soap vs dodecylbenzenesulfonate detergent
  structures; cleaning action; **hard water**.
- Plastics:
  - polyethene, PTFE, nylon, PET
  - structure–property links (cross-linking, chain length, intermolecular forces)
- SHE: green chemistry for ethanol and biodiesel production. The glossary definition of green chemistry
  includes atom economy.

### Years 7–10 (Western Australian Curriculum: Science)

- WA adapts rather than adopts AC v9.0.
- The **2024 consultation draft** (read) kept WA's own wording alongside AC v9 codes:
  - **Year 9:** atomic structure and isotopes; ions and ionic compounds; rearranging atoms, word and balanced
    equations, conservation of mass (AC9S9U06/U07).
  - **Year 10:**
    - atomic structure and the periodic table
    - ionic vs covalent bonding and naming
    - **reaction patterns** (precipitation; acids with bases, metals and carbonates)
    - "**The rate at which a reaction occurs can be altered by changing factors, including temperature,
      concentration and the surface area of a reactant**" (AC9S10U06/U07)
  - The draft Year 10 rate wording does **not** name catalysts.
- **No hydrocarbons or organic chemistry and no enthalpy** in the Years 7–10 draft. Energy change appears only as
  "indicators" of chemical change (Year 8: colour, temperature, gas, light).
- The final 2026 WA wording was not read **(unverified)**.

## 4. Conventions and notation

**Verified from SCSA primary sources:**
- **Locant style:** the 2024 exam uses *butan-1-ol*, *pentan-1-ol*, *butan-1-amine*, *butan-2-one*, *butanal*,
  *methanal*. The examiners' report tells teachers to be "up to date with the latest IUPAC conventions".
- **Isomer terms:** "chain and position structural isomerism", "**cis-trans isomerism**" (not E/Z).
- **Formula terms:** "structural formulae (condensed or showing bonds)". WA writes "formulae"; Queensland
  writes "formulas". The examiners want connectivity drawn clearly and functional groups written explicitly in
  condensed formulae.
- **Names used in the syllabus:**
  - "ethanoate (acetate)", "hydrogencarbonate", "dihydrogenphosphate" (closed-up spelling), "ethanoic acid"
  - "acetic (ethanoic) acid", "ethyl ethanoate", "glycerol"
  - "polyethene", "polytetrafluoroethene", "polyethylene terephthalate [PET]", "nylon", "**α-amino acids**"
  - "Le Châtelier's Principle" (capital P)
  - "caesium", "sulfate", "aluminium"
- **Reagents as written:** "acidified MnO₄⁻ or Cr₂O₇²⁻" in the syllabus; "acidified potassium permanganate
  solution" and "bromine water" in the 2024 exam. WA uses *permanganate*, not *manganate(VII)* as Queensland
  does.
- **Observation conventions** (2024 examiners' report, paraphrased):
  - Describe what is seen before and after, including all changes.
  - Do not name chemicals in observations; metals are "silver/grey solids".
  - Solutions are "colourless" or coloured, never just "clear".
  - "Colourless, odourless gas" is an inference.
  - Watch for "excess".
  - The data booklet colour tables are the reference set.
- **Units and constants** (data booklet):
  - energy changes in **kJ**; volumes in L/mL; mol L⁻¹
  - STP = 0.0 °C and 100.0 kPa, 22.71 L mol⁻¹; R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; NA = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
  - Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C
  - WA uses more significant figures than Queensland (22.71 vs 22.7)
- **Required ions and molecules** are listed in Year 11 Appendix 2 (e.g. oxalate, dichromate, permanganate,
  hydrogensulfate; molecules such as ethanoic acid and sulfurous acid).
- **Calculation presentation:** show every number (no "empty brackets and arrows"), no rounding mid-calculation,
  and give appropriate significant figures and units.

**Game implication:** a WA profile should show equilibrium K only qualitatively, give no bond-enthalpy or
calorimetry numbers unless marked as enrichment, and include an observations mode with the booklet colours.

## 5. Cognitive verbs / command terms

- SCSA uses a single **"Glossary of key words used in the formulation of questions"** for all Year 11–12 courses
  (current file coded 2026/1553).
- It warns that "some terms have a course specific meaning". The glossary definitions are generic.

Paraphrased definitions of the terms most relevant to Chemistry:

| Term | Meaning (paraphrase) |
|---|---|
| Analyse | identify components and how they relate; draw out implications |
| Apply | use in a particular situation |
| Calculate | work out from given facts, figures or information |
| Classify | put into classes or categories |
| Compare | show how things are alike and different |
| Contrast | show how things differ |
| Deduce | draw conclusions |
| Define | state the meaning and essential qualities |
| Describe | give characteristics and features |
| Determine | decide, find out |
| Discuss | identify issues and give points for and/or against |
| Distinguish | note the differences between things |
| Evaluate | judge value or amount carefully |
| Explain | relate cause and effect; show how and/or why |
| Extrapolate | infer from what is known |
| Identify | recognise and name |
| Interpret | draw meaning from |
| Justify | support an argument or conclusion with reasons |
| List | give a series of related items in order |
| Name | give the word or term that identifies something |
| Outline | sketch in general terms; give the main features |
| Predict | say what may happen based on the available information |
| Sketch | draw quickly and roughly; give a brief outline |
| State | express the particulars of an idea or topic |
| Suggest | propose a possible action, solution or hypothesis |

observations", "write a balanced equation", "draw the structural formula".
